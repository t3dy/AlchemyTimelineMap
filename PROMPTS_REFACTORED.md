# ALCHEMYTIMELINEMAP: Canonical Vision and Historiographical Framework

**Purpose:** Single source of truth for why this project exists and what historiographical principles govern it. Read before starting content work to understand the deeper context.

---

## Part I: Project Vision

ALCHEMYTIMELINEMAP is an authoritative, interactive portal for the history of alchemy and the chemical sciences from Late Antiquity through the early modern period. It combines a **500-event interactive timeline** with a **geo-pinned map** covering Europe, North Africa, and the Middle East.

**What it is:** A rigorous, provenance-aware scholarly resource for academics, students, and serious independent researchers exploring chemical discoveries, alchemical theories, key texts, and pivotal figures that shaped the tradition.

**What it is NOT:**
- An esoteric or mystical resource
- A guide to alchemical practice or transmutation claims
- A Wikipedia mirror
- A collection of AI-generated summaries without scholarly grounding

**Tone:** Critical, reportorial, provenance-aware. Disagreements between scholars are named explicitly.

## Part II: Three Constituencies (Design Principle)

Every page must serve all three simultaneously:

### 1. Scholars
Need precision, historiographical nuance, named sources, complete bibliography, relational links. Tolerate no vagueness.

### 2. Students
Need accessible prose, definitions on first use (distillation, calcination, transmutation), clear chronological scaffolding. Will be lost without context.

### 3. Serious Independent Researchers
Need depth, the ability to follow a thread from one event to a related figure to a text to a concept. Should never reach a dead end.

**Design consequence:** Every event must link to ≥1 person, text, concept. No isolated entries.

## Part III: Historiographical Framework (Non-Negotiable)

### 3.1: Alchemy ≠ Chemistry

Modern historiography distinguishes:

- **Alchemy:** A tradition of texts, practices, and ideas about transformation, spiritual perfection, and the manipulation of matter through secret operations. Practitioners believed they were working toward transmutation and spiritual perfection.
- **Chemistry:** The modern discipline that emerged from alchemy but abandoned transmutational claims, grounding itself in empirical experimentation and eventually atomic theory.

**Do not collapse these registers.** Historical actors called themselves *alchemists*, not chemists. When discussing their work, use their language. When discussing modern understanding, signal clearly we are using retrospective analytical categories.

### 3.2: Medieval Continuity in Arabic and Latin

Alchemy is **not** an invention of the European Renaissance. It has continuous roots in:

- **Late Antique and Byzantine:** Zosimos of Panopolis (3rd century), Syrian alchemical traditions
- **Arabic alchemical tradition:** Jabir ibn Hayyan / Geber (8th–9th century), Abu Ma'shar, al-Razi, the *Emerald Tablet* and its Arabic recensions
- **Latin medieval tradition:** Gerard of Cremona translations (12th century), Roger Bacon (13th century), alchemical encyclopedias and compilations

The Renaissance "discovery" of classical alchemy was built on this continuous, unbroken medieval transmission. Never treat the medieval period as a gap or dark age.

### 3.3: Chemical Operations Are Real

When an alchemist describes distillation, sublimation, fermentation, or crystallization, they are describing **actual chemical operations** that produce measurable, observable results. These operations were genuine advances in practical chemistry.

Separate the operational knowledge (valuable) from the transmutational theory (historically contingent). Both deserve serious treatment, but they are different things.

### 3.4: Provenance on Every Claim

Every substantive assertion must be traceable to a named source. Integrate citations organically: "As William Newman argues in *Atoms and Alchemy* (2006)..." Do not use footnote brackets. Every timeline event must conclude with a single-sentence historiographical significance statement.

### 3.5: Geographic Specificity

Every event must have a specific location (city ideally; region at minimum) with coordinates. The map is not decoration—it is a primary research tool. Clustering of events in certain regions (Baghdad, 9th century; medieval Iberia; Renaissance Italy; early modern Antwerp) reveals the geography of alchemical knowledge.

### 3.6: Chemical Taxonomy

Chemical operations and theoretical categories (distillation, fermentation, transmutation, quintessence, etc.) are **first-class concepts** in this portal. They appear as filterable categories on the timeline and as browsable concept pages, not buried in text.

## Part V: The Actor/Analyst Distinction (Critical)

Following Wouter J. Hanegraaff's *Dictionary of Gnosis and Western Esotericism*, distinguish:

### ACTOR_TERMs
Words used by historical practitioners. They would recognize these terms and understand them as part of their own vocabulary.

Examples:
- *distillatio*, *calcination*, *transmutatio* (Latin)
- *dhiqa*, *riqq* (Arabic)
- *sublimatio*, *coagulatio* (Latin technical terms)

### ANALYST_TERMs
Modern retrospective scholarly categories imposed after the fact. Historical actors may NOT have recognized themselves under these labels.

Examples:
- *Alchemy* (modern category, though "alchemist" was used historically)
- *Hermeticism* (did Ficino call himself a "Hermeticist"? Probably not)
- *Esotericism*, *Mysticism*, *Magic* (modern analytical categories)
- *Artisanal epistemology*, *Material culture approach* (modern scholarly frameworks)

**Rule:** Never collapse these registers. If historians disagree about whether a term was truly used or truly meant what we think it means, state the disagreement explicitly.

## Part VI: Transmission and Misreading Matter

Historical knowledge travels, gets mistranslated, gets absorbed into unexpected frameworks, and generates new meanings. This is as important as "original" discovery.

Example transmission chains:
- Zosimos (Greek, 3rd c.) → Arabic alchemical tradition (8th–9th c.) → Latin translation (12th c.) → Medieval European alchemy → Renaissance recovery → Early modern chemistry → Modern history of science
- *Emerald Tablet* (Arabic, unknown date) → Multiple Latin recensions → Integration into Renaissance Neoplatonism → Reinterpretation by Ficino and Pico → Further reinterpretation by modern scholars

**Consequence:** Track who read whom, how meanings shifted in translation, what was emphasized by different readers, and why. This is not duplication—it is the heart of historiography.

## Part VII: Material Culture and Embodied Knowledge

Following Pamela Smith: Alchemists learned through embodied, practical engagement with laboratory apparatus. They experienced:
- The heat of furnaces
- The smell of toxic vapors
- The danger of explosions
- The color changes in liquids
- The resistance of matter

This embodied experience produced both **observable chemical effects** and **theoretical speculation** about matter's nature. The two cannot be separated in historical understanding.

Consequence for content: When describing operations (distillation, calcination, sublimation), ground them in material reality. What tools? What dangers? What sensory experiences? What observable results?

## Why This Project Matters

The history of alchemy has been distorted by two opposite errors:

1. **Nineteenth-century positivism:** Alchemy was "failed mysticism" waiting for chemistry's success.
2. **New Age esotericism:** Alchemy was esoteric wisdom encoded in spiritual allegory.

**Neither is true.**

Modern scholarship (Newman, Pereira, Smith, Principe, Hanegraaff) has shown:
- Alchemists pursued reproducible chemical operations and documented them carefully
- Their transmutational beliefs were separate from (but inseparable in practice from) their operational success
- Medieval Islamic and Latin continuity was unbroken—there was no "dark age" gap
- The language of alchemy travels through cultures and gets reinterpreted in different contexts
- Material, embodied knowledge in the laboratory shaped both practical success and theoretical claims

This portal is built on this modern historiography. It is authoritative, specific, grounded in primary sources, and transparent about scholarly disagreements.
