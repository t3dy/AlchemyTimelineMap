## Core Scholarly Values & Methods

### 1. **Ludic Translation as Research**
- **Pattern**: You don't separate "creative work" from "scholarly work." Game design is a form of historiographical inquiry.
- **Example**: Your alchemy board game disaster mechanics directly translate historical alchemists' awareness of material danger (fumes, toxins, explosions) into playable systems.
- **Implication for ALCHEMYTIMELINEMAP**: Timeline events should embed ludic possibility — not just describe what happened, but gesture toward how it could be mechanized, simulated, or played.

### 2. **Material Grounding & Craft Knowledge**
- **Pattern**: You insist on material specificity: crucibles, lead, steam, glass chemistry, workshop conditions.
- **Example**: Your board game tokens changing color based on alchemist injuries (green for fumes, burned for heat) reflects Pamela Smith's *artisanal epistemology* — knowledge embedded in the body and tools.
- **Implication for ALCHEMYTIMELINEMAP**: Descriptions should reference actual materials, tools, operations, and bodily knowledge. Avoid abstraction. Ground concepts in the sensory experience of the laboratory.

### 3. **Historiographical Precision & the Actor/Analyst Problem**
- **Pattern**: You repeatedly engage with Hanegraaff's actor/analyst distinction, question received terminology (e.g., "Hermeticism," "alchemy" itself), and ask who gets to define terms.
- **Example**: Your close reading of Ficino questions whether he was truly a "Hermeticist" or a Christian Platonist using Hermetic texts. You demand primary sources, exact citations, and methodological transparency.
- **Implication for ALCHEMYTIMELINEMAP**: Explicitly flag the actor/analyst distinction in concept definitions. Distinguish between what practitioners called their work vs. retrospective categories. Use Hanegraaff-style historiography.

### 4. **Interdisciplinary Synthesis Across Unlikely Domains**
- **Pattern**: You connect alchemy to game design (roguelike mechanics), narrative design, music (chiptune), book history (Grafton), emanation metaphysics (Pico, Kabbalah), and computational theory (Sefer Yetzirah gematria).
- **Example**: You want to turn *Atalanta Fugiens* into a roguelike where each emblem is a playable puzzle, with chiptune interpretations of Maier's scores.
- **Implication for ALCHEMYTIMELINEMAP**: Don't silo alchemy into "History of Science." Create cross-links to Kabbalah, Renaissance magic, grimoire studies, music, computational systems. Treat alchemical knowledge as a *nexus* not an *island*.

### 5. **Suspicion of Top-Down Categorization**
- **Pattern**: You push back on received frameworks. You ask whether medieval continuity matters. You question periodization. You want to see how practitioners actually understood their own work.
- **Example**: You critique Newman for conflating "practical chemistry" with "alchemy" in ways that change the historical picture. You ask whether Lazzarelli was "truly" Hermetic or using it for other purposes.
- **Implication for ALCHEMYTIMELINEMAP**: Invite ambiguity. Flag where historians disagree. Present multiple interpretations of the same figure or text. Don't collapse actor-categories into analyst-categories.

### 6. **Genealogical Interest in Transmission & Misreading**
- **Pattern**: You care deeply about how ideas travel, get mistranslated, get absorbed into unexpected frameworks, and generate new meanings.
- **Example**: You trace Ficino's misreading of the Hermetic corpus, Pico's encounter with Kabbalah and its impact on his reception of the agent intellect, Lazzarelli's absorption of alchemical allegory into Christian mysticism.
- **Implication for ALCHEMYTIMELINEMAP**: Track transmission chains. Show where texts or ideas get reused, reinterpreted, embedded into different contexts. This is as important as "original" discovery.

## Preferences on Content Depth & Granularity

### Level of Detail: **Granular + Connective**
- You prefer **specificity over synthesis**: Exact citations, named scholars, primary source references.
- But you also want **connections across domains**: How does this alchemist's theory of matter relate to contemporary Kabbalah? Neoplatonic emanation? Renaissance patronage systems?
- **Content model**: Medium-length (1,000–2,200 words) with heavy cross-referencing and relational linking.

### On Certainty: **Embrace Uncertainty**
- You tolerate (even prefer) entries that say "scholars disagree," "the source is disputed," "we don't know his sources."
- You distrust entries that present contested ideas as settled fact.
- **Implication for ALCHEMYTIMELINEMAP**: When historiographical disagreement exists, present it. Use provenance metadata (`review_status: DRAFT`, confidence levels) to signal uncertainty.

### On Periodization: **Skeptical but Pragmatic**
- You question whether "Renaissance," "Medieval," "Early Modern" are real categories or imposed frameworks.
- But you use them pragmatically for navigation while questioning them internally.
- **Implication for ALCHEMYTIMELINEMAP**: Keep the era taxonomy (ANTIQUITY, LATE_ANTIQUE, MEDIEVAL, RENAISSANCE, EARLY_MODERN) for sorting, but in prose, highlight how these periods actually blend and contradict neat boundaries.

## Historiographical Markers

### You Favor:
- **Granular case studies** over grand narratives
- **Primary sources with citations** over secondary summaries
- **Contested interpretations** over consensus
- **Material evidence** (manuscripts, equipment, recipes) over philosophy
- **Practice and transmission** over abstract theory
- **Actor's categories** over retrospective classification
- **Ambiguity and nuance** over clean categories

### You Distrust:
- Teleology ("alchemy → chemistry" as inevitable progress)
- Reductionism ("alchemy is just psychology" or "just chemistry")
- Lack of source citations
- False unity ("Hermeticism" as a monolithic tradition)
- Esotericist romanticism (treating alchemy as crypto-spirituality without historical grounding)
- Top-down periodization (treating Renaissance as a discrete break)

## Red Flags (What Not to Do)

1. ❌ Esotericist romanticizing: "Alchemy was really about enlightenment/spirituality all along"
2. ❌ Reductionism: "Alchemy was just pre-chemistry"
3. ❌ Ignoring actor/analyst: Mixing what practitioners said with retrospective categories
4. ❌ Missing transmission: Treating ideas as stable instead of showing how they travel and mutate
5. ❌ Over-synthesis: Collapsing Ficino, Pico, Lazzarelli into "Renaissance Hermeticism" without noting their disagreements
6. ❌ Lack of citation: Claims without sources, especially on contested topics
7. ❌ Ignoring material culture: Focusing only on ideas, ignoring actual laboratories, tools, bodily risk
8. ❌ Artificial periodization: Treating "Medieval" and "Renaissance" as clean breaks without showing continuity
