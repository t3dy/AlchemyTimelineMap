Johannes Aurelius Augurellus

Role: SCHOLAR | Era: MODERN

Johannes Aurelius Augurellus (Giovanni Aurelio Augurelli, ca. 1441-1524) was an Italian humanist, Latin poet, and alchemical author, best known for his three-book Latin poem Chrysopoeia (Gold-Making), published at Venice in 1515 and dedicated to Pope Leo X. Lynn Thorndike, in Ambix (1938), notes that Augurellus apparently began composing the poem around 1500 and that the Chrysopoeia contains internal references to the siege of Padua in 1509. G. Pavanello, who gave the most detailed earlier analysis of the poem with quotations (Un maestro del quattrocento, Giovanni Aurelio Augurello, Venice, 1905), considered it a significant work of early sixteenth-century alchemical literature. A second edition of the Chrysopoeia appeared at Basel in 1518, published by Froeben. Augurellus was a friend of the humanist circle associated with Aldus Manutius in Venice. His decision to dedicate the poem to Pope Leo X -- whose Medicean patronage extended to both poetry and occult arts -- represents the characteristic integration of humanist learning with alchemical aspiration in early sixteenth-century Italian intellectual culture.
