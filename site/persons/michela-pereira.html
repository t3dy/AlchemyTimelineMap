Michela Pereira

Role: SCHOLAR | Era: MODERN

Michela Pereira is an Italian historian of medieval philosophy and alchemy, emerita professor at the University of Siena, whose scholarly work on the pseudo-Lullian alchemical corpus and on medieval Latin alchemy more broadly has established the foundational texts and contexts for understanding the transmission of alchemical knowledge in medieval Europe. Her research demonstrated that the large body of alchemical texts attributed to Raymond Lull is in fact a pseudonymous corpus composed after Lull's death, largely drawing on the medical-alchemical theories of John of Rupescissa, and she has provided critical editions, translations, and analyses of key texts in this tradition. Her work situates medieval Latin alchemy within the broader context of scholastic natural philosophy, university medicine, and the institutional structures of mendicant orders.

Central Thesis

Pereira's central scholarly contribution is the disentanglement of the pseudo-Lullian alchemical corpus from the authentic writings of Ramon Lull, the Catalan mystic and missionary philosopher (c. 1232–1316) who emphatically rejected alchemy during his lifetime. Through careful philological and historical analysis, she demonstrated that the alchemical texts attributed to Lull — a corpus that came to include over 100 texts by the early modern period — were composed from the mid-fourteenth century onward, primarily drawing on John of Rupescissa's quintessence theory and adapting it into an elaborate pseudo-Lullian framework that exploited the prestige of Lull's name. This finding has implications not only for the history of Lullism but for understanding how alchemical texts were authorized and transmitted through attribution to prestigious names.

Key Works

Her major publications include The Alchemical Corpus Attributed to Raymond Lull (Warburg Institute, 1989), which remains the foundational study of the pseudo-Lullian alchemical tradition; numerous articles on specific texts within the corpus and on the broader intellectual context of medieval alchemy; and contributions to collaborative volumes on the history of chemistry and natural philosophy in the Middle Ages. Her work has influenced subsequent scholars including Lawrence Principe and William Newman, who have built on her philological foundations in their own analyses of medieval and early modern alchemical texts.
