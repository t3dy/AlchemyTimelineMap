Robert of Chester

Role: TRANSLATOR | Era: MEDIEVAL

Robert of Chester (fl. c. 1141–1150) was an English scholar who worked as a translator in Spain, most likely in the orbit of the Toledo translation movement, and who produced one of the earliest Latin translations of an Arabic alchemical text — his 1144 translation of the Liber de Compositione Alchimiae (sometimes called the Book of the Composition of Alchemy, attributed to Khalid ibn Yazid in the Arabic tradition) — as well as important translations of Arabic mathematical and astronomical texts, including al-Khwarizmi's Algebra. Robert is significant in the history of alchemy primarily as a conduit for Arabic alchemical knowledge into Latin Europe: his alchemical translation is the earliest dated Latin alchemical text in the tradition deriving from Arabic, and it helped establish the vocabulary and framework within which Latin alchemists would work for the following century. Almost nothing is known of his biography beyond what can be inferred from his translations and from his association with the English scholar Hermann the Dalmatian, with whom he collaborated on the translation of the Quran.

The Translation of the Liber de Compositione Alchimiae

Robert's 1144 Latin translation, made in Spain, introduced to Latin European readers a text presenting alchemy as a systematic art — defining its subject matter, its principles (the sulphur-mercury theory of metallic composition), and its operations — in a format deriving from Arabic alchemical tradition. Whether the Arabic original was genuinely associated with Khalid ibn Yazid (the Umayyad prince to whom Arab tradition attributed an interest in alchemy) or was a later pseudonymous composition remains uncertain. What is clear is that Robert's translation, along with the slightly later alchemical translations by Gerard of Cremona, established the textual foundation for the Latin alchemical tradition of the twelfth and thirteenth centuries. Robert also translated the tables of al-Zarqali and, most importantly for the history of mathematics, al-Khwarizmi's Liber algebrae et almucabola, making him a significant figure in the transmission of multiple scientific traditions from Arabic to Latin.
