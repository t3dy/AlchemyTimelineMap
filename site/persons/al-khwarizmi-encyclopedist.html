Abu Abdallah Muhammad ibn Ahmad al-Khwarizmi

Role: SCHOLAR | Era: MEDIEVAL

Abu Abdallah Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Yusuf al-Khwarizmi (d. ca. 997) was a tenth-century Arabic encyclopedist and court scribe employed by the Samanid dynasty, best known as the author of the Mafatih al-Ulum (Keys to the Sciences), a systematic reference handbook composed between 976 and 980 for members of the Samanid court at Bukhara. He is entirely distinct from the ninth-century mathematician Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Musa al-Khwarizmi (d. ca. 850), from whom the term 'algorithm' derives. Karin C. Ryding, in her study and translation of the alchemical chapter of the Mafatih al-Ulum published in Ambix (1994), describes the encyclopedist al-Khwarizmi as a 'pragmatic encyclopedist' who approached alchemy 'with directness and brevity,' providing copious definitions of technical terms intended to be 'comprehensible to a layman' — that is, to court scribes and secretaries who needed to understand the professional vocabulary of the sciences without being practitioners themselves.

The Mafatih al-Ulum is organized in two main discourses: the first covers the traditional religious and administrative sciences of Islamic culture; the second covers secular sciences including logic, philosophy, medicine, arithmetic, geometry, astronomy, music, mechanics, and alchemy. The chapter on alchemy (ninth chapter of the second discourse) provides what Ryding identifies as 'a unique and valuable cultural artifact': an 'objective description of medieval alchemy written by a non-alchemist,' designed to elucidate rather than render the subject obscure, and based primarily on the works of the great physician-alchemist Muhammad ibn Zakariya al-Razi (d. 925). The chapter is divided into three sections: apparatus (fifteen instruments including furnace, crucible, cucurbit, alembic, and athanor), substances (metals, spirits, salts, stones, and man-made compounds), and operations (thirteen procedures including sublimation, distillation, calcination, amalgamation, and lixiviation). Al-Khwarizmi was a contemporary of the Samanid vizier Abu al-Hasan Ubayd Allah ibn Ahmad al-Utbi, to whom the Mafatih al-Ulum was dedicated.
