Nicholas Eymerich

Role: SCHOLAR | Era: MODERN

Nicholas Eymerich (Nicolau Eimeric, ca. 1316–1399), a Catalan Dominican friar, served as Inquisitor General of Aragon under Pope Gregory XI and is best known as the author of the Directorium Inquisitorum (ca. 1376), the most comprehensive medieval manual of inquisitorial procedure. Eymerich received his doctorate at Avignon, served as a professor of theology, and was appointed Inquisitor General of Aragon ca. 1357. His relentless pursuit of heresy — including his campaigns against the followers of Ramon Lull — brought him into conflict with the Aragonese crown, and he was twice exiled from Aragon: once in 1360 and again in 1393, spending extended periods in Avignon. The Directorium Inquisitorum, composed in Avignon during his first exile, became the authoritative handbook for inquisitors throughout Catholic Europe; it was rediscovered and published in Rome in 1578 by the Spanish canonist Francisco Peña (ca. 1540–1612), whose commentary expanded its scope and influence dramatically. Andrew Campbell, Lorenza Gianfrancesco, and Neil Tarrant, in Ambix (2018), draw on Tarrant's research to identify the Directorium as the foundational document in the Church's conceptual shift toward treating alchemy as potentially superstitious or sorcerous. Eymerich himself did not condemn alchemy outright, but his framing of the question introduced a lasting association between alchemical practice and the demonic invocations that the Directorium targeted.
