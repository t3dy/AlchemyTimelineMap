Kenelm Digby

Role: ALCHEMIST | Era: MEDIEVAL

Kenelm Digby (1603–1665) was an English natural philosopher, courtier, and Catholic convert whose Two Treatises, in One of which the Nature of Bodies; in the Other, the Nature of Mans Soule is Looked into (1644) presented a corpuscular account of material nature influenced by Descartes and Gassendi, and who maintained active interests in practical chemistry, medicine, and what contemporaries called "chymistry." He is perhaps best known outside scholarly circles for his advocacy of the "powder of sympathy" — a preparation claimed to heal wounds through sympathetic action at a distance — which he described in lectures at Montpellier and in a published account (1658). While Digby's intellectual ambitions placed him at the intersection of early modern natural philosophy, practical medicine, and learned magic, he was a genuine participant in the chymical culture of his period rather than simply a credulous occultist.
