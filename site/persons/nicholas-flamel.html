Nicholas Flamel

Role: ALCHEMIST | Era: MEDIEVAL

Nicholas Flamel (c. 1330–1418), a Parisian scribe and alleged alchemist, became one of the most famous figures in medieval and Renaissance alchemy through a combination of genuine historical records and elaborate legend. The historical Flamel was a successful merchant and book dealer who became wealthy and was known for philanthropic activities in Paris. The legendary Flamel—through the elaborate pseudo-biography composed by later alchemists—became famous as the discoverer of the Philosopher's Stone and the secret of transmutation. Whether Flamel engaged in actual alchemical work remains historically uncertain; modern scholarship emphasizes the legendary dimensions of his reputation. Nevertheless, his name became synonymous with alchemical success, and later alchemists attributed to him sophisticated philosophical texts claiming to detail the transmutational procedures. Flamel's example demonstrates how historical and legendary dimensions of alchemy became intertwined in early modern thought.
