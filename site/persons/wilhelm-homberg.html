Wilhelm Homberg

Role: CHEMIST | Era: MEDIEVAL

Wilhelm Homberg (1652–1715) was a Dutch-German natural philosopher and physician who became one of the most prominent chemists of the Académie Royale des Sciences in Paris, conducting influential experiments on the nature of fire, light, and chemical combination and contributing to the debates over phlogiston and the elemental composition of matter that would eventually lead to the Chemical Revolution of the late eighteenth century. Born in Batavia (Jakarta) to a Dutch family and educated in medicine at Leiden, he traveled widely before settling in Paris under the patronage of Philippe II, Duke of Orléans. Homberg's experiments — including his famous synthesis of "sal sedativum" (boric acid) from boron — combined practical chemical skill with theoretical engagement with contemporary debates about the nature of elementary substances, fire, and the relationships between chemical properties and the structure of matter.

Literature
