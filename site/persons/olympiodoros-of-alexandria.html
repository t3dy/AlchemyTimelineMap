Olympiodoros of Alexandria (Alchemist)

Role: SCHOLAR | Era: MODERN

Olympiodoros of Alexandria (fl. ca. 425 CE) was a Neoplatonist philosopher and commentator on Aristotle who also wrote alchemical works, making him one of the most important transitional figures in the Greek alchemical tradition between Zosimos of Panopolis (fl. ca. 300 CE) and Stephanos of Alexandria (fl. ca. 620 CE). F. Sherwood Taylor, in Ambix (1937), places Olympiodoros among the major authorities on Greek alchemy cited by Stephanos and the later Byzantine commentators, identifying him as the earliest author to quote and systematize the symbolic literature on the 'Egg in the Pyramid' — the alchemical symbolism in which the world-egg, described by Heroies (a legendary source), is equated with the universe and its 'golden hair.' Olympiodoros quotes an impressive range of earlier Greek alchemical authorities including Zosimos, Synesius, and fragments of the lost works of Hermes Trismegistos and Maria the Jewess, making his work a major witness to the early Greek alchemical corpus, much of which is now lost in its original form.

Olympiodoros should be distinguished from Olympiodoros the Younger (ca. 495–570 CE), a later Neoplatonist commentator on Aristotle who wrote extensive surviving philosophical commentaries. The identification of the alchemical Olympiodoros with either the earlier or later figure of that name has been a matter of scholarly debate. His surviving alchemical writings are preserved in Berthelot's Collection des Alchimistes Grecs (Paris, 1888) and include commentary on Zosimos's On the Letter Omega and a systematic discussion of alchemical symbolism including the planet-metal correspondences and the symbolism of the egg. C. A. Browne, in Ambix (1946), identifies Olympiodoros as a source for Stephanos of Alexandria's nine lectures on the Great and Sacred Art, which were the foundational text of Byzantine alchemy in the 7th century.
