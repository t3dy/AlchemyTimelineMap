Muhammad al-Razi

Role: CHEMIST | Era: MEDIEVAL

Muhammad al-Razi — as a separate database entry from the more fully documented Abu Bakr Muhammad al-Razi (Abu Bakr Muhammad ibn Zakariyya al-Razi, c. 854–925 CE, known in Latin as Rhazes) — represents a stub record that should be understood in relation to the comprehensive entry for Al-Razi (Rhazes). The historical figure, Abu Bakr Muhammad ibn Zakariyya al-Razi, was a Persian physician, philosopher, and alchemist active in Ray (Persia) and Baghdad, and is one of the most important figures in the history of Islamic medicine and of practical alchemy. His Kitāb al-Asrār (Book of Secrets) provided the most systematic practical manual of laboratory alchemy in the Arabic tradition, cataloguing operations, apparatus, and substances with a precision unmatched in earlier alchemical literature. For full scholarly context, see the entry for Abu Bakr Muhammad al-Razi.
