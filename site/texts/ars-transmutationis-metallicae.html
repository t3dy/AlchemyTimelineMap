Ars Transmutationis Metallicae (Pantheus)

Type: TREATISE | Language: None | Composed: 1518 CE

Ars Transmutationis Metallicae (The Art of Metallic Transmutation) is an alchemical treatise published at Venice on September 7, 1518, by the Venetian priest and goldsmith Giovanni Agostino Pantheus, dedicated to Pope Leo X and the Council of Ten of Venice. Lynn Thorndike, in Ambix (1938), identifies the work as a major early sixteenth-century alchemical text in which Pantheus cites the Turba Philosophorum, Geber, Alphidius, Avicenna, Rhodianus, Rosinus, and Vincent among his sources. Its full title on the title page is Ars transmutationis metallicae cum Leonis X Ponti. Max. et Conci. Cap. decemvirorum Venetorum edicto. Following the main text, dated September 7, 1518, the volume includes a Commentary on the Theory of Metallic Transmutation addressed to a Polish nobleman, William Hyerosky, with columns of numbers and Hebrew characters. Pantheus apparently had to reformulate his alchemical program in 1530, when he published the Voarchadumia contra alchimiam (Venice, 1530), claiming to present not alchemy but an entirely distinct art called Voarchadumia, a supposed cabala of metals attributed to Tubal Cain via the Chaldeans and Indians, while actually repeating most of the 1518 content. Both the Ars and the Voarchadumia were reprinted at Paris in 1550 and included in Zetzner's Theatrum Chemicum (volumes 2, 1613 and 1659), indicating their lasting influence on the European alchemical tradition. The Voarchadumia's woodcuts of alchemical furnaces and apparatus are among the early printed illustrations of laboratory equipment.
