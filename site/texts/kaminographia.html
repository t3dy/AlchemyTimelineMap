Kaminographia (On Apparatus and Furnaces)

Type: TREATISE | Language: None | Composed: ca. 200 CE

Kaminographia (On Apparatus and Furnaces, also known under the fuller title Peri Kaminon kai Organon) is an alchemical treatise attributed to Maria the Jewess (Maria Hebraea, fl. ca. 200 CE), preserved only in quotation and paraphrase within the alchemical writings of Zosimus of Panopolis (fl. late 3rd–early 4th century CE). Raphael Patai, in his study of Maria published in Ambix (1982), identifies the Kaminographia as the primary source for Maria's descriptions of alchemical apparatus: Zosimus himself says that 'a great number of constructions of apparatus have been described by Maria; not only those which concern the divine (or: sulphurous) waters, but also many kinds of kerotakis and furnaces,' and states that he is unwilling to provide information on the subject of furnaces because he is 'unable to treat it more authoritatively than was done in that ancient treatise.'

Content. The Kaminographia's most famous passage is the description of the tribikos, a three-pronged copper still that Zosimus quotes directly from Maria's text: copper tubes 'a little thicker than that of a pastry-cook's copper frying pan,' approximately a cubit and a half long, with their openings 'adapted like a nail to the neck of a light receiver' and fitted with glass flasks 'large and strong so that they may not break with the heat coming from the water in the middle.' Maria also described the balneum Mariae (water bath) and the kerotakis, a metal plate apparatus for treating metals with alchemical vapors, as well as numerous other furnaces, distillation vessels, and alchemical apparatus. The treatise also contained procedures for preparing the 'divine water' (vapors of sulphur and arsenical substances) and instructions for the coloration of metals through sequential phases of blackening, whitening, yellowing, and reddening.

Significance and Survival. The Kaminographia does not survive as an independent text; it is known entirely through quotations and paraphrases in Zosimus of Panopolis, who appears to have drawn on it extensively for his own treatise on apparatus and furnaces. The apparatus Maria described — particularly the balneum Mariae and the kerotakis — became foundational terms in the Latin and Arabic alchemical traditions, demonstrating the Kaminographia's extraordinary influence despite the loss of its original text. Berthelot, Lippmann, and Patai are the key modern scholars who have analyzed the Kaminographia's contents through the Zosimean quotations.
