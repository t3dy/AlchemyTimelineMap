De Multiplicitione Specierum

Type: PRIMARY_SOURCE | Language: Latin | Composed: 13th century

De Multiplicitione Specierum (On the Multiplication of Species), authored by Roger Bacon] in the 13th century, is an optical-philosophical treatise that relates optical theory to alchemical transformation. The work addresses how light and essence "multiply" and propagate through space, drawing parallels between optical propagation and the transformation of materials in alchemical operations. While not primarily alchemical, the treatise demonstrates how Roger Bacon] integrated optical science with alchemical thought, viewing both as expressions of fundamental natural principles. The work exemplifies medieval attempts to systematize and rationalize alchemical knowledge through grounding it in optical and natural-philosophical frameworks.

Content and Theory

De Multiplicitione Specierum addresses how the essences of things propagate through space and time, using optical terminology and concepts. Bacon argues that just as light multiplies and extends through space following geometric principles, so essences of alchemical substances multiply and extend through operations. The treatise draws parallels between the propagation of light and color and the transformation of materials in the laboratory. This integration of optical and alchemical thought reflects medieval scholasticism's attempt to unify knowledge under comprehensive philosophical frameworks. Bacon's approach suggests that understanding the mathematical and optical principles governing the propagation of qualities and essences will enable more effective manipulation of matter.

Composition and Textual Tradition

Roger Bacon composed De Multiplicitione Specierum as part of his broader intellectual project of systematizing knowledge and demonstrating the unity of different disciplines. The work survives in medieval manuscripts and circulated among scholars interested in optical theory and natural philosophy. The work was not widely printed or circulated in the early modern period, suggesting it remained more specialized in its appeal than Bacon's more famous Opus Majus.

Modern Scholarship

Modern scholarship on De Multiplicitione Specierum has focused on understanding Bacon's optical science and his integration of optics with broader natural philosophy. The work's relationship to alchemy has received less scholarly attention, though the parallels Bacon drew between optical propagation and alchemical transformation are significant for understanding how medieval thinkers conceptualized transformation and causality in nature.
