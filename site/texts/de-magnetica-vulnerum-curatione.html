De magnetica vulnerum curatione (Van Helmont)

Type: TREATISE | Language: None | Composed: 1621 CE

De magnetica vulnerum curatione (On the Magnetic Cure of Wounds, 1621) is a short treatise by Jan Baptist van Helmont (1579–1644) defending the weapon salve, a Paracelsian remedy that involved treating a wound by applying ointment to the weapon that had caused it rather than to the wound itself. Georgiana D. Hedesan, in Ambix (2021), identifies the treatise as the text that precipitated Van Helmont's collision with ecclesiastical authorities: the Mechelen ecclesiastical curia condemned it (along with Otto Van Veen's Physicae et Theologicae Conclusiones) as having been written uno eodemque daemonis spiritu (in one and the same demonic spirit), and characterized both authors as disciples of Paracelsus. The condemnation of the treatise began Van Helmont's long legal confrontation with the Inquisition, culminating in house arrest from ca. 1634 onward. The weapon salve controversy itself had a longer genealogy: Rudolf Goclenius of the University of Marburg and the Jesuit Jean Roberti of Douai had already been engaged in a polemic over the salve before Van Helmont's treatise appeared. Van Helmont's defense of the weapon salve was grounded in his concept of the Blas (vital force) and his theory that alchemical seminal principles could act at a distance through sympathetic resonance — a doctrine linked to Paracelsian notions of the weapon's retained vital connection to the wounded person. The condemnation marked a turning point not only in Van Helmont's personal trajectory but in the broader conflict between Paracelsian iatromechanical doctrines and Counter-Reformation Catholic orthodoxy in the Spanish Netherlands.
