The Twelve Gates

Type: TREATISE | Language: None | Composed: ca. 1475

The Twelve Gates is George Ripley's presentation of the Great Work as twelve sequential operations. The numbered structure allowed practitioners to memorize and teach the sequence, making it a pedagogically effective tool. Each gate represents both a physical operation in the laboratory and a metaphorical progression toward philosophical understanding.

Surviving in numerous manuscript copies, The Twelve Gates became a standard reference for understanding alchemical operations in English-speaking circles and beyond. Different manuscript versions show evidence of copying, annotation, and adaptation, indicating the text's continuous engagement by practitioners across generations.
