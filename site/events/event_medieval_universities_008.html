Scholarly Context

Paul Kristeller showed Bologna was a major center for medical and natural philosophical study, including alchemical topics, in *Renaissance Thought and its Sources* (1979).

c. 1296 CE, Bologna: Bologna's faculty of medicine and natural philosophy continues to expand its teaching in Operational Chemistry, incorporating new translations and commentaries on Jabir ibn Hayyan's works and the broader corpus of Islamic alchemical and pharmaceutical knowledge transmitted through Sicily and southern Italy. Medical students at Bologna study distillation and sublimation not only as theoretical knowledge but as practical skills required for pharmaceutical preparation and the production of concentrated medicinal essences. The convergence of alchemy, medicine, and natural philosophy in Bologna's curriculum reflects the practical value that universities attribute to alchemical operations as genuine chemical and medical techniques. This integration ensures that alchemical knowledge remains embedded in professional medical training.
