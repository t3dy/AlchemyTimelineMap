Scholarly Context

Craig Harline showed that Strasbourg was a major printing and manuscript center for alchemical texts in the 15th-16th centuries in *Pamphlets, Printing, and Political Culture* (2008) ch.6.

c. 1500, Strasbourg: Ecclesiastical and reform-era Strasbourg sat at the intersection of preaching, printing, medicine, and learned controversy. Alchemical writing could be read as useful natural knowledge, dangerous fraud, moral allegory, or medical promise, depending on the reader and institution. Tara Nummedal's work on the social risks of early modern alchemy and Lawrence Principe's synthesis both show that debates over Transmutation were also debates over credibility, authority, and fraud. Its historiographical significance is that Strasbourg places alchemy inside the religious and civic institutions that judged whether laboratory claims were legitimate, suspect, or useful.
