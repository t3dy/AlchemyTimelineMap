Scholarly Context

Danielle Jacquart and Francoise Micheau showed transmutation aspirations structured Arabic alchemical practice and medicine in *The Natural History of the Universe in Arabic Sources* (2008) ch.5.

c. 1614, Paris: The early seventeenth century sees increasing articulation of chemical-practice as a discipline distinct from both medieval alchemy and mechanical philosophy, with Parisian scholars proposing that chemistry constitutes a middle path between occultism and strict mechanism. This emergent chemistry claims to derive reliable knowledge from careful experiment and mathematical reasoning without recourse to transmutation or occult sympathy. The reframing of alchemical knowledge as chemistry enables practitioners to retain technical achievements while abandoning discredited metaphysics. The development of chemistry as a distinct discipline in early seventeenth-century Paris established the conceptual framework for modern chemical science.
