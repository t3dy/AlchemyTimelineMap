Scholarly Context

John Read documented this appointment from the Accounts of the Lord High Treasurer of Scotland in 'Alchemy Under James IV of Scotland' in Ambix vol. 2 no. 2 (1938) pp.60-67.

In March 1503–4, the Accounts of the Lord High Treasurer of Scotland record that James IV appointed his court alchemist John Damian as Abbot of Tungland in Galloway, explicitly to provide him with an ecclesiastical income and leisure for alchemical research. John Read, in Ambix (1938), quotes the Treasurer's entry: 'payit, be the Kingis command, to Bardus Altovite, Lumbard, for Maister Johne, the Franch medicinar, new maid Abbot of Tungland.' Read notes that the Scottish poet William Dunbar, who had coveted the Tungland appointment, satirized Damian in verse for neglecting his religious duties in favor of laboratory work: 'Unto no mess pressit this prelat, / For sound of sacring bell nor skellat…/ On him come now thir stole nor fannoun, / Ffor smowking of the smydy.' The appointment illustrates a distinctive feature of early modern royal alchemy patronage: the integration of alchemical practitioners into the ecclesiastical-courtly patronage network, providing them with sinecures that funded sustained laboratory work. Damian's subsequent activities show him continuing to draw on the Treasurer's accounts for materials and expenses through 1513.
