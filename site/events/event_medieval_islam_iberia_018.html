Córdoba

Spain

Scholarly Context

Danielle Jacquart and François Micheau demonstrated Cordoba was a major center for Arabic alchemical translation and synthesis in *La Science arabe* (1990).

c. 1016 CE, Córdoba: The Summa Perfectionis (Sum of Perfection) (Sum of Perfection), attributed to the foundational figure Jabir ibn Hayyan and representing the apex of systematic Islamic alchemical teaching and methodology, is widely known and actively studied in Cordoban scholarly circles. This authoritative comprehensive treatise details practical operations of Distillation, Fermentation, and graduated Transmutation experiments, providing both rigorous theoretical justification and concrete practical instruction for practitioners. Cordoban scholars recognize in the Summa an authoritative model for systematic alchemy—rigorous, empirically grounded, and properly philosophical—that validates their own investigative traditions and scholarly approaches. The text becomes an authoritative reference point for generations of scholars across the Islamic Mediterranean and, later crucially, in medieval Europe. Jabir becomes the canon's foundational authority.
