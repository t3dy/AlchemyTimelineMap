c. 1686 CE Early Modern (15th–18th c.)

Paris

France

c. 1686, Paris: The late seventeenth century marks the completion of alchemy's institutional eclipse in France, with chemistry emerging as the legitimate heir to alchemical knowledge about material transformation. Parisian academies and universities increasingly exclude transmutation from legitimate research agendas while maintaining laboratories for chemical work with therapeutic and industrial applications. The separation of chemistry from alchemy represents a selective inheritance—retaining operational achievements while abandoning metaphysical speculation. The late seventeenth-century emergence of chemistry from alchemy in Paris inaugurated the modern scientific discipline.
