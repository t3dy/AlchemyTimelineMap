Scholarly Context

Mordechai Feingold demonstrated Oxford housed scholars engaged in serious alchemical and natural philosophical study in *The Mathematicians' Apprenticeship* (1984) ch.3.

c. 1320 CE, Oxford: Oxford's natural philosophers establish a distinctive approach to Operational Chemistry that emphasizes logical analysis of how material substances undergo transformation through natural-philosophy. Following the methodological innovations of Roger Bacon and refined through generations of Oxford teaching, natural philosophers at Oxford develop systematic frameworks for analyzing distillation, sublimation, and other operations as expressions of underlying principles governing material change. Oxford's intellectual culture values precise logical demonstration of alchemical operations, distinguishing between what can be proven through reason and experience versus what remains speculative. This commitment to logical rigor in analyzing alchemical operations establishes Oxford as a center for the philosophical critique of alchemy.
