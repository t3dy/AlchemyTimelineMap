Scholarly Context

Deborah Harkness demonstrated that Lisbon was a crucial hub for the circulation of alchemical knowledge through maritime trade and printing in early modern Europe in *The Jewel House* (2007) ch.6.

c. 1566 CE, Lisbon: Portuguese physicians and alchemists publish treatises on medical-chemical practice, synthesizing Arabic, Jewish, and European alchemical traditions. These works systematically describe distillation apparatus, mineral acid production, and the preparation of quintessences through Operational Chemistry. The explicit claim to empirical validity—that these operations produce measurable, repeatable results—grounds Transmutation in observable chemical change rather than mystical allegory. Lisbon's scholarly networks, enriched by Sephardic Jewish exiles and their knowledge of medieval Arabic alchemy, create a uniquely cosmopolitan alchemical tradition. The integration of multiple traditions marks Lisbon as a major European center of alchemical synthesis and innovation.
