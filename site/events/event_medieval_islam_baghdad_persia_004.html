Scholarly Context

Charles Homer Haskins established Gerard of Cremona as the leading Latin translator of the Toledo school in *Studies in the History of Mediaeval Science* (1924) ch.5.

c. 748 CE, Baghdad: The translator Gerard of Cremona, though best known for his later work in Toledo, represents the eventual transmission pathway for Islamic alchemical knowledge into the Latin West. During the height of Abbasid intellectual activity in Baghdad, the mechanisms of translation and cultural exchange begin their long development. Islamic scholars including Al-Razi (Rhazes) and Jabir ibn Hayyan produce works on Distillation, Sublimation, and Calcination that will require Latin translation within centuries. This period establishes the intellectual foundation that Gerard and later translators will systematize.
