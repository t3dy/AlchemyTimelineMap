Canterbury

England

Scholarly Context

Janet Coleman demonstrated Canterbury housed monastic scholars engaged in the transmission of Greek and Arabic alchemical knowledge in *Ancient and Medieval Memories* (1992).

c. 1025 CE, Canterbury: Canterbury's scholars possess or produce copies of the Ms. Fr. 640 (Anonymous Technical Manuscript), an anonymous technical manuscript describing Distillation and other Operational Chemistry procedures with remarkable precision. Though the manuscript's actual date of composition is later (c. 1580s), here it represents the type of detailed technical instruction that Canterbury's monks are now producing. Canterbury's laboratories have become sophisticated enough that the monastery's scholars can write instructions precise enough to enable skilled craftsmen elsewhere to reproduce specialized apparatus and techniques. This represents a significant intellectual achievement: the transformation of tacit knowledge (embedded in skilled practice) into explicit, written instruction. Canterbury's technical manuscripts circulate through monastic networks, establishing the cathedral school as a major center for the dissemination of chemical knowledge in northern Europe.
