Scholarly Context

Lynn Thorndike identified the 1514 Venice printing as the first pseudo-Lullian alchemical text to appear in print, in Alchemy During the First Half of the Sixteenth Century in Ambix vol. 2 no. 1 (1938) pp.26-38.

In 1514, the first alchemical text from the vast pseudo-Lullian corpus appeared in print, when the Liber de Secretis Naturae seu de Quinta Essentia (Book of the Secrets of Nature, or On the Fifth Essence), attributed to Raymond Lull, was printed at Venice in the same volume as the medical Consilia of Giovanni Matteo Ferrari da Gradi, works of Maimonides, and works of Arnald of Villanova. Lynn Thorndike, in Ambix (1938), identifies this as the first printing of a pseudo-Lullian alchemical text and notes that the work itself comprised the preliminary dialogue with a monk (opening Contristatus erat Raymundus) and the Lullified version of John of Rupescissa's work on the fifth essence. The work was subsequently reprinted at Augsburg in 1518, and again at Venice 1521, Lyons 1535, Strasburg 1541, Venice 1542, and Nürnberg 1546, making it the most frequently reprinted Lullian alchemical text before the mid-century. Thorndike notes, however, that Philip Ulstad in his 1543 preface to the Caelum Philosophorum considered the Lullian alchemical corpus still little known, writing that he had seen the Mercuriorum librum, Testamentum, Codicillum, and other Raymond books reach only a few hands. The 1514 printing marks the beginning of the printed dissemination of pseudo-Lullian alchemy, which would eventually exert enormous influence on Ripley, Dee, Newton, and other English alchemists.
