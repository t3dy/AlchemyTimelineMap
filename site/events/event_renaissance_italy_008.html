Scholarly Context

Leah DeVun showed transmutation was pursued within mystical and theological frameworks by medieval and early modern alchemists in *Prophecy, Alchemy, and the End of Time* (2020) ch.4.

c. 1364, Florence: The Florentine scholar Benvenuto Cellini's grandfather begins his career as a metalworker and alchemist, combining practical Operational Chemistry with artistic vision in the creation of fine metalwork and jewelry. This generation witnesses the birth of the Renaissance master craftsman whose technical mastery encompasses both the practical distillation and refinement processes and the aesthetic principles of Transmutation. Cellini's legacy would later exemplify the fusion of alchemical operations with artistic genius that characterizes Italian Renaissance culture.
