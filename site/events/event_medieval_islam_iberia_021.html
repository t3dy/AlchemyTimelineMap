Scholarly Context

Leah DeVun showed transmutation was pursued within mystical and theological frameworks by medieval and early modern alchemists in *Prophecy, Alchemy, and the End of Time* (2020) ch.4.

c. 1052 CE, Toledo: The Liber XXIV Philosophorum (Book of Twenty-Four Philosophers) (Book of Twenty-Four Philosophers), a sophisticated compilation of natural philosophical wisdom and alchemical instruction, circulates actively in Toledo among Muslim and Christian scholars and academicians. This authoritative text presents orderly, systematic accounts of material substance, transformation processes, and the key operations of Distillation, Fermentation, and Transmutation within a coherent philosophical framework. Toledo's increasingly multicultural intellectual community—maintaining library collections and scholarly discourse in both Arabic and Latin—actively engages with this and other alchemical texts, thereby facilitating enhanced cross-cultural understanding. The Liber XXIV's growing circulation and influence marks Toledo's steadily strengthening role as a strategic nexus for transmitting advanced alchemical knowledge northward to European kingdoms. Multilingualism accelerates transmission.
