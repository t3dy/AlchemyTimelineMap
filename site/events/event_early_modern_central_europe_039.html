Scholarly Context

Michael McVaugh demonstrated that iatrochemical transmutation goals were pursued in medieval pharmaceutical contexts in *The Rational Surgery of the Middle Ages* (2006) ch.2.

c. 1735 CE, Vienna: After thirty-five years of experimental transmutation research, the Vienna alchemical laboratory's director concludes and publishes findings demonstrating that though material transformations are real and reproducible, the transmutation of base metals to gold exceeds the current capabilities of chemical knowledge. This institutional pivot—from failed proof of transmutation to systematic advancement of Operational Chemistry—marks the pragmatic boundary between alchemy and chemistry. The Vienna declaration, widely circulated among European scholars, establishes that operational success in distillation, metallurgy, and assay is valuable independent of transmutational claims. The publication signals to the international scholarly community that the transmutation question has been effectively abandoned by serious experimenters in favor of advancing the practical chemistry whose operations were always the real achievement of alchemical work.
