Scholarly Context

Lawrence Principe situated the transition from Greek alchemy to Islamic alchemy in late antiquity and the early medieval period in *The Secrets of Alchemy* (2013) ch.2.

c. 575 CE, Cairo: In the last decades of the sixth century, Cairo remains an important center for alchemical learning and practice, even as Byzantine political authority in Egypt grows increasingly tenuous. Alchemists working in this period inherit a sophisticated tradition of Operational Chemistry stretching back through Zosimos to Egyptian and Hellenistic craft practice. The Distillation apparatus, refined through six centuries of continuous innovation and use, has achieved a level of standardization that allows practitioners across vast distances to understand and replicate procedures. The preservation of alchemical texts in monastic libraries throughout Egypt and Syria ensures the survival of the discipline through the impending political and religious transformations. By 575 CE, alchemy has become thoroughly embedded in the Mediterranean intellectual landscape, recognized as a legitimate discipline by scholars, valued by practitioners for its practical applications, and preserved through institutional structures that transcend particular political authorities. The stage is set for the transmission of alchemical knowledge to the Islamic world, where it will flourish and undergo profound elaboration.
