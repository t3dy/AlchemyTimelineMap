Scholarly Context

Garth Fowden situated Constantinople as a bridge between Greek and Islamic alchemical traditions in *The Egyptian Hermes* (1986) ch.6.

c. 870 CE, Baghdad: The Emerald Tablet (Tabula Smaragdina), compiled from earlier Hermetic and alchemical traditions, circulates in Arabic translation and becomes foundational to Islamic alchemical theory. This text links Operational Chemistry to cosmological principles, providing philosophical grounding for Distillation and other material transformations. The Emerald Tablet's synthesis of Hermetic philosophy and chemical practice establishes a framework that will dominate European alchemy until the early modern period.
