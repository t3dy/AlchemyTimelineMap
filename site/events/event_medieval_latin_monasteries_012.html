c. 980 CE Medieval (7th–15th c.)

Monte Cassino

Italy

c. 980 CE, Monte Cassino: Monte Cassino's monks acquire or produce a copy of the Picatrix (Ghāyat al-Hakīm), the Latin translation of the Islamic magical and astrological treatise known as the Ghāyat al-Hakīm. While primarily concerned with astral magic, the Picatrix contains detailed descriptions of Operational Chemistry procedures embedded within its magical framework. Monte Cassino's scholars work to extract and evaluate the technical content of these operations, separating the practical chemistry from the astrological and magical claims. This effort exemplifies the medieval monastic approach: absorbing knowledge from Islamic sources regardless of context, then systematically extracting and reformulating it according to Christian and rational frameworks. Monte Cassino's engagement with the Picatrix establishes the monastery as a center for critical philosophical alchemy, where practical operations are studied independent of their magical framing.
