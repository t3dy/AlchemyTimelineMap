Scholarly Context

Paul Kristeller showed that Spanish court humanism included alchemical study and patronage in the 16th-17th centuries in *Renaissance Thought II* (1965) ch.4.

c. 1584 CE, Madrid: Spanish alchemists and apothecaries engaged in large-scale production of mineral acids, especially aqua fortis (nitric acid) through distillation, establish the technical and economic foundations of early modern chemical industry. The systematic Operational Chemistry of acid production transforms Transmutation from theoretical exercise into commercial enterprise. Madrid's royal patronage ensures access to materials and markets, allowing alchemists to operate at scales previously impossible. The emergence of alchemical industry marks a decisive shift in the discipline's status and significance: alchemy becomes economically consequential, producing substances essential to metallurgy, dye manufacture, and medicine.
