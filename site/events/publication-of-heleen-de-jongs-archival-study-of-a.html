Heleen M. E. de Jong's monumental study Michael Maier's Atalanta Fugiens: Sources of an Alchemical Book of Emblems is published by E. J. Brill in Leiden. Through systematic archival research and source analysis, de Jong traces Maier's borrowings from Arabic alchemical texts (Al-Razi, Jabir ibn Hayyan), medieval Latin translations (Gerard of Cremona, Roger Bacon), contemporary botanical and pharmaceutical knowledge, and classical natural philosophy. This genealogical method transformed scholarly understanding of the Atalanta from an isolated philosophical curiosity into a crucial document revealing how early modern alchemists synthesized medieval operational traditions with Renaissance erudition. De Jong's work established that the emblem book's visual-textual form was not decorative but epistemologically central: woodcuts and engravings encoded chemical procedures; epigrams provided philosophical interpretation; the composite form enabled transmission of tacit, embodied knowledge about laboratory practice.
