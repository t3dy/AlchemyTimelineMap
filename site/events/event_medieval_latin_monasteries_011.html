Scholarly Context

Irene Baldriga demonstrated that manuscript circulation networks preserved alchemical knowledge through medieval and early modern periods in *The Eyes of the Heart* (2011).

c. 965 CE, Canterbury: Canterbury's laboratories continue to expand in scale and reputation. Monks trained at Canterbury travel to establish or reform monastic houses elsewhere, carrying with them detailed knowledge of Distillation techniques and apparatus design. The cathedral school at Canterbury, increasingly separate from the monastic scriptorium, begins to engage with more theoretical aspects of Operational Chemistry, influenced by contacts with renewed Byzantine scholarship. Canterbury's position in England, which is beginning to develop stronger trade and intellectual ties to the continent, places it at a nexus of knowledge exchange. The monastery produces beautifully illuminated manuscripts of technical treatises, indicating that chemical knowledge is now considered worthy of the same scholarly respect as theology or natural philosophy. Canterbury's influence spreads through the monastic network, establishing England as a significant center for alchemical study.
