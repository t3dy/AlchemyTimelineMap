21 September 1583 Early Modern (15th–18th c.)

London

England

21 September 1583, Mortlake/London: John Dee and Edward Kelley depart England for the Holy Roman Empire, accompanied by Dee's family and the Polish nobleman Albrecht Łaski (palatine of Sieradz), whose pretensions to the Polish throne had received encouragement in the angelic conversations mediated by Kelley at Mortlake. The English party takes with them a substantial proportion of Dee's vast library — approximately 800 books — including alchemical manuscripts and works attributed to George Ripley. Jennifer M. Rampling, in 'John Dee and the Alchemists' (Studies in History and Philosophy of Science 43:3, 2012), identifies the departure as a turning point in the European reception of English Ripleian alchemy: the books Dee carried to the continent would pass through Kelley's hands into the collections of alchemical practitioners in Bohemia, seeding the networks that produced the first printed editions of Ripley's Latin works sixty years later. The party makes an inauspicious start at Cracow and Prague before settling in Třeboň (Wittingau) as clients of Vilém of Rožmberk (1535–1592), one of the greatest alchemical patrons in Bohemia.
