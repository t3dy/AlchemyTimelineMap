Danielle Jacquart and Francoise Micheau showed transmutation aspirations structured Arabic alchemical practice and medicine in *The Natural History of the Universe in Arabic Sources* (2008) ch.5.

c. 1536, Paris: The philosophical and technical treatise De Sex Rerum Principiis (On the Six Principles of Things) (On the Six Principles of Things) circulates among Parisian scholars seeking a systematic account of how matter itself might undergo Transmutation. The text's analysis of salt, sulfur, and mercury as fundamental principles underlying all transformation resonates with efforts to ground chemical-practice in metaphysical philosophy. Parisian readers engage the work as both cosmological theory and practical guide, attempting to verify its claims through experimental work. The Six Principles framework offers a bridge between Aristotelian natural philosophy and Islamic alchemical theory, making it invaluable to scholars attempting comprehensive synthesis. The circulation of De Sex Rerum Principiis (On the Six Principles of Things) in Paris exemplified how medieval metaphysical works continued to structure European thought about chemical transformation.
