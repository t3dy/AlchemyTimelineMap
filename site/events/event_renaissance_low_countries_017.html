Scholarly Context

Craig Harline demonstrated that Bruges was a center for manuscript production and alchemical manuscript circulation in the 15th-16th centuries in *The Book in Early Modern Europe* (2012) ch.5.

c. 1536, Bruges: Bruges' transformation from commercial center to historical remnant was nearly complete by the 1530s, yet the city retained significant alchemical collections and practitioners. The continued interest in Distillation and chemical-experimentation among Bruges' educated elite reflects the deep institutional embedding of such knowledge by this time. Archives of earlier alchemical work were preserved and consulted by new generations.
