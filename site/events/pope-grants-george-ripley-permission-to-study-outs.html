Scholarly Context

Jennifer M. Rampling cited and analyzed the papal letter of 1458-59 from the Calendar of Papal Letters XI (1893) in 'Establishing the Canon: George Ripley and his Alchemical Sources' in Ambix vol. 55 no. 3 (2008) pp.189-208.

A papal letter of 1458–1459, the only surviving contemporary document about George Ripley outside his own writings, granted 'George Ryphey... a canon of the Augustinian monastery or priory of Bitzidlington... in the diocese of York' the right to leave his priory 'and to dwell for seven years in an university, even without the realm of England, and study theology,' while holding in commendam benefices, on the proviso that he return at the end of the period. Jennifer M. Rampling, in Ambix (2008), identifies this papal dispensation — recorded in the Calendar of Entries in the Papal Registers Relating to Great Britain and Ireland: Papal Letters, vol. XI (1893) — as the sole external biographical evidence for Ripley outside his own writings, and notes that 'whether or not he ever returned, Ripley seems to have made good use of his student years.' The papal letter is consistent with the colophon dates of Ripley's two authenticated works: the Compound of Alchymy (colophon dated 1471) and the Medulla Alchimiae (colophon dated 1476) were both composed well within the period covered by the permission, raising the possibility that Ripley acquired his knowledge of pseudo-Lullian alchemy and of the works of Guido de Montanor during his years of licensed study on the Continent. Rampling notes that the historical Lull, who lived from 1232 to 1316, 'denied the possibility of alchemy,' while the vast corpus of pseudo-Lullian alchemical texts (over 120 texts attributed to him posthumously) constituted one of the two major literary foundations of Ripley's alchemical system.
