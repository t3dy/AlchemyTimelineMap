Scholarly Context

William Newman established that early modern London housed a vibrant community of practical alchemists and chymists engaged in experimental programs in *Gehennical Fire* (1994) ch.5.

c. 1554, London: During Mary I's reign, the ancient wisdom of the Emerald Tablet (Tabula Smaragdina) gains new prominence among English Catholic intellectuals seeking to recover the prisca sapientia before the Reformation disruptions. Multiple English translations and commentaries on Hermes Trismegistus's foundational aphorism circulate in manuscript, establishing this text as the philosophical bedrock of English alchemical thought for the next century. The Emerald Tablet becomes the single most quoted alchemical authority in Elizabethan England.
