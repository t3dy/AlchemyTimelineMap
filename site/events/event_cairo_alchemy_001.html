c. 1100 Medieval (7th–15th c.)

Cairo (Heliopolis/Babylon)

Egypt

c. 1100, Cairo: Fatimid and post-Fatimid Cairo stood within an Egyptian manuscript, medical, and craft environment where Hermetic reputation, pharmacy, and practical laboratory procedures could be recombined. Garth Fowden's account of Egyptian Hermetism and Lawrence Principe's broader synthesis both support treating Egypt as a durable symbolic and practical reference point, while also warning against imagining one uninterrupted Egyptian school of alchemy. The practices grouped here as Operational Chemistry include extraction, heating, separation, and preparation rather than proof of transmutation. Its historiographical significance is that Cairo helps connect late antique Egyptian authority to medieval Arabic learned and medical contexts without collapsing different periods into a single tradition.
