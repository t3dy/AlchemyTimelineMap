c. 1580 Early Modern (15th–18th c.)

Paris

France

c. 1580 CE, Paris: The technical manuscript Ms. Fr. 640 (Anonymous Technical Manuscript), likely compiled in Southern France but circulating in Paris through royal and merchant networks, documents detailed procedures for craft-practice in metallurgy, pharmacology, and the alchemical arts. The manuscript's preservation in the French royal library—acquired through royal confiscation or purchase—reflects emerging recognition that practical technical knowledge constitutes valuable patrimony. The manuscript's language (French rather than Latin) reflects its intended audience: practitioners, merchants, and artisans rather than university-trained scholars. The detailed procedural documentation of artisanal-knowledge—exact measurements, temperature management, apparatus configuration, timing—makes this Tacit Knowledge explicit and replicable. The manuscript exemplifies a crucial late sixteenth-century development: the emergence of the technical treatise documenting practical alchemy in vernacular languages accessible to non-Latin-educated practitioners. Paris's role as capital of France and seat of royal authority ensures that texts acquired by the royal library reach influential audiences of court scholars and patrons. The Ms. Fr. 640's circulation and preservation demonstrate that late sixteenth-century alchemy encompasses both elite philosophical theory and practical craft knowledge, both Latin erudition and vernacular practicality.
