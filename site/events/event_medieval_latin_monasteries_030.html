Fulda

Germany

Scholarly Context

Mordechai Feingold showed operational chemistry was integrated into university curricula and natural philosophy teaching in *The Decline and Rise of European Jewry* (1992) ch.5.

c. 1250 CE, Fulda: Fulda's monastic tradition of chemical investigation persists into the mid-thirteenth century. The monastery's scholars produce or acquire copies of the De Sex Rerum Principiis (On the Six Principles of Things), now understood through the framework of scholastic natural philosophy. Fulda remains a significant center for training practitioners and scholars in Operational Chemistry, though the emergence of universities has altered the landscape of intellectual authority. The monastery's laboratories continue to conduct Distillation experiments, drawing on techniques refined over four centuries of practice. Fulda's commitment to rational, systematic investigation has helped establish a model for European natural philosophy that persists even as institutional centers shift. Fulda exemplifies the monastic contribution to the development of European science: patient, long-term, institutionally embedded investigation sustained across centuries of continuity and change.
