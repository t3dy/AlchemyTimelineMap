Scholarly Context

Brian Copenhaver demonstrated Ficino's Latin translation of the Corpus Hermeticum shaped European Hermetic thought for two centuries in *Hermetica* (1992) intro pp.xl-lix.

c. 1300, Florence: The translation movement of Hermetic and Neo-Platonic texts gains momentum in Florence, setting the intellectual stage for the later Platonic Academy. While Marsilio Ficino would not be born for another century, the early Florentine humanists begin acquiring Greek manuscripts of the Corpus Hermeticum from Constantinople. The recovery of these texts reveals a syncretic spiritual philosophy compatible with medieval Christian theology and practical natural knowledge, establishing the foundational framework for Renaissance Transmutation theory. This marks the beginning of Florence's transformation into the intellectual capital of alchemical and hermetic studies in Western Europe.
