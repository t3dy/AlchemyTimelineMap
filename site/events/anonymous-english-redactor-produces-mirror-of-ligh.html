Scholarly Context

Peter Grund analyzed the Mirror of Lights's scholastic redactional strategies and argued for its composition in late 14th or early 15th century England, in 'Textual Alchemy: The Transformation of Pseudo-Albertus Magnus's Semita Recta into the Mirror of Lights' in Ambix vol. 56 no. 3 (2009) pp.202-225.

In the late fourteenth or early fifteenth century, an anonymous English scholar — probably with university training given his intimate familiarity with scholastic textual techniques — produced the Speculum luminum (Mirror of Lights) by radically reworking the pseudo-Albertan Semita recta. Peter Grund, in Ambix (2009), argues that the redactor's motivating goal was to provide a more rigorous defence of alchemy's legitimacy as a science than was offered by the Semita recta, which had some logical gaps in its introductory defence of alchemy. The redactor imposed a strict scholastic structure on his source, dividing the text into three clearly marked parts through a divisio textus at the opening: Part 1 investigates 'the truth of alchemy' through thirteen philosophical conclusions, eight objections, and eight solutions; Part 2 explicitly notifies the 'conditions pertaining to alchemy' (material, formal, and preceptual); Part 3 declares 'true and perfect procedures of alchemy.' This format exactly mirrors the dialectic structure found in the most prestigious academic theological treatises, especially Capreolus's Defensiones theologiae divi Thomae Aquinatis, composed in the early fifteenth century. The redactor's goal was to claim the same intellectual prestige for alchemy that such textual strategies implied. Grund notes that the Mirror of Lights survives in approximately eleven manuscripts, the overwhelming majority in Middle English rather than Latin — evidence that the vernacular version circulated more widely than the Latin original (if there was one). The complete three-part text survives only in Trinity College, Cambridge, MS O. 2. 33; most copies contain only Parts 2 and 3, suggesting use primarily as a practical handbook.
