c. 1670 Early Modern (15th–18th c.)

Cambridge

England

c. 1670, Cambridge: Isaac Newton copied, compared, and interpreted alchemical manuscripts while also developing mathematical natural philosophy. William Newman and Lawrence Principe have shown that Newton's chymical studies were not an embarrassing eccentricity but part of his larger investigation into matter, activity, and hidden processes. Cambridge matters here as a scholarly setting where manuscript alchemy, experiment, and natural philosophy overlapped. The category Operational Chemistry marks the laboratory and textual practices Newton studied without implying that his alchemical expectations were chemically correct. Its historiographical significance is that Newton forces historians to treat alchemy as part of early modern learned inquiry rather than as a discarded opposite of science.
