c. 290 CE Late Antiquity (3rd–7th c.)

Alexandria

Egypt

c. 290 CE, Alexandria: Zosimos of Panopolis completes crucial sections of his major treatises, including material later attributed to the De Sex Rerum Principiis (On the Six Principles of Things) tradition. This period marks Zosimos's mature synthesis of Greek philosophical concepts (drawn from Aristotle, the Stoics, and Neoplatonism) with practical chemical knowledge. His account of the six principles or primary substances reflects an attempt to provide theoretical grounding for the material transformations he observes in the laboratory. Zosimos argues that alchemy is a legitimate science because it studies the underlying principles of matter and demonstrates how those principles govern observable change. His integration of theory and practice establishes a template for medieval European alchemy, where transmutational ambitions would be framed within philosophical arguments about the nature of matter. Zosimos's mature work shows how chemical practice and natural philosophy can be unified, a synthesis that would profoundly influence the medieval and Islamic alchemical traditions.
