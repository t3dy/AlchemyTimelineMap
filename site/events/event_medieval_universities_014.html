c. 1368 CE Medieval (7th–15th c.)

Oxford

England

c. 1368 CE, Oxford: Oxford's faculty of natural philosophy continues teaching in Operational Chemistry, maintaining the university's distinctive emphasis on logical rigor in analyzing how material substances transform through distillation, sublimation, and other operations. The accumulated intellectual tradition at Oxford—refined through generations of teaching and debate—produces scholars capable of critically engaging with alchemical claims while acknowledging the genuine chemical knowledge embedded in alchemical practice. Oxford's culture of scholarly skepticism applied to alchemy establishes a model for critical engagement with alchemical texts that will influence Renaissance and early modern natural philosophy.
