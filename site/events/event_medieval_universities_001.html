Scholarly Context

Pamela Smith showed that Paris universities integrated alchemical and medical knowledge as part of the broader natural philosophy curriculum in *The Body of the Artisan* (2004) ch.4.

c. 1212 CE, Paris: The expanding University of Paris incorporates teaching on Operational Chemistry, drawing heavily on the medical and alchemical authorities of Al-Razi (Rhazes) (Rhazes), whose systematic accounts of distillation, acid production, and pharmaceutical preparation dominate the Faculty of Medicine. Al-Razi's emphasis on observable, repeatable operations in metallurgy and substance manipulation becomes the model for natural philosophy teaching at Paris, where alchemy is positioned as part of practical knowledge rather than mystical speculation. The integration of al-Razi's methods into the Parisian curriculum establishes a lasting bridge between Islamic practical alchemy and European medical and philosophical training, ensuring that alchemical operations become normalized within the highest academic institutions.
