c. 1150 Medieval (7th–15th c.)

Baghdad

Iraq

c. 1150 CE, Baghdad: The great Abbasid caliphate, though intellectually vital, shows signs of political fragmentation and diminished state patronage. However, Baghdad remains the preserve of accumulated alchemical manuscripts, apparatus collections, and a living community of practitioners engaged in Operational Chemistry. The city's markets, hospitals, and private workshops continue producing medicines through distillation and crystallization techniques perfected over four centuries. Though state-sponsored research may decline as political power disperses, the practical embedding of alchemy in medicine and pharmacy ensures continuity. The collection of texts attributed to Jabir ibn Hayyan and al-Razi, preserved in Baghdad's libraries and in private collections, forms the intellectual capital that will eventually reach Europe through translation. Baghdad at mid-twelfth century represents not alchemy's decline but its maturation into a stable, economically embedded craft tradition. The production of reliable medicines, cosmetics, and industrial substances through alchemical techniques becomes self-sustaining, requiring no special state patronage beyond the market demand for high-quality pharmaceutical and cosmetic products.
