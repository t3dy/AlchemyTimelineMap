Scholarly Context

Mordechai Feingold showed operational chemistry was integrated into university curricula and natural philosophy teaching in *The Decline and Rise of European Jewry* (1992) ch.5.

c. 1236 CE, Prague: The newly founded University of Prague incorporates alchemical and natural philosophical instruction from its establishment, influenced by the empiricist approach pioneered by the Franciscan Roger Bacon, whose insistence on experimentation and observable evidence reshapes how alchemy is taught in Northern European universities. Bacon's study of the Kitāb al-Ḥāsib, attributed to Jabir ibn Hayyan, provides Prague scholars with a model of systematic natural inquiry in which alchemical operations are valued for their demonstrable results in distillation, acid production, and metallurgical processes. Prague's integration of Baconian empiricism into its natural philosophy curriculum establishes Central Europe as a center for the experimental investigation of alchemical operations.
