Scholarly Context

Janet Coleman demonstrated that Paris university natural philosophy included alchemical theory and practice in 13th-14th centuries in *Medieval Readers and Writers* (1981) ch.6.

c. 1692, Paris: The final decades of the seventeenth century see alchemy firmly established as a historical subject, with Parisian scholars treating chemical-practice and Transmutation theory as phenomena requiring historical explanation rather than contemporary endorsement or refutation. The institutionalization of history as a learned discipline enables serious engagement with alchemical texts and traditions without contemporary scientific commitment. This historical approach allows modern chemists and natural philosophers to acknowledge alchemy's technical contributions while maintaining methodological skepticism toward transmutation claims. The historicization of alchemy in late seventeenth-century Paris enabled its scholarly recovery.
