Scholarly Context

Allen Debus demonstrated Paracelsus's tria prima replaced Aristotelian elements with chemically observable principles, grounding chemical medicine in *The Chemical Philosophy* (1977) vol.1.

c. 1524 CE, Madrid: Paracelsus (Theophrastus Bombastus von Hohenheim), the revolutionary Swiss physician and alchemist, influences medical and alchemical thought across Europe, and his doctrines penetrate Spanish intellectual circles through translated works and correspondence. Paracelsus's insistence that Operational Chemistry and pharmaceutical preparation constitute genuine knowledge challenges scholastic Galenism and elevates the alchemist-physician to the status of natural philosopher. His theory of Transmutation through understanding the active principles (arcana) of substances reframes alchemy as chemical medicine rather than mystical fantasy. Spanish physicians and apothecaries embrace Paracelsian doctrines, creating a lasting tradition of medical-alchemical practice that would flourish through the seventeenth century.
