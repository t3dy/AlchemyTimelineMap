Scholarly Context

Lindberg, David C. demonstrated Bacon's defense of alchemy in <em>Roger Bacon's Philosophy of Nature</em> (1983) and <em>The Beginnings of Western Science</em> (1992), ch. 7, pp. 228–267.

Roger Bacon completes his Opus Majus, a monumental treatise defending experimental science and alchemy as essential to understanding nature. Bacon systematically argues that alchemical operations reveal truths about matter inaccessible through theoretical speculation alone, and that repeatable, observable results of alchemical practice demonstrate its intellectual legitimacy. His defense of alchemy against charges of superstition—treating transmutation as a serious question requiring investigation rather than settling it as fact or dismissing it as impossibility—profoundly influences medieval scholarly attitudes toward alchemy. Through Bacon's advocacy, alchemy gains support as a legitimate discipline worthy of university study and patronage.
