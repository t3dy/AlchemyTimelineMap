c. 260 CE Late Antiquity (3rd–7th c.)

Alexandria

Egypt

c. 260 CE, Alexandria: Zosimos of Panopolis refines his understanding of Distillation and Operational Chemistry in Alexandria's thriving craft quarters, where glassmakers, perfumers, and metalworkers demonstrate advanced technical knowledge. The city's role as a center of pharmaceutical and cosmetic production provides Zosimos with direct access to practitioners who employ distillation in the creation of perfumes, medicines, and metal alloys. His treatises increasingly incorporate technical terminology derived from workshop practice, such as names for apparatus, descriptions of heating methods, and accounts of material changes observable during chemical processes. This period consolidates Zosimos's reputation as the authoritative voice on alchemy in the Hellenistic world. His commitment to recording procedures with precision and clarity sets a standard that later alchemists in Islamic, Byzantine, and European contexts would both emulate and reinterpret, making Zosimos the effective founder of alchemy as a textually-mediated discipline.
