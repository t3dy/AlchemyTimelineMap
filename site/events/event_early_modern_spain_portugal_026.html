Scholarly Context

Paul Kristeller showed that Spanish court humanism included alchemical study and patronage in the 16th-17th centuries in *Renaissance Thought II* (1965) ch.4.

c. 1656 CE, Madrid: Spanish alchemists, increasingly influenced by mechanical philosophy and nascent scientific empiricism, begin reconceptualizing Operational Chemistry in terms of particle motion and collision rather than sympathetic magic. The theoretical sophistication of Madrid's alchemists now encompasses both traditional hermetic philosophy and emerging mechanical frameworks. Transmutation remains the ultimate goal, but its means are reframed in terms compatible with new natural philosophy. Madrid exemplifies alchemy's capacity for theoretical self-renovation, adapting to new intellectual currents while maintaining commitment to the empirical investigation of material transformation.
