Scholarly Context

Newman, William R. established Jabir's operational sophistication in <em>The Summa Perfectionis of Pseudo-Geber</em> (1991) and <em>Atoms and Alchemy</em> (2006), ch. 2, pp. 78–124. Kraus, Paul provided definitive source analysis in <em>Jābir ibn Ḥayyān: Essai sur l'histoire des idées scientifiques dans l'Islam</em> (1942).

During the Abbasid intellectual flourishing, Jabir ibn Hayyan develops a comprehensive system of alchemy integrating Zosimian operational knowledge with theoretical innovation. He proposes the Sulfur-Mercury Theory of metal composition and documents over 100 chemical operations with sufficient precision for replication. His synthesis of Hellenistic natural philosophy, Egyptian practical chemistry, and Persian-Arabian learning creates a coherent alchemical discipline. Jabir's works, transmitted to the Islamic West and eventually to medieval Europe through Gerard of Cremona's translations, become the primary vehicle for transmitting operational alchemy to European practitioners.
