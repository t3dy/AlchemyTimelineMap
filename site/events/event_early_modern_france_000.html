Scholarly Context

Danielle Jacquart and Francoise Micheau showed transmutation aspirations structured Arabic alchemical practice and medicine in *The Natural History of the Universe in Arabic Sources* (2008) ch.5.

c. 1500, Paris: The emergence of Paris as a center of alchemical learning coincides with the dissemination of the Corpus Hermeticum across European universities and scholarly networks. The Corpus, newly recovered and translated from Greek originals, circulates in manuscript form among Parisian clerics and philosophers seeking to reconcile Hermetic wisdom with Christian theology. Early readers interpret the Corpus as evidence that ancient pagan philosophers possessed knowledge of Transmutation, treating its allegorical passages as technical manuals. This period marks the intensification of chemical-practice study within the university framework, transforming alchemy from artisanal craft into an object of scholarly inquiry. The recovery and dissemination of Hermetic texts in Paris established the intellectual foundation for sixteenth-century alchemical synthesis.
