Scholarly Context

Raphael Patai analyzed Zosimos's quotations of Maria the Jewess in 'Maria the Jewess–Founding Mother of Alchemy' in Ambix vol. 29 no. 3 (1982) pp.177-197.

Zosimos of Panopolis (fl. late 3rd–early 4th century CE), the most important early Greek alchemical author, extensively quoted and paraphrased Maria the Jewess throughout his alchemical writings, treating her as the supreme authority in the alchemical tradition and repeatedly calling her 'the divine Maria,' 'the first' of the ancient authors, and the repository of alchemical wisdom. Raphael Patai, in Ambix (1982), identifies Zosimos's writings as the primary source of knowledge about Maria: 'the earliest, most detailed, and most reliable information about Maria the Jewess is contained in the writings of Zosimus.' Zosimos quoted Maria's descriptions of the tribikos (three-pronged copper still) and other apparatus at length, apparently reproducing large portions of her treatise Kaminographia (On Apparatus and Furnaces); he stated that he was unable to treat the subject of furnaces 'more authoritatively than was done in that ancient treatise.' He also quoted her alchemical axioms, including the famous formula 'one becomes two, two becomes three' — which Christianos, the early seventh-century anonymous author, summarized as the 'Hebrew prophetess' saying 'one becomes two, two becomes three, and by means of the third the fourth achieves unity; thus two are but one.' Zosimos's treatment of Maria's knowledge as uniquely authoritative, combined with his general view of the Jews as 'the repositories of alchemical wisdom,' makes his quotations of Maria the single most important vehicle through which her work was preserved and transmitted to subsequent Greek, Arabic, and Latin alchemical traditions.
