Scholarly Context

Michael McVaugh demonstrated that iatrochemical transmutation goals were pursued in medieval pharmaceutical contexts in *The Rational Surgery of the Middle Ages* (2006) ch.2.

c. 1704 CE, Prague: In the court of Holy Roman Emperor Joseph I, alchemists and natural philosophers conduct systematic experiments on Transmutation in newly established laboratory-practice facilities. The Prague laboratory represents a paradigm shift from medieval conjecture to quantitative measurement and repeated observation of chemical processes. Rather than consulting classical texts, experimenters implement standardized apparatus, document observations in parallel trials, and compare results across multiple operators. This institutional setting—combining court patronage, systematic record-keeping, and coordination with other European centers—marks the emergence of alchemy as an experimental discipline subject to verification and replication. The Prague model bridges medieval textual alchemy and early modern chemistry, establishing procedures that demand evidence rather than authority.
