Scholarly Context

Newman, William R. discusses Avicenna's influence on alchemy and medicine in <em>Atoms and Alchemy</em> (2006), pp. 78–112.

Avicenna (Ibn Sina), the preeminent Persian physician and philosopher, composes his monumental Canon of Medicine, which synthesizes Greek medical theory with Islamic pharmaceutical knowledge and alchemical medicine. The Canon emphasizes chemical preparation of medicines through distillation, extraction, and refinement, establishing alchemy as essential to medical education. Avicenna's philosophical framework provides theoretical grounding for understanding material transformation through natural principles, integrating alchemy within a comprehensive natural philosophy. His work becomes the standard medical authority in European universities for centuries, profoundly influencing how Western medicine understood the role of chemical pharmacy and alchemical knowledge in therapeutic practice.
