c. 900 Medieval (7th–15th c.)

Ray (Rhages)

Persia

c. 900 CE, Ray (near modern Tehran): The alchemist and physician Al-Razi (Rhazes), sometimes called Rhazes in Latin texts, conducts his systematic investigations of mineral substances, metals, and pharmaceutical preparations in his laboratory at Ray. Al-Razi's Book of Secrets documents careful procedures for Distillation of mineral acids, Calcination of metals, and crystallization of pharmaceutical compounds. His methodology—repeated trials, careful observation of color changes, precise documentation of proportions—establishes al-Razi as the most rigorously empirical alchemist of the Islamic Golden Age. Working independently of court patronage, he develops distillation apparatus with improved condensation chambers and fuel management. His classification of substances into mineral, vegetable, and animal categories, each with characteristic chemical behaviors, advances alchemical theory beyond previous taxonomies. Al-Razi's integration of alchemy with practical pharmacy and medicine—producing reliable medicinal preparations from alchemical operations—demonstrates the tangible utility of laboratory craft. His influence extends through countless Arabic manuscripts and later European adaptations, making al-Razi the model of the rational, empiricist alchemist.
