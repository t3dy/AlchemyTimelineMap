Scholarly Context

William Newman traced Jabir's corpus as the foundational Arabic synthesis that established the sulphur-mercury theory as the dominant framework for metallic composition in *The Summa Perfectionis* (1991).

c. 780 CE, Baghdad: Jabir ibn Hayyan, now in the later phase of his intellectual career, refines his theoretical framework for the seven basic operations of alchemy, emphasizing Sublimation—the direct transformation of solid to vapor—and Calcination, the reduction of substances to ash through intense combustion. Jabir's elaboration of these operations moves beyond mere description toward systematic categorization grounded in observed chemical properties and product characteristics. His systematic investigation of mercury's behavior under heat, particularly its tendency to sublime and recondense, furnishes empirical grounding for theories of metallic transformation. These operations, conceptualized as manipulations of elementary qualities and principles, establish the foundation for all subsequent Islamic and medieval European alchemy. Jabir's systematic operational chemistry—treating the laboratory as a site of rational investigation rather than mystical revelation—marks a decisive shift in alchemy's intellectual status within the Islamic world, elevating it from marginal esotericism to a recognized branch of natural philosophy worth serious scholarly engagement.
