Scholarly Context

Lindberg, David C. examined Albertus's natural philosophy in <em>The Beginnings of Western Science</em> (1992), pp. 283–298. Weisheipl, James A. edited <em>Albertus Magnus and the Sciences</em> (1980), establishing Albertus's engagement with alchemy.

Albertus Magnus, teaching at Cologne University, systematically engages with alchemical texts including the Summa Perfectionis and incorporates alchemical operational knowledge into his comprehensive natural philosophy. His integration of Aristotelian hylomorphism (matter-form theory) with alchemical understanding of substantial change provides theoretical grounding for treating alchemical operations as philosophically legitimate. Albertus demonstrates that medieval scholars can engage with alchemical material operations at sophisticated technical levels while maintaining philosophical rigor. His intellectual authority—stemming from mastery of Aristotle and compatibility with Christian theology—establishes precedent that alchemy deserves serious scholarly attention. Through students including Thomas Aquinas, Albertus's integration of alchemy into scholasticism becomes influential across European universities.
