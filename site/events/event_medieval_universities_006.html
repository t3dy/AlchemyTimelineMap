c. 1272 CE Medieval (7th–15th c.)

Oxford

England

c. 1272 CE, Oxford: The natural philosopher teaching at Oxford encounters and begins circulating the De Sex Rerum Principiis (On the Six Principles of Things), a Latin treatise on the six principles underlying material transformation, which synthesizes Aristotelian metaphysics with alchemical and medical theory. The text's systematic treatment of how matter manifests through different forms and transformations provides Oxford scholars with a philosophical framework for understanding Operational Chemistry as legitimate natural inquiry rooted in universal principles rather than arbitrary practice. De Sex Rerum Principiis becomes a standard reference work in Oxford's Faculty of Natural Philosophy, offering an intellectual bridge between abstract metaphysical principle and empirical alchemical operation.
