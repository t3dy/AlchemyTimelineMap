Scholarly Context

Michael McVaugh established that Salerno medical school integrated alchemical theory and practice into university medical training in *The Rational Surgery of the Middle Ages* (2006) ch.3.

c. 1150, Salerno: The medical school and translation culture of Salerno made southern Italy a major site for the movement of Arabic and Greek medical knowledge into Latin practice. Recipes, materia medica, and apparatus-based preparation gave Distillation a practical medical setting before alchemy became firmly embedded in later university and courtly networks. Michela Pereira's work on medieval medical alchemy and Lawrence Principe's history of chymistry both emphasize that healing, extraction, and drug preparation often legitimized laboratory operations. Its historiographical significance is that Salerno shows alchemy entering Latin Europe not only as a theory of metals, but also as a set of useful techniques for medicine and pharmacy.
