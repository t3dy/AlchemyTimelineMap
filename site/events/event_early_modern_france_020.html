France

c. 1620, Paris: The continued royal patronage of alchemical research in early seventeenth-century France, despite mounting skepticism from the emerging scientific establishment, testifies to the persistent political appeal of Transmutation. Parisian court alchemists maintain laboratories and publish treatises defending chemical-practice against critics who deny the possibility of true Transmutation. The coexistence of alchemical research and mechanical philosophy within early seventeenth-century French intellectual culture demonstrates the complex transition from medieval to modern science. Royal sponsorship of alchemical research in Paris extended into the seventeenth century even as academic reputation declined.
