Corpus Hermeticum

Type: TREATISE | Language: None | Composed: c. 100–300

The Corpus Hermeticum is a collection of philosophical and mystical texts attributed to Hermes Trismegistus, the legendary Egyptian sage. Composed in Greek during the second and third centuries CE, these texts synthesize Platonic and Neoplatonic philosophy with Egyptian religious traditions and Gnostic thought. The Corpus Hermeticum profoundly influenced medieval and Renaissance alchemy through its teaching of universal correspondences—the principle that operations performed on matter mirror cosmic transformations, and that understanding the hidden connections between different orders of reality enables the practitioner to work with nature toward transformation. The central teaching of Hermetic philosophy—that the human being contains in microcosm all the principles of the macrocosm—became fundamental to how alchemists understood transmutation as participation in divine transformation. Renaissance scholars, particularly after the 15th-century recovery and translation of the Corpus Hermeticum into Latin, integrated Hermetic philosophy into alchemical theory, creating the Hermetic-Neoplatonic synthesis that characterized Renaissance alchemy.
