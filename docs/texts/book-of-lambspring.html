Book of Lambspring

Type: TREATISE | Language: None | Composed: c. 1300

The Book of Lambspring is a medieval alchemical treatise of disputed authorship and unclear origins, known for its elaborate emblematic imagery and philosophical treatment of the Great Work. The work presents the alchemical process as a spiritual journey, depicting the union and transmutation of opposing principles (often represented as King and Queen, or Red King and White Queen) through a series of mystical transformations. The Book of Lambspring circulated widely in the late medieval and Renaissance periods, particularly in German-speaking lands, where it influenced alchemical illustration and theory. Modern scholarship remains uncertain about whether the work represents a genuinely medieval composition or a later Renaissance synthesis. Nevertheless, the Book of Lambspring became canonical in Renaissance and early modern alchemy, shaping how practitioners understood the relationship between spiritual development and material transformation.
