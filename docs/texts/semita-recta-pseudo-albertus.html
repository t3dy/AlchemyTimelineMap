Semita Recta (Pseudo-Albertus Magnus)

Type: TREATISE | Language: None | Composed: ca. 13th century CE

The Semita recta (The Straight Path) is an alchemical treatise attributed to the Dominican friar and natural philosopher Albertus Magnus (ca. 1200–1280), but almost certainly pseudepigraphic, composed by an unknown author probably during the thirteenth century and surviving in more than one hundred manuscripts from the thirteenth century through the seventeenth century. Peter Grund, in Ambix (2009), identifies the Semita recta as an alchemical 'bestseller' from the thirteenth century onward: unlike many alchemical texts, it is focused on practical procedures, logically structured, and almost entirely free of the metaphorical language characteristic of many alchemical treatises. The text covers, in order: an introduction including errors of other alchemists and a defence of alchemy's validity; descriptions of alchemical substances and equipment; theoretical and practical sections on sublimation, calcination, dissolution, and distillation; and, finally, recipes for the elixir and for transmutation. Pearl Kibre's research has demonstrated convincingly that Albertus did not write the Semita recta or the many other alchemical texts attributed to him in the Middle Ages; the false attribution was a strategy for appropriating the authority of the greatest natural scientist of the medieval period in order to lend credibility to an art that was regarded with suspicion by many scholars and clergy. The Semita recta was translated into several vernacular languages from the fifteenth century onward, and appeared in printed editions including the Borgnet collected edition of Albertus Magnus's authentic writings (Paris, 1898). A redaction of the Semita recta produced in late medieval England — the Speculum luminum or Mirror of Lights, probably composed in the late fourteenth or early fifteenth century — survives in more than ten manuscripts, mostly in Middle English.
