Bücher und Schrifften des Paracelsus (Huser Edition)

Type: COMPILATION | Language: None | Composed: 1589–1591 CE

The Bücher und Schrifften des Paracelsus (Books and Writings of Paracelsus), edited by Johannes Huser (ca. 1545–1600/1601), published in 10 volumes at Basel by Conrad Waldkirch (1589–1591), with a surgical supplement edited by Huser in Strasbourg by Lazarus Zetzner (1605), constitutes the most important early collected edition of Paracelsus's medical and natural-philosophical writings and set the textual canon for more than three centuries. Urs Leo Gantenbein, in Ambix (2020), identifies the Huser edition as the foundational text event in Paracelsus scholarship: before the Huser edition, Paracelsus's works had circulated in scattered and frequently corrupted early printings; Huser's ten-volume collection, based on manuscript consultation and editorial judgment, gave readers their first systematically organized access to the Paracelsian corpus. The edition was sufficiently authoritative that readers encountering it 'would hardly have expected Paracelsus to have left behind an at least equally extensive theological oeuvre,' since Huser deliberately excluded the theological writings as too controversial in the post-Reformation era of hardening confessional boundaries. This selective editorial decision meant that Paracelsus's theology remained largely unknown until partial editions in the seventeenth century (beginning with Johannes Staricius in 1618) and systematic modern editions in the twentieth century (Karl Sudhoff's critical edition, 1922–1933, followed by Gantenbein's New Paracelsus Edition of the theological works, 2008).
