The Marrow of Alchemy (Eirenaeus Philoponos Philalethes)

Type: TREATISE | Language: None | Composed: 1654-1655 CE

The Marrow of Alchemy is an alchemical poem published in two parts in 1654 and 1655, attributed to Eirenaeus Philoponos Philalethes with prefaces bearing anagrams of the name George Stirk (i.e. George Starkey, 1628-1665). William Newman, in Ambix (1990), demonstrates that the Marrow was authored by Starkey himself. The Marrow presents Philoponos Philalethes as a pupil who received the white elixir (capable of transmuting mercury to silver) from a mysterious English-born Cosmopolite -- a figure Newman shows to be a literary invention based on the Sendivogian legend. Newman demonstrates that Starkey derived his antimonial processes from Alexander von Suchten's Mysteria gemina, and these same processes underlie both the Marrow and the Introitus apertus. William Cooper's 1669 English printing of the Introitus created the canonical name Eyrenaeus Philaletha Cosmopolita for Starkey's fictional adept. The Marrow influenced Isaac Newton's alchemical practice.
