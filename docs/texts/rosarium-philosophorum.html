Rosarium Philosophorum

Type: TREATISE | Language: None | Composed: c. 1550

The Rosarium Philosophorum (Garden of Philosophers) is one of the most elaborately illustrated alchemical treatises of the Renaissance, known for its rich emblematic imagery depicting the stages of the Great Work. Traditionally attributed to Arnaud de Villanova or other medieval authors, the work was likely composed or compiled in the sixteenth century and circulated widely in printed form. The Rosarium combines practical laboratory operations with mystical and spiritual interpretation, presenting alchemical transformation as simultaneously material, philosophical, and spiritual. The work's detailed engravings—depicting laboratory apparatus, the union of royal and solar/lunar principles, and stages of transformation—made it enormously influential on Renaissance and early modern alchemists. The Rosarium remains one of the most visually striking and philosophically complex alchemical texts, synthesizing medieval operational alchemy with Renaissance Neoplatonic philosophy.
