Eisagoge in artem medicam à Paracelso restitutam (Van Helmont)

Type: TREATISE | Language: None | Composed: 1607 CE

Eisagoge in artem medicam à Paracelso restitutam (Introduction to the Medical Art as Restored by Paracelsus, 1607) is an unpublished treatise by Jan Baptist van Helmont (1579–1644), composed in 1607 and written in the tradition of the Danish physician Petrus Severinus (1540–1602), whose Idea medicinae philosophicae (1571) had presented Paracelsus as a restorer of Hippocratic medicine within a Neoplatonic humanist framework. Georgiana D. Hedesan, in Ambix (2021), identifies the Eisagoge as Van Helmont's first properly Paracelsian treatise, and as the text that contains his account of his dramatic conversion vision: on 24 September 1599, on the banks of the River Schelde near Antwerp, he had a vision of a great palace where alchemical adepts were gathered, with Hermes Trismegistus ruling over them, and the ghost (Evestrum) of Paracelsus showing him alchemical secrets. The Eisagoge follows Severinus's argument that Paracelsus had restored the true ancient medicine of Hippocrates. Van Helmont's manuscript was preserved unpublished; it was first edited and printed by Corneille Broeckx in the Annales de l'Académie d'archéologie de Belgique (1853–1854). Together with his three unpublished commentaries on Hippocratic works (written 1608–1634), the Eisagoge represents Van Helmont's early attempt to reconcile Paracelsian alchemy with Neoplatonic Hippocratism, a synthesis later superseded by his more original work in the Ortus medicinae (1648).
