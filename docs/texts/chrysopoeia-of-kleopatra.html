Chrysopoeia of Kleopatra

Type: PRIMARY_SOURCE | Language: None | Composed: ca. 2nd-4th century CE

The Chrysopoeia of Kleopatra (Gold-Making of Kleopatra) is one of the earliest surviving Greek alchemical texts. H. J. Sheppard, in Ambix (1957), identifies the Chrysopoeia as the earliest known alchemical text containing the Ouroboros symbol -- the serpent devouring its tail -- accompanied by the inscription hen to pan (The All is One). Alongside the Ouroboros is a variant symbol of three concentric circles with the planetary signs for gold, silver, and mercury, accompanied by the axioms: One is the serpent which has its poison according to two compositions; and One is All and through it is All and by it is All. The text also contains fragments of dialogues with mystic themes capable of dual interpretation as either the death and regeneration of the soul or the revivification of metals. Sheppard sees strong Valentinian Gnostic affinities: the eight-rayed star adjacent to the Ouroboros recalls the Valentinian Ogdoad. The Chrysopoeia is known primarily through Berthelot, Collection des anciens alchimistes grecs (Paris, 1888).
