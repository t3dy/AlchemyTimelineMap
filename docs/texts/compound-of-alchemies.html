The Compound of Alchemies

Type: TREATISE | Language: None | Composed: ca. 1470

The Compound of Alchemies is George Ripley's major systematic treatise presenting alchemy as a methodical science with clear stages and operations. The work became one of the most widely copied and influential English alchemical texts, circulating in both manuscript and print throughout the early modern period.

The Compound exemplifies Ripley's approach: a blend of Latin-learned philosophy with vernacular English accessibility, organized pedagogy, and practical laboratory concern. The text's emphasis on systematic stages—distillation, sublimation, putrefaction, fermentation, calcination—provided a pedagogical framework for teaching alchemy to English-speaking practitioners. Modern scholarship treats the Compound as foundational to understanding how alchemy functioned as a structured discipline with recognizable stages of mastery.
