Phlogiston

Category: ACTOR_TERM

Phlogiston was a theoretical principle posited by Georg Ernst Stahl (c. 1703) to explain combustion, calcination, and related chemical transformations: a subtle, invisible substance present in combustible bodies that was released during burning and calcination, leaving behind a calx (ash or metal oxide). This is an ACTOR_TERM — eighteenth-century chemists explicitly invoked phlogiston in their theoretical language and structured experimental programs around its detection and recovery.
