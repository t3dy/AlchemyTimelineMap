Johann Rudolph Glauber

Role: CHEMIST | Era: EARLY_MODERN

Johann Rudolph Glauber (1604–1670) was a German-Dutch chemist and alchemist whose experimental investigations of mineral acids and salts represent a crucial transition from alchemy toward modern chemistry. Working in Amsterdam and Cologne during a period of intense alchemical and commercial activity, Glauber conducted systematic experiments with mineral salt preparations, documenting the production of distinctive crystalline compounds with reproducible properties. His discovery of what became known as Glauber's salt (sodium sulfate decahydrate) exemplifies the period's fusion of alchemical ambition with genuine chemical discovery. Glauber's emphasis on producing commercially valuable compounds rather than pursuing transmutation shifted the focus of alchemy toward practical chemical manufacture, demonstrating how alchemical methods could generate economically significant innovations. His work influenced the emergence of chemical manufacturing in early modern Europe and contributed to the transformation of alchemy into applied chemistry.
