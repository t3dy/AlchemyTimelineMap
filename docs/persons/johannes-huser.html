Johannes Huser

Role: PHYSICIAN | Era: EARLY_MODERN

Johannes Huser (ca. 1545–1600/1601) was a German physician and editor, known primarily as the compiler and publisher of the first complete critical edition of the works of Paracelsus. Urs Leo Gantenbein, in 'Cross and Crucible: Alchemy in the Theology of Paracelsus' (Ambix 67:1, 2020), identifies Huser's editorial project as the foundational act that shaped all subsequent reception of Paracelsus: by gathering, transcribing, and ordering Paracelsus's scattered and hitherto mostly manuscript writings into a coherent corpus, Huser effectively created the Paracelsus whom later centuries would read and debate.

Huser's Bücher und Schrifften des edlen, hochgelehrten und bewehrten Philosophi und Medici Philippi Theophrasti Bombast von Hohenheim, Paracelsi genannt was published in ten volumes at Basel by Conrad Waldkirch between 1589 and 1591, followed by the Chirurgische Bücher und Schrifften (Strasbourg: Lazarus Zetzner, 1605). Together these volumes — conventionally cited as H1–H10 — constitute the standard text of Paracelsus's medical and natural philosophical writings used by all subsequent scholars until the twentieth-century critical edition by Karl Sudhoff. Huser's editorial methodology was meticulous for its time: he sought out manuscripts, compared readings, and attempted to identify authentic Paracelsian texts against the considerable body of spurious works circulating under Paracelsus's name. The 1589–1591 Huser edition fixed the canon of Paracelsus's works and made his ideas available to the broad reading public of learned Europe — its publication in Basel was a landmark event in the history of Paracelsian medicine and alchemy.
