Thaddeus the Physician

Role: PHYSICIAN | Era: MEDIEVAL

Thaddeus of Bologna (c. 1100–1165) was a medieval Italian physician whose integration of Islamic alchemical and pharmaceutical knowledge into European medical practice helped establish the legitimacy of chemical pharmacy within university medicine. Working at the University of Bologna during the period of intense translation and transmission of Islamic medical texts, Thaddeus engaged with pharmaceutical alchemy and chemical preparation of medicines. His medical treatises incorporate discussion of distillation, sublimation, and chemical refinement of medicinal substances, demonstrating how Islamic operational alchemy was being absorbed into European medical education. Thaddeus represents the crucial moment when pharmaceutical alchemy became embedded in university curricula and recognized as an essential component of medical knowledge.
