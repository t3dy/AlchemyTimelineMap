Samuel Norton

Role: ALCHEMIST | Era: MEDIEVAL

Samuel Norton (c. 1548–1604) was an English alchemist and great-great-grandson of Thomas Norton, the fifteenth-century English alchemist who composed the Ordinal of Alchemy. Samuel Norton composed several alchemical texts of his own, including the Mercury Revived and the Mercurius Redivivus, and he pursued alchemical practice throughout his career, reportedly performing experiments for patrons and maintaining correspondence with other practitioners. He represents the persistence of English alchemical practice across the sixteenth century and into the Elizabethan period, in a tradition that traced itself explicitly back to his ancestor's work and to George Ripley's poetry. Jennifer Rampling's analysis of Norton and his milieu in The Experimental Fire (2020) provides the most thorough recent scholarly treatment.

Literature

Principe, Lawrence M. The Secrets of Alchemy. University of Chicago Press, 2013.
Rampling, Jennifer M. The Experimental Fire: Inventing English Alchemy, 1300–1700. University of Chicago Press, 2020.
