Agostino Nifo

Role: SCHOLAR | Era: EARLY_MODERN

Agostino Nifo (ca. 1469, Sessa Aurunca or Nifo near Salerno – after 1538) was an Italian Aristotelian philosopher and prolific commentator at the University of Naples, Pisa, Rome, and Salerno. He is one of the most important Renaissance commentators on Aristotle's natural philosophical corpus, producing extensive commentaries on the Physics, De anima, Meteorologica, and other works. His In libris Aristotelis Meteorologicis commentaria (Venice, 1560 composed ca. 1503–1520) addresses the alchemical implications of the fourth book of the Meteorologica, which dealt with the generation and transformation of minerals and metals.

Craig Martin, in 'Alchemy and the Renaissance Commentary Tradition on Meteorologica IV' (Ambix 51:3, 2004), documents that Nifo was among the most hostile of the Renaissance commentators toward the claims of practical alchemy. He denied outright the possibility of artificial transmutation of base metals into gold, on the Aristotelian grounds that art is weaker than nature and cannot alter the specific forms ('substantialforms') of metals. Nifo also objected even to the name 'alchimia', regarding it as a barbarous and unclassical term. His negative attitude reflected both his conservative Aristotelianism and his humanist preference for classical Latin terminology. Despite his rejection of metallic transmutation, Nifo acknowledged the possibility of chemical change (alteratio) within species — for example, the coagulation of mercury — but not transmutation across species boundaries. His views on alchemy influenced the Florentine humanist Benedetto Varchi, who cited Nifo's commentary in his disputation on alchemy of 1544.
