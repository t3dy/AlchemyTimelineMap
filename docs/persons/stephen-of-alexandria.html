Stephanus of Alexandria

Role: SCHOLAR | Era: MODERN

Stephanus of Alexandria (fl. c. 610–640) was a late antique alchemist and natural philosopher whose treatises represent the transition from Zosimosian alchemy to Islamic and Byzantine traditions. Working in Alexandria during the tumultuous period of Persian and Arab invasions, Stephanus documented chemical operations and theoretical frameworks that would influence both Byzantine and Islamic alchemy. His surviving works demonstrate engagement with Zosimos's texts and the broader Hermetic and Neoplatonic philosophical frameworks. Stephanus emphasized the physical reality of alchemical operations while integrating them within mystical and cosmological systems. His work represents a crucial moment in the transmission of alchemy from late antique Alexandria to the medieval Islamic world, bridging the gap between Zosimos of Panopolis and Jabir ibn Hayyan.
