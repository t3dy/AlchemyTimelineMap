Ludwig Combach

Role: PHYSICIAN | Era: EARLY_MODERN

Ludwig Combach (1590–1657) was a German physician and alchemical editor who served as ordinary physician to Moritz, Landgraf of Hesse-Kassel (1572–1632), and his successors Wilhelm V and Wilhelm VI. His editions of alchemical texts represent the culmination of a manuscript tradition rooted in the alchemical court culture of Rudolf II's Prague: the primary exemplar for his edition of George Ripley's works was Kassel Landesbibliothek 4º MS chem. 67, a codex that had been annotated by the Prague alchemist Nicolaus Mai (who received it through the circle of Edward Kelley) and contained recipe fragments attributed to Kelley himself.

Combach's two major editorial projects were the Tractatus aliquot chemici singulares (Geismar, 1647), a compilation of works attributed to English, Dutch, French, German, and Italian alchemists including John Dastin, John Belye, and Kelley; and the Opera omnia chemica (Kassel, 1649), the first attempt to assemble the complete works attributed to George Ripley (d. ca. 1490). The preface to the Opera is a careful exercise in editorial and apologetic strategy: Combach opens with a denunciation of fraudulent practitioners, enumerates the chief alchemists by nation (following Michael Maier's model in the Symbola aureae mensae), defends Pseudo-Raymond Lull against the attacks of the Lotharingian physician Nicolas Guibert, and closes with a measured endorsement of Ripley's clarity and his close relationship to pseudo-Lullian sources. Jennifer M. Rampling, in 'Transmission and Transmutation' (Early Science and Medicine 17, 2012), analyses the editorial strategy of the Opera and the relationship between Combach's textual tradition and the Kelley-Mai manuscript network. Portions of Combach's preface were translated by Elias Ashmole and incorporated into the Theatrum Chemicum Britannicum (London, 1652).
