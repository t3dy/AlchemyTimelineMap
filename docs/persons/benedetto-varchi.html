Benedetto Varchi

Role: SCHOLAR | Era: EARLY_MODERN

Benedetto Varchi (1503, Florence – 18 December 1565, Florence) was a Florentine humanist, historian, poet, and amateur natural philosopher, known for his Storia Fiorentina (history of Florence), his activities in the Accademia degli Umidi (later the Accademia Fiorentina), and his wide-ranging literary and philosophical production. He spent many years in Padua under the patronage of Cardinal Alessandro Farnese (later Paul III), where he encountered the Aristotelian natural philosophy of the northern Italian universities.

In 1544 Varchi delivered a disputation at the Accademia Fiorentina on the question of alchemy, later published as the Questione sull'alchimia (circulating in manuscript; first printed Florence: Magheri, 1827). Craig Martin, in 'Alchemy and the Renaissance Commentary Tradition on Meteorologica IV' (Ambix 51:3, 2004), identifies this as a significant case study: Varchi, drawing heavily on the Aristotelian Meteorologica commentary tradition and particularly on the arguments of Agostino Nifo and Lodovico Boccadiferro, presented the philosophical arguments against metallic transmutation to a Florentine court audience, translating the technical debates of university philosophy into the vernacular humanist context. The Questione reflects Varchi's characteristic approach: the careful marshalling of classical authorities, the presentation of opposing arguments, and the final suspension of judgment. The disputation was delivered in Florence in an intellectual environment shaped by Cosimo de' Medici's court and represents one of the clearest examples of how Aristotelian anti-alchemical arguments circulated beyond the university into humanist literary culture.
