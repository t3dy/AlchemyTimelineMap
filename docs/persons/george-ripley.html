George Ripley

Role: ALCHEMIST | Era: MEDIEVAL

George Ripley (ca. 1415–ca. 1490) was an English alchemist and one of the most influential figures in medieval and early modern English alchemy. His systematic corpus of alchemical writings, including The Compound of Alchemies and The Twelve Gates, became canonical texts in the English alchemical tradition. Jennifer Rampling's research establishes Ripley as the central figure in a distinct English alchemical intellectual tradition, characterized by vernacular writing, practical experimentation, and systematic pedagogy.

Ripley's significance lies in systematizing alchemy into teachable stages that combine theoretical philosophy with practical laboratory operations. His works were copied and taught for centuries, creating a continuous intellectual tradition. The Ripley corpus represents one of the most important vernacular alchemical heritages in early modern Europe.
