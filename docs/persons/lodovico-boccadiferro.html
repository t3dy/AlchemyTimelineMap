Lodovico Boccadiferro

Role: SCHOLAR | Era: EARLY_MODERN

Lodovico Boccadiferro (1482–1545) was a prominent Italian Aristotelian philosopher and professor of natural philosophy at the University of Bologna, known for his lectures on Aristotle's Meteorologica, which were published posthumously as Lectiones in librum IV Meteororum (Venice: F. De Franceschi, 1563). He was one of the leading Aristotelian commentators in northern Italy in the early sixteenth century.

Craig Martin, in 'Alchemy and the Renaissance Commentary Tradition on Meteorologica IV' (Ambix 51:3, 2004), documents Boccadiferro's sharply sceptical attitude toward alchemy. In his lectures on the fourth book of the Meteorologica, he explicitly rejected the claims of alchemists ('io non credo istis chymicis') on Aristotelian grounds, arguing that mercury cannot be coagulated as alchemists claimed, and that those who sought the philosopher's stone wasted their patrimonies in pursuit of something they could never achieve. His rejection was more emphatic and less nuanced than that of Agostino Nifo, whom Martin compares him to, and his views were influential on Benedetto Varchi, who cited both Nifo and Boccadiferro in his Questione sull'alchimia (1544).
