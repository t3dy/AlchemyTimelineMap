William Backhouse

Role: ALCHEMIST | Era: MEDIEVAL

William Backhouse (1593–1662) was an English gentleman, translator, and alchemical practitioner associated with the circle of Elias Ashmole, who claimed to have transmitted the secret of the Philosophers' Stone to Ashmole verbally on his deathbed in 1651 — a transmission Ashmole recorded in his diary as occurring "by his bed's side" and receiving "his secret." Backhouse translated several alchemical texts from Latin and French, including portions of the pseudo-Paracelsian corpus, and was an active collector of alchemical manuscripts. He is most significant historically as an example of the network of educated English gentlemen who maintained alchemical interests in the early and mid-seventeenth century, and as the specific individual through whom Ashmole claimed to have received an orally transmitted alchemical secret, illustrating the period's culture of initiated transmission.
