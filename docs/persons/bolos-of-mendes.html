Bolos of Mendes (Pseudo-Demokritos)

Role: SCHOLAR | Era: MODERN

Bolos of Mendes (fl. ca. 200 BCE) was an Egyptian natural philosopher from the Nile Delta town of Mendes who is regarded by modern scholars as a likely originator of the theoretical tradition in Western alchemy. H. J. Sheppard, in Ambix (1957), draws on Max Wellmann and A. J. Festugiere to identify Bolos as the probable source of both the technical content of the Leiden and Stockholm papyri and the theoretical-practical framework of the text of Pseudo-Demokritos (the Physika kai Mystika). Bolos authored or compiled a work on the tinting of metals entitled Baphika (On Dyeing), one of the earliest systematic attempts to extend traditional Egyptian metallurgical practice toward genuine transmutation through philosophical theory. Bolos also composed texts under the persona of the Greek atomist Democritus, establishing the practice of pseudonymous attributions that would characterize alchemical literature through the medieval period.
