Bernard Georges Penot

Role: PHYSICIAN | Era: EARLY_MODERN

Bernard Georges Penot (du Port, ca. 1519–1617), also styled Bernardus Georgius Penotus a Portu Sanctae Mariae Aquitanus, was a French Paracelsian physician and alchemical editor who published the first Latin version of George Ripley's work: an abbreviated prose translation of the Compound of Alchymy, titled Axiomata philosophica or 'Epitome of the Twelve Gates,' in a Frankfurt collection of 1595. Penot was also the editor of Gaston DuClo's Apologia chrysopoieae et argyropoeiae adversus Thomam Erastum (Geneva, 1598), a key defence of alchemical transmutation against the criticism of Thomas Erastus. His circle overlapped with Nicolas Barnaud, whom he had met in Prague; Barnaud's later and more complete edition of the Ripley Latin text (1599) was explicitly positioned as a supplement to Penot's 1595 extract.

Eugène Olivier's biographical study (edited and revised by Didier Kahn, Chrysopoeia 5, 1992–1996) establishes Penot as a remarkable figure who combined active medical practice, alchemical compilation, and polemic across a publishing career spanning several decades. His 1595 Frankfurt collection — in which Ripley's axioms are placed alongside the Dialogus of Ægidius de Vadis, axioms of Albertus Magnus, a work by Pseudo-Trithemius, and a fragment of Isaac Hollandus — is a deliberate construction of international alchemical authority, mustered to defend the truth of the art against its critics. Jennifer M. Rampling, in 'Transmission and Transmutation' (Early Science and Medicine 17, 2012), demonstrates that Penot's compilation was reprinted in the Theatrum chemicum (1602) immediately preceding a reprint of DuClo's Apologia, making Ripley's axioms part of an explicit apologetic sequence against Erastus's critique.
