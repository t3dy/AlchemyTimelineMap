Jean-Paul Filleau

Role: SCHOLAR | Era: MODERN

Jean-Paul Filleau (1524–1597) was a French alchemist and scholar whose systematic investigations of distillation and mineral acids contributed to the emergence of practical chemistry in Renaissance Europe. Working in Lyon, a major printing and scholarly center, Filleau engaged with both medieval alchemical texts and contemporary experimental work, attempting to systematize chemical operations and ground them in repeatable procedures. His writings on the preparation of mineral acids and the analysis of metals demonstrate the degree to which Renaissance alchemy was grounded in careful observation and technical precision. Filleau represents the intellectual milieu in which alchemy was increasingly embedded within emerging scientific method and Renaissance natural philosophy.
