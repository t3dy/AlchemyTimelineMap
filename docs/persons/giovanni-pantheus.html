Giovanni Agostino Pantheus

Role: SCHOLAR | Era: MODERN

Giovanni Agostino Pantheus (fl. 1515-1535), described on his title pages as a priest and refiner of gold of Venice (De Vinegia Affinatore de oro et sacerdote), was a Venetian clergyman and alchemical author who published two important alchemical works under the patronage of Pope Leo X and the Venetian Council of Ten. Lynn Thorndike, in Ambix (1938), identifies Pantheus as the author of the Ars Transmutationis Metallicae (Venice, 1518), dedicated to Pope Leo X, and the Voarchadumia contra alchimiam (Venice, 1530), which despite ostensibly disavowing alchemy in favor of a newly-named art called Voarchadumia (a supposed cabala of metals handed down from Tubal Cain) nevertheless repeated most of the 1518 material. Pantheus apparently published the Ars after obtaining permission from the Apostolic Legate and the Council of Ten, then in 1530 was required by a papal decretal (Pope John XXII's Spondent quas non exhibent) and a Venetian decree against alchemists to reframe his work. Both the Voarchadumia and the 1518 work were reprinted together at Paris in 1550 and in Zetzner's Theatrum Chemicum (1613 and 1659). His later publication was a Lunario perpetuo (Venice, 1535).
