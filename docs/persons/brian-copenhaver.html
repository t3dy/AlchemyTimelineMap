Brian P. Copenhaver

Role: SCHOLAR | Era: MODERN

Brian P. Copenhaver (born 1937) is Distinguished Professor Emeritus of History and Philosophy at the University of California, Los Angeles, whose critical edition and translation Hermetica: The Greek Corpus Hermeticum and the Latin Asclepius in a New English Translation (Cambridge University Press, 1992) provided the first reliable modern English translation of these foundational texts with full scholarly apparatus, establishing the standard reference text for anglophone study of Hermetic philosophy. His subsequent work Magic in Western Culture: From Antiquity to the Enlightenment (Cambridge University Press, 2015) provided a comprehensive intellectual history of magic as a learned tradition from the ancient Near East through the Renaissance, situating Marsilio Ficino, Pico della Mirandola, and other Renaissance Neoplatonists within a continuous history of philosophical reflection on magic rather than as aberrations from a rationalist norm.

Central Contribution

Copenhaver's translation of the Corpus Hermeticum was a landmark because earlier English versions (including the Victorian translation by G.R.S. Mead) were philologically unreliable and ideologically driven; Copenhaver provided a translation based on the best available Greek and Latin texts with notes addressing the specific philosophical and religious content of each tractate. His introductory and contextual materials drew on the scholarship of André-Jean Festugière, who had provided the foundational philological analysis of the Hermetic texts in his four-volume French study, and on Garth Fowden's historical contextualization, synthesizing this work for an English-language scholarly audience. The translation established what the Hermetic texts actually say, as opposed to what later interpreters claimed they said, a distinction of considerable importance for the historiography of Western esotericism.
