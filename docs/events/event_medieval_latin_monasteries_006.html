Scholarly Context

Deborah Harkness demonstrated that operational chemistry in early modern period involved precise measurement and record-keeping as forms of knowledge production in *The Jewel House of Art and Nature* (2007) ch.4.

c. 890 CE, Fulda: The monastery at Fulda acquires or produces copies of the De Sex Rerum Principiis (On the Six Principles of Things), a Latin treatise on the fundamental principles of material reality. This text, likely compiled from earlier Neoplatonic and Islamic philosophical sources, attempts to ground the observable changes studied in Operational Chemistry within a coherent metaphysical framework. Monks at Fulda engage with the work's arguments about the nature of substance, quality, and transformation, seeking to understand not merely how to perform Distillation but why such operations produce their characteristic results. The intellectual effort to synthesize practical chemistry with natural philosophy strengthens at this period. Fulda's scholars begin to distinguish between mystical claims about transmutation and the legitimate study of material properties, establishing critical distance from purely alchemical speculation while maintaining commitment to operational investigation.
