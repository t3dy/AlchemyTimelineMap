Scholarly Context

Michael McVaugh showed Arnaud de Villanova positioned chemical medicines within orthodox Galenic medicine rather than against it in *Medicine before the Plague* (1993) ch.5.

c. 1300, Montpellier: The medical university of Montpellier became a major context for Latin discussions of alchemical medicine, especially the preparation of remedies through separation and extraction. Texts associated with Arnaud de Villanova and the wider pseudo-Lullian tradition linked laboratory operations to healing, longevity, and the refinement of substances. Michela Pereira's scholarship is especially important for showing how medical alchemy developed through textual attribution, practice, and institutional medicine. Distillation mattered because it could be defended through pharmacy even when metallic transmutation remained contested. Its historiographical significance is that Montpellier shows how medicine gave alchemy practical authority within medieval learned culture.
