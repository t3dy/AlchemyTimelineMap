1595 Early Modern (15th–18th c.)

Frankfurt

Germany

1595, Frankfurt: The French Paracelsian physician Bernard Penot (ca. 1519–1617) publishes a Frankfurt collection of alchemical tracts (Egidii de Vadis Dialogus …) containing the first Latin version of work attributed to the English alchemist George Ripley (d. ca. 1490): an abbreviated prose translation of the Compound of Alchymy titled Axiomata philosophica (or 'Epitome of the Twelve Gates'). The compilation places Ripley's axioms alongside the Dialogus of Ægidius de Vadis, axioms of Albertus Magnus, a work attributed to Pseudo-Trithemius, and a fragment by Isaac Hollandus, constructing an explicitly international alchemical authority. Jennifer M. Rampling, in 'Transmission and Transmutation' (Early Science and Medicine 17, 2012, pp.485-486), demonstrates that Penot drew on an existing French manuscript tradition of Ripley's Liber duodecim portarum but excised the controversial sections on fraudulent alchemists and the 'Admonition' describing Ripley's failed experiments, pruning the text for apologetic purposes. The 1595 compilation was reprinted in the Theatrum chemicum (1602) alongside a defence of alchemy against Thomas Erastus, and provided the textual basis upon which Nicolas Barnaud later compiled the more complete Quadriga aurifera (1599) and upon which Andreas Libavius based his 'Analysis duodecim portarum' commentary.
