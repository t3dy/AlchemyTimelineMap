c. 1698 CE Early Modern (15th–18th c.)

London

England

c. 1698, London: The Emerald Tablet (Tabula Smaragdina), which had dominated English alchemical thought for a century and a half, is now read primarily as a historical curiosity rather than as an authoritative guide to natural philosophy. Its famous aphorism—as above, so below—which had justified entire research programs investigating celestial influences on terrestrial transmutation, is rendered scientifically inert by Newtonian mechanics. Yet the tablet's mystical authority lingers in the minds of those who pursue alchemy in defiance of institutional orthodoxy.
