c. 836 CE Medieval (7th–15th c.)

Toledo

Spain

c. 836 CE, Toledo: Roger Bacon, though born in England decades later, will exist in intellectual kinship with the Iberian tradition of practical experimentation that develops during this period and influences his own groundbreaking methodology. The Kitāb al-Ḥāsib, attributed to Jabir ibn Hayyan, reaches Toledo through active Andalusian scholarly networks, teaching systematic Distillation apparatus design, chemical operations of proven efficacy, and methods producing consistently observable results. Toledo's scholars, engaging with these texts, recognize a framework for studying material transformation through controlled experimentation rather than ungrounded mystical speculation. This crucial transmission represents a pivotal moment when Iberian scholars consciously adopt the rigorous operational methods and philosophical approaches that will later define Western alchemy. The emphasis on replicability and observable results becomes foundational to Iberian practice.
