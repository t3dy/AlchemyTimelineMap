Scholarly Context

Ursula Klein demonstrated that chemical laboratory apparatus enabled new forms of systematic experimentation in 18th-century chemistry in *Experiments, Models, Paper Tools* (2003) ch.4.

c. 750 CE, Baghdad: Jabir ibn Hayyan, the Arab polymathprovisioning, begins compiling his systematic treatise on practical alchemy, the Kitāb al-Ḥāsib, as Baghdad emerges as the intellectual center of the Islamic world under the Abbasid caliphate. Jabir's work synthesizes earlier Hellenistic and Egyptian protocols with Arabic mathematical rigor and Persian patronage. His formalization of Distillation—fractional separation of substances through heat-driven vaporization and condensation—establishes it as a repeatable, teachable operation grounded in reproducible physical phenomena rather than mystical assertion. The Kitāb al-Ḥāsib describes apparatus construction, temperature management, and product verification with unprecedented precision. Jabir's integration of Operational Chemistry with theoretical metallurgy and medicine establishes him as foundational to the Islamic Golden Age of alchemy. His corpus would dominate European alchemy for eight centuries after Latin translation, making Jabir's Baghdad synthesis the single most influential alchemical voice in the Western tradition.
