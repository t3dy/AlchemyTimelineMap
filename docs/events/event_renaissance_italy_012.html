c. 1396 CE Medieval (7th–15th c.)

Florence

Italy

c. 1396, Florence: A Florentine scholar translates Picatrix (Ghāyat al-Hakīm) (Ghāyat al-Hakīm) from Arabic into Latin, making this foundational text on astrological Transmutation and celestial-terrestrial correspondences widely accessible to Western European intellectuals. The translation integrates Arabic cosmological theory with Neoplatonic philosophy, providing a coherent framework for understanding how Operational Chemistry participates in universal harmonic principles. This work becomes a standard reference for Renaissance alchemists and astrologers seeking to understand the deeper metaphysical significance of their operations.
