Scholarly Context

Andrew Pettegree showed the Low Countries were crucial nodes in print-enabled alchemical knowledge dissemination in *The Book in the Renaissance* (2010) ch.7.

c. 1616, Bruges: The early 17th century saw Bruges continuing to serve as a repository of historical alchemical knowledge and practice. The study of Kitāb al-Ḥāsib, the medieval Islamic technical manual attributed to Jabir ibn Hayyan, remained important for practitioners seeking authoritative frameworks for Distillation and chemical-experimentation. The city's manuscripts and libraries continued to attract scholars even as political and economic forces reshaped the Low Countries.
