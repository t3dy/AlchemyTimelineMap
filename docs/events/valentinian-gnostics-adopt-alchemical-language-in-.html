Scholarly Context

H. J. Sheppard analyzed the Valentinian Gnostics adoption of alchemical language, drawing on Sagnard's study of Valentinian Gnosis, in Gnosticism and Alchemy in Ambix vol. 6 no. 2 (1957) pp.86-101.

The Valentinian Gnostic sect, active in Alexandria in the first half of the second century CE, adopted alchemical terminology and processes as analogies for their cosmological doctrines. H. J. Sheppard, in Ambix (1957), analyzes F. M. M. Sagnard's study of Valentinian Gnosis to recover the alchemical language embedded in the theological writings reported by Church Fathers Irenaeus (ca. 133-203 CE) and Clement of Alexandria (ca. 155-220 CE). Sagnard identified multiple parallels: the term ebulliens applied to Sophia recalls one of the classical means of separating the pure from the impure; the Spirit (pneuma) signifies in alchemy the product of sublimation; and the Gnostic cosmological terms above and below directly correspond to alchemy's distillation and sublimation processes. Sheppard concludes that the Valentinian sect must have been conversant with alchemical texts compiled no later than ca. 100 CE -- calling to mind the school of Maria the Jewess (fl. ca. 200 CE) with its emphasis on distillation and sublimation. The mutual borrowing between Gnosticism and alchemy forms what Sheppard identifies as the distinctive character of Alexandrian intellectual culture in the first three centuries CE: alchemy and Gnosticism were contemporary in origin, both drawing on Stoicism and Oriental doctrines.
