c. 1580 Early Modern (15th–18th c.)

Toulouse

France

c. 1580 CE, Toulouse: An anonymous technical manuscript, Ms. Fr. 640, is compiled in Toulouse or transmitted through Toulouse's craft and merchant networks, documenting practical knowledge of alchemical, metallurgical, pharmaceutical, and artistic operations. The manuscript exemplifies Artisanal Epistemology—the claim that practitioners' hands-on experience constitutes valid knowledge distinct from and potentially superior to book-learning. The Ms. Fr. 640 preserves Tacit Knowledge—knowledge embedded in craft practice and transmitted through apprenticeship—in written form. The manuscript's compilation in Toulouse reflects the city's position as significant craft center with active metallurgical, textile, and pharmaceutical industries. The anonymous author's decision to commit tacit knowledge to writing, to create a transmissible record of operative secrets, reflects growing recognition that practical knowledge merits preservation and transmission to wider audiences. The Ms. Fr. 640 represents the voice of actual practitioners—metallurgists, apothecaries, artisans—whose knowledge had previously remained embedded in workshop practice. The manuscript's eventual preservation through the French royal library ensures its recognition by subsequent centuries' scholars as crucial document of late sixteenth-century practice. The Ms. Fr. 640 demonstrates that alchemy in this period exists not merely as elite philosophical pursuit but as active craft practice among urban artisans and merchants.
