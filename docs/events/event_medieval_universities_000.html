c. 1200 CE Medieval (7th–15th c.)

Bologna

Italy

c. 1200 CE, Bologna: The recovery and circulation of Arabic alchemical texts begins at the nascent University of Bologna, where scholars encounter the works of Jabir ibn Hayyan (known as Geber in the Latin West) through earlier translations. The Corpus Hermeticum, rediscovered through Byzantine and Islamic intermediaries, enters the European curriculum alongside systematic treatments of Operational Chemistry drawn from Jabir's systematic accounts of distillation and metal dissolution. Bologna's legal and natural philosophy faculties begin incorporating alchemical texts as legitimate extensions of Aristotelian natural-philosophy, establishing alchemy as a university discipline. This marks the institutional legitimization of alchemy in Latin Europe, transforming it from marginal practice into scholastic inquiry grounded in reproducible operations.
