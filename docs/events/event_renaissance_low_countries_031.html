Scholarly Context

Andrew Pettegree showed the Low Countries were crucial nodes in print-enabled alchemical knowledge dissemination in *The Book in the Renaissance* (2010) ch.7.

c. 1648, Bruges: The mid-17th century marked the transition from alchemy as living tradition to alchemy as historical knowledge preserved in archives. Bruges' role in this transition was primarily conservatorial: the city maintained manuscripts, libraries, and institutional memory of the Low Countries' long engagement with Distillation and chemical-experimentation. The Peace of Westphalia (1648) fundamentally reordered European politics, creating new conditions for the development of modern chemistry.
