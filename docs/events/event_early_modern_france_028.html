c. 1668 CE Early Modern (15th–18th c.)

Paris

France

c. 1668, Paris: The later seventeenth century finds Parisian intellectuals increasingly viewing alchemy as a historical phenomenon rather than a living discipline, with scholars documenting alchemical texts and theories as objects of antiquarian interest. This historiographical approach enables serious engagement with alchemical content without endorsing transmutation claims. The shift toward treating chemical-practice and Transmutation as historical categories rather than current research programs marks alchemy's relegation from active science to history of science. The historiographical treatment of alchemy in late seventeenth-century Paris positioned it as an intelligible precursor to modern chemistry.
