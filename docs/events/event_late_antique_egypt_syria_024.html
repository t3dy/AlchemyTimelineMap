c. 560 CE Late Antiquity (3rd–7th c.)

Alexandria

Egypt

c. 560 CE, Alexandria: In the late sixth century, alchemical knowledge becomes increasingly intertwined with Hermetic and mystical traditions, particularly through texts like the Corpus Hermeticum, which synthesizes Greek philosophy, Egyptian wisdom traditions, and mystical spirituality. The Corpus Hermeticum, attributed to Hermes Trismegistus (the thrice-great Hermes), presents a vision of the cosmos in which material transformation reflects underlying spiritual principles. Alexandria, as the legendary home of Hermetic wisdom, becomes the symbolic center for this synthesis of chemistry and mysticism. By 560 CE, alchemists understand their practice as participation in a grand cosmos in which Operational Chemistry serves not only practical goals but also spiritual transformation. This Hermetic framework, which would profoundly influence medieval and Islamic alchemy, reflects the Late Antique impulse to find unity among diverse intellectual traditions. The integration of alchemical practice with Hermetic philosophy demonstrates alchemy's capacity to accommodate multiple interpretative frameworks while maintaining its core commitment to observable material transformation.
