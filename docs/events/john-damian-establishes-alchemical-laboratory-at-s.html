Scholarly Context

John Read analyzed the Accounts of the Lord High Treasurer of Scotland (1473–1513) in 'Alchemy Under James IV of Scotland' in Ambix vol. 2 no. 2 (1938) pp.60-67.

John Damian, a French or Italian physician and alchemist attracted to the Scottish court of James IV, established an alchemical laboratory at Stirling Castle under active royal patronage. John Read, in Ambix (1938), analyzed the contemporary Accounts of the Lord High Treasurer of Scotland (Compota Thesaurariorum Regum Scotorum, vols. i–iv, Edinburgh 1877–1916), which record detailed expenditures on furnaces, apparatus, materials, wages, and Damian's personal provisions from 1501 through James IV's death at Flodden in 1513. Read identifies James IV as an unusually active and scientifically minded royal patron whose interest in alchemy was driven primarily by the medicinal applications of the Philosopher's Stone and Elixir of Life — the quinta essencia — rather than purely by gold-making. The Treasurer's accounts provide exceptional documentary evidence for the day-to-day costs of maintaining a royal alchemical establishment: furnaces and apparatus, imported materials including gold, silver, and quicksilver, wages for laboratory assistants ('ministers'), travel expenses for Damian's continental visits to procure materials, and extraordinary household costs. The laboratory operated continuously until 1513, making James IV's court one of the most thoroughly documented instances of royal alchemical patronage in early modern Europe.
