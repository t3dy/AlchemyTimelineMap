Paris

France

c. 1626, Paris: The medieval philosophical compilation Liber XXIV Philosophorum (Book of Twenty-Four Philosophers) (Book of Twenty-Four Philosophers), circulating in early seventeenth-century Paris, provides metaphysical grounding for attempts to reconcile chemical-practice with Christian theology and mechanistic natural philosophy. Readers invoke the Liber's accounts of divine creativity and cosmic order to argue that human Transmutation constitutes a legitimate imitation of divine creative power. The text enables alchemists to position themselves as participants in an ancient philosophical tradition extending from Hermeticism through medieval scholasticism. The appeal to Liber XXIV Philosophorum (Book of Twenty-Four Philosophers) in Parisian alchemical theory shows how medieval philosophical authorities continued to structure seventeenth-century debates.
