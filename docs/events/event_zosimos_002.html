c. 310 CE Late Antiquity (3rd–7th c.)

Alexandria

Egypt

c. 310 CE, Alexandria: Zosimos of Panopolis records detailed accounts of the operation of Sublimation alongside Distillation, expanding the repertoire of documented chemical processes. Sublimation—the direct transformation of solid to vapor and back to solid, bypassing the liquid state—fascinated ancient and medieval alchemists because it appeared to transcend ordinary material categories. Zosimos distinguishes carefully between sublimation and other forms of material transformation, noting the specific apparatus, heating regimes, and observable signs that characterize the process. His precision in describing these phenomena suggests either direct experimental experience or consultation with highly skilled practitioners. Alexandria's role as a center of pharmaceutical and cosmetic production likely provided Zosimos with access to craftsmen who employed sublimation in the production of cinnabar, sulfur preparations, and other refined materials. His systematic documentation of sublimation helps establish it as a central operation in alchemical theory and practice, where it symbolizes transcendence and spiritual elevation.
