c. 1660 Early Modern (15th–18th c.)

Oxford

England

c. 1660, Oxford: Robert Boyle pursued experiments with air, salts, acids, and chemical analysis while engaging critically with inherited chymical and corpuscular theories. Lawrence Principe's work has been central in showing that Boyle should be read within chymistry rather than as a clean break from alchemy. Oxford's experimental circles made Distillation and related operations part of a broader program of disciplined observation, replication, and argument. Boyle rejected many traditional claims about metallic transmutation, but he did not reject laboratory chemistry as a route to knowledge. Its historiographical significance is that Oxford marks a transformation within chymical practice, not a simple replacement of alchemy by modern chemistry.
