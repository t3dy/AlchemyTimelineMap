c. 1444 CE Early Modern (15th–18th c.)

Florence

Italy

c. 1444, Florence: A Florentine copyist produces an elegant Latin manuscript of the Summa Perfectionis (Sum of Perfection), traditionally attributed to Jabir ibn Hayyan, incorporating glosses from recent European commentaries on Operational Chemistry and Transmutation. This work synthesizes centuries of medieval and early Renaissance interpretation of Jabir's texts, providing Florentine alchemists with a comprehensive guide to reproducible operations in distillation, crystallization, and metalworking. The manuscript's production quality and textual apparatus testify to Florence's preeminence in philological and intellectual engagement with alchemical texts.
