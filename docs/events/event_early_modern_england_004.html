c. 1524 CE Early Modern (15th–18th c.)

London

England

c. 1524, London: Physicians and natural philosophers in Henry VIII's court begin studying the medical alchemical writings attributed to Paracelsus (Theophrastus Bombastus von Hohenheim), whose chemical-pharmaceutical approach to disease promises new remedies based on Operational Chemistry principles. Paracelsus's claim that alchemy is not primarily about gold-making but about extracting the vital quintessence of substances directly addresses the concerns of court physicians seeking legitimacy for their transmutational experiments. This establishes the crucial English link between alchemical practice and medical innovation.
