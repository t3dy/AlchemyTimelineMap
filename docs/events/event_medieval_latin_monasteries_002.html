c. 830 CE Medieval (7th–15th c.)

Fulda

Germany

c. 830 CE, Fulda: The monastery at Fulda, a center of Carolingian learning under the patronage of Louis the Pious, receives translations of the works associated with Al-Kindi (al-Kindy), the Islamic philosopher and natural scientist. Al-Kindi's treatises on the properties of minerals and his critiques of alchemical transmutation reach Western scholars through intermediary translations and merchant networks. Monks at Fulda engage with al-Kindi's rationalist approach to Operational Chemistry, attempting to systematize the principles underlying Distillation and sublimation. Rather than pursuing gold-making, Fulda's scholars focus on understanding the underlying theory of material change and the behavior of substances under heat. This represents a crucial moment when Islamic rationalism enters Western Christian scholarship, establishing a framework for chemical analysis that will dominate medieval monastic science for centuries.

Persons Involved
