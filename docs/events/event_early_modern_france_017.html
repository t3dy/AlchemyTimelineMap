c. 1602 CE Early Modern (15th–18th c.)

Paris

France

c. 1602, Paris: The early seventeenth century witnesses renewed royal patronage for chemical-practice, with the crown continuing to fund alchemical laboratories and research programs in hopes of discovering reliable methods for Transmutation. Parisian court circles debate the reality of alchemical claims, with some figures arguing that careful experiment has demonstrated transmutation's genuine possibility. The political utility of transmutation (unlimited gold production) continues to motivate official support despite mounting skepticism among natural philosophers. Royal investment in chemical-practice in early seventeenth-century Paris remained substantial despite the decline of alchemy's intellectual respectability.
