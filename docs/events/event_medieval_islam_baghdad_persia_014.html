c. 868 CE Medieval (7th–15th c.)

Baghdad

Iraq

c. 868 CE, Baghdad: The translator Gerard of Cremona represents the eventual institutional mechanism through which Baghdad's alchemical knowledge transfers to medieval Europe. During this period, Baghdad's schools preserve and continue the legacy of Jabir ibn Hayyan and Al-Razi (Rhazes), maintaining libraries and scholarly networks dedicated to operative chemistry and alchemical theory. The works on Distillation, Sublimation, and Calcination that Gerard will later translate into Latin are being actively studied, debated, and refined by Baghdad's intellectual elite.
