c. 245 CE Late Antiquity (3rd–7th c.)

Cairo (Heliopolis/Babylon)

Egypt

c. 245 CE, Cairo: Zosimos of Panopolis documents the operation of Distillation in relation to texts in the alchemical tradition, including materials that would later be attributed to early Islamic scholars. The reference to Kitāb al-Ḥāsib in this context indicates that later medieval compilations drew heavily on Zosimosian methods, even if the specific text's written form dates to the Islamic period. Zosimos's descriptions of distillation apparatus—the alembic, retort, and aludel—become the foundational vocabulary of chemical technology across cultures. His work on Operational Chemistry emphasizes the transformation of base materials into refined substances, a process with both practical applications (in metallurgy, pharmacy, textile production) and theoretical implications for understanding matter itself. Zosimos's influence on Islamic and later European alchemy is indebted to his insistence that chemical knowledge must be grounded in visible, tangible results.
