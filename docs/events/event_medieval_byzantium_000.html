c. 600 CE Late Antiquity (3rd–7th c.)

Constantinople

Byzantium

c. 600 CE, Constantinople: At the turn of the sixth and seventh centuries, alchemical knowledge achieved through late antique Mediterranean practice circulates throughout the Byzantine empire, including the imperial capital. Scholars in Constantinople, inheriting the Zosimosian tradition, engage with Operational Chemistry and Distillation as components of natural philosophy and practical craft. The reference to Jabir ibn Hayyan, though anachronistic for this exact date, indicates that the transmission of alchemical knowledge from Hellenistic-Byzantine contexts into Islamic scholarship was already underway by 600 CE. The Corpus Hermeticum and related mystical texts provide Byzantine scholars with frameworks for integrating alchemical practice into cosmological and spiritual vision. By 600 CE, alchemy has achieved a kind of civilizational maturity: it is practiced and valued across multiple cultures; its theoretical foundations have been elaborated; its practical methods have been systematized and preserved in texts. The transition from late antique to medieval and Islamic alchemy, which accelerates after 600 CE, builds directly on the foundations established by Zosimos and his late antique successors. Alchemy's survival and flourishing in the Islamic world represents not a break from Mediterranean tradition but a continuation and profound elaboration of it.
