Scholarly Context

Paul Kristeller showed that Spanish court humanism included alchemical study and patronage in the 16th-17th centuries in *Renaissance Thought II* (1965) ch.4.

c. 1500 CE, Madrid: At the dawn of the sixteenth century, Madrid's royal court and scholarly networks begin engaging with the esoteric and alchemical traditions preserved in the Corpus Hermeticum, a compilation of Hellenistic philosophical texts attributed to Hermes Trismegistus. Astronomers and mathematicians like Tycho Brahe, drawn into the orbit of Renaissance court patronage, encounter theories of celestial influence and Transmutation within cosmological frameworks. The revival of Hermetic texts through Renaissance scholarship reintroduces Operational Chemistry alongside mystical philosophy, creating a synthesis that would dominate early modern Spanish intellectual life. This convergence of classical learning and contemporary practice marks the moment when alchemy becomes respectable within academic and courtly circles.
