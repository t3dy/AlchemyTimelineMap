c. 1630 Early Modern (15th–18th c.)

Amsterdam

Low Countries

c. 1630, Amsterdam: Amsterdam's presses, merchants, and apothecary networks made it a major center for the circulation of chymical books and materials in the Dutch Republic. Discussions of mercury, salts, acids, and metallic change belonged to a commercial and medical culture shaped by trade as well as scholarship. Lawrence Principe and William Newman both warn that early modern matter theory must be understood in historical terms rather than translated too quickly into modern chemistry. The label Operational Chemistry marks procedures that could be observed and repeated, while mercurial theories remained actor explanations. Its historiographical significance is that Amsterdam shows alchemy embedded in print capitalism, commerce, and practical medicine.
