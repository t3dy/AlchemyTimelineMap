c. 1580 Early Modern (15th–18th c.)

Prague

Bohemia

c. 1580, Prague: The courtly culture of Rudolf II made Prague a meeting place for artisans, physicians, instrument makers, miners, and natural philosophers interested in laboratory practice. Pamela Smith's work on artisanal knowledge and Lawrence Principe's studies of early modern chymistry help frame this environment as a mixed world of craft skill, patronage, and learned speculation. The analytical label Operational Chemistry identifies practices such as heating, extraction, assaying, and preparation without endorsing claims that alchemists could perfect metals into gold. Its historiographical significance is that Prague shows the early modern laboratory as a social institution supported by courts, workshops, and markets, not merely by solitary adepts.
