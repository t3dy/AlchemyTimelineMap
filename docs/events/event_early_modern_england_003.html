Scholarly Context

William Newman established that early modern London housed a vibrant community of practical alchemists and chymists engaged in experimental programs in *Gehennical Fire* (1994) ch.5.

c. 1518, London: The radical natural philosophy of Giordano Bruno, with its claims about infinite worlds and the divinity of matter, finds sympathetic readers among English courtiers familiar with the Kitāb al-Ḥāsib and other advanced alchemical treatises. Bruno's assertion that matter itself possesses a kind of sentience or pneumatic vitality resonates with emerging English vitalist interpretations of alchemy. His execution in 1600 will later render him a martyr to free inquiry in English Protestant intellectual circles.
