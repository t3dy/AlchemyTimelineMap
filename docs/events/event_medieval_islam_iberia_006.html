Córdoba

Spain

Scholarly Context

William Newman argued transmutation was a genuinely sought experimental goal supported by systematic laboratory practice, not mere allegory, in *Newton the Alchemist* (2019) pp.1-15.

c. 872 CE, Córdoba: The sophisticated text known as De Sex Rerum Principiis (On the Six Principles of Things) (On the Six Principles of Things) gains increasing circulation among Cordoban scholars, presenting a remarkably advanced theoretical account of how material substances fundamentally transform through Distillation, Fermentation, and other systematic operations. This important treatise effectively bridges Islamic natural philosophy with practical alchemical operations and experimentation, offering an intellectual framework that legitimizes alchemy within broader learned scientific discourse. Cordoban scholars actively engage with the text's complex arguments about substance, essence, and the philosophical nature of chemical transformation, thereby strengthening the permanent institutional place of alchemy within the city's premier academies. The work's recognized authority and comprehensiveness mark a transition toward more systematic, rigorously philosophy-grounded approaches that establish alchemy as legitimate natural science deserving of academic respect and institutional support.
