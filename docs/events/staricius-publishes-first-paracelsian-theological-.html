Scholarly Context

Urs Leo Gantenbein identified Staricius's 1618 Magdeburg publication of the Philosophia De Limbo Aeterno as the first printing of Paracelsian theological writings, in 'Cross and Crucible: Alchemy in the Theology of Paracelsus' in Ambix vol. 67 no. 1 (2020) pp.1-12.

In 1618, the poet, composer, and Rosicrucian sympathizer Johannes Staricius (fl. ca. 1580–after 1623) published the first selection of Paracelsus's theological writings in print: the Philosophia De Limbo Aeterno (Philosophy of the Eternal Limbus), part of Paracelsus's cycle of writings on the Last Supper, at Magdeburg. Urs Leo Gantenbein, in Ambix (2020), identifies this publication as the first moment when Paracelsus's theological dimension became accessible in print: in the decades since Johannes Huser's collected medical edition (1589–1591), Paracelsus's extensive theological oeuvre had remained unpublished, since every printer feared the 'explosive heretical doctrines' it contained. Staricius's 1618 edition broke this silence. The Philosophia De Limbo Aeterno is a central text for understanding Paracelsus's doctrine of the Last Supper and his theory of the New Adam: it describes the mortal human 'descended from Adam' as needing to be brought to a 'new birth through baptism with the Holy Spirit,' in language that closely parallels the alchemical concept of the transformation of prima materia into ultima materia — although Paracelsus is careful to distinguish the two processes and explicitly states that alchemical renovation does not apply to humans, who cannot be 'reduced to the three principles and recomposed' as metals can. Gantenbein notes that Staricius was a Rosicrucian follower, linking the 1618 publication to the period of the Rosicrucian manifestoes (Fama Fraternitatis 1614; Confessio Fraternitatis 1615) and their enthusiasm for Paracelsian natural philosophy. The Paracelsian theological writings would not receive a complete critical edition until Gantenbein's own New Paracelsus Edition, begun in 2008.
