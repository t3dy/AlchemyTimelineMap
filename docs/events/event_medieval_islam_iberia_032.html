Scholarly Context

Leah DeVun showed transmutation was pursued within mystical and theological frameworks by medieval and early modern alchemists in *Prophecy, Alchemy, and the End of Time* (2020) ch.4.

c. 1184 CE, Córdoba: Cordoban alchemical traditions, though now pursued under Christian rule and within transformed social and political circumstances, maintain remarkable continuity with centuries of accumulated knowledge and scholarly practice. Scholars in Córdoba—Muslim, Christian, and Jewish—continue active research and careful teaching in Distillation, Fermentation, and the sustained pursuit of Transmutation. The city's well-maintained and comprehensive libraries, though diminished from their unparalleled golden-age vastness, remain valuable repositories of comprehensive alchemical literature and scholarship. The institutional persistence of Cordoban alchemy, sustained across multiple centuries of significant political transformation and religious change, demonstrates the profound integration of the discipline into Iberian intellectual culture and identity. This remarkable continuity ensures the survival and ongoing transmission of knowledge through subsequent historical changes and transformations. Tradition survives transformation.
