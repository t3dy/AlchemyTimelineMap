Scholarly Context

Deborah Harkness demonstrated that Lisbon was a crucial hub for the circulation of alchemical knowledge through maritime trade and printing in early modern Europe in *The Jewel House* (2007) ch.6.

c. 1710 CE, Iberia: As the War of Spanish Succession concludes, Iberian rulers establish new institutions for Operational Chemistry study, integrating alchemical knowledge into court-patronized natural philosophy. Spanish alchemists, working under Bourbon patronage (Philip V), conduct systematic experiments in distillation, metallurgy, and mineral analysis. The institutional shift reflects a broader European recognition that Operational Chemistry produces real, measurable results—even if claims of gold-making remain unproven. Royal patronage elevates chemical research from private practitioners to court institutions comparable to academies. Iberian contributions to European chemical knowledge increase through publication and correspondence networks linking Madrid, Lisbon, and Prague, establishing Spain and Portugal as significant participants in the Continental transition from alchemy to chemistry.
