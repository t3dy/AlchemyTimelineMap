Scholarly Context

Brian Copenhaver demonstrated Ficino's Latin translation of the Corpus Hermeticum shaped European Hermetic thought for two centuries in *Hermetica* (1992) intro pp.xl-lix.

c. 1400, Antwerp: The merchant-scholars of Antwerp's emerging trading republic acquired and began circulating copies of the Corpus Hermeticum, a foundational Hellenistic text on divine wisdom and natural philosophy. Marsilio Ficino's contemporary Latin translation of these texts in Florence found ready audiences in Antwerp's humanist circles, where interest in Distillation and chemical theory merged with the pursuit of hermetic knowledge. This moment marks the Low Countries' entry into the broader Renaissance reclamation of ancient Greek and Egyptian alchemical sources, establishing Antwerp as a center for the transmission of philosophical alchemy to northern Europe.
