Scholarly Context

Lawrence Principe demonstrated that Alexandria remained a major center of alchemical transmission from Late Antiquity through the medieval Islamic period in *The Secrets of Alchemy* (2013) ch.1-2.

c. 590 CE, Alexandria: At the very end of the sixth century, as the Byzantine era of stability comes to an end, Alexandria represents the culmination of late antique alchemical achievement. The discipline has been systematically documented, theoretically elaborated, and practically refined over nearly four centuries. Distillation and related Operational Chemistry methods have become integral to pharmaceutical, metallurgical, and textile production throughout the Mediterranean world. Alchemical texts, preserved in multiple copies across Egypt, Syria, and Byzantine centers, represent a corpus of knowledge that will survive the political upheavals and religious transformations of the early medieval period. The commitment to observable material transformation, grounded in the work of Zosimos, has proven sufficiently robust to accommodate diverse philosophical and theological frameworks. As the Byzantine era in Egypt draws to a close, alchemical knowledge stands poised for transmission to Islamic scholars, who will become the primary custodians and elaborators of the tradition. The achievements of late antique alchemy—the establishment of operative procedures, the grounding of the discipline in observable phenomena, and the creation of a textual tradition—represent foundational contributions to the history of chemistry.
