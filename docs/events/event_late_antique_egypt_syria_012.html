c. 380 CE Late Antiquity (3rd–7th c.)

Alexandria

Egypt

c. 380 CE, Alexandria: In the late fourth century, alchemical knowledge becomes increasingly integrated with astrological and magical traditions, particularly in relation to the Picatrix (Ghāyat al-Hakīm), a foundational text on talismanic magic and celestial influence. The Picatrix, composed in the Islamic world but drawing on Hellenistic and Byzantine sources, synthesizes philosophy, astronomy, and practical magic into a comprehensive system. Its influence on Mediterranean alchemy suggests that Operational Chemistry and Distillation are no longer understood solely as technical procedures but as part of a larger cosmological order. The preparation of materials—through distillation, sublimation, and refinement—becomes entangled with astrological timing, invocations, and the manipulation of celestial correspondences. This fusion of chemistry and magic reflects the broader intellectual context of late antiquity, in which natural philosophy, theology, and magical practice form an integrated whole. Zosimos's own writings contain mystical and cosmological elements, making him a natural ally for later alchemists who would explicitly link chemical transformation to the manipulation of cosmic forces.
