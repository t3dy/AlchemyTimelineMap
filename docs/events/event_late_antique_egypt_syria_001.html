c. 215 CE Late Antiquity (3rd–7th c.)

Cairo (Heliopolis/Babylon)

Egypt

c. 215 CE, Cairo: The alchemist Zosimos of Panopolis is active in Egypt, developing systematic accounts of Distillation and Operational Chemistry practices. Working in the Nile Delta region, Zosimos draws upon Egyptian metalworking traditions and Hellenistic chemical knowledge, synthesizing Greek theoretical frameworks with Egyptian practical craft. His investigations into distillation apparatus, liquid separation, and material transformation mark a turning point in the documentation of chemical practice. Rather than treating alchemy as a collection of magical formulae, Zosimos grounds his work in repeatable operations and material observation, emphasizing the embodied knowledge of the craftsman over speculative philosophy. This period represents the emergence of alchemy as a self-conscious discipline, merging manual practice with textual authority in a way that would define the tradition for centuries.
