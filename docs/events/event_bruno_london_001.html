c. 1584 Early Modern (15th–18th c.)

London

England

c. 1584 CE, London: Giordano Bruno, after his departure from Paris, settles in London where he encounters a vibrant intellectual community of poets, natural philosophers, and occultists. Bruno's neoplatonism and his engagement with Hermeticism, articulated through vernacular Italian works and lectures, influence English intellectuals. London's intellectual circles, particularly those surrounding the Sidney circle and the court, prove receptive to Bruno's radical philosophy. His insistence that matter itself possesses divinity, that transmutation of matter reflects divine action, resonates with English alchemists and natural philosophers. Bruno's presence in London, though relatively brief, establishes direct intellectual connection between Italian Renaissance philosophy and English intellectual culture. His cosmological theories and his integration of Hermetic principles into natural philosophy influence English practitioners and theorists. Though Bruno's London sojourn proves turbulent and brief, his intellectual presence disseminates Renaissance Hermeticism within English intellectual circles. His impact on English alchemy, though mediated through subsequent intermediaries and printed works, proves significant. Bruno's London period represents the European-wide dissemination of Renaissance Hermeticism and its alchemical dimensions across linguistic and national boundaries.
