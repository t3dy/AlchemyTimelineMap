Scholarly Context

William Newman and Lawrence Principe demonstrated that Islamic alchemy synthesized Greek and Indian traditions into a sophisticated experimental science in *Alchemy Tried in the Fire* (2002) ch.2.

c. 900, Damascus: Syrian scholarly and medical networks helped carry Arabic practical knowledge between Iraq, Egypt, and the Mediterranean. In this setting, pharmacy, mineral treatment, glassware, and furnace practice made operations such as Distillation and Sublimation useful beyond any claim about metallic transmutation. George Saliba's work on Islamic science and Lawrence Principe's history of alchemy both stress that transmission moved through cities, manuscripts, physicians, and artisans rather than through a single chain of masters. Damascus therefore functions here as a corridor of practice and teaching. Its historiographical significance is that it gives the Syrian node visibility in the wider Arabic reshaping of late antique alchemical and medical knowledge.
