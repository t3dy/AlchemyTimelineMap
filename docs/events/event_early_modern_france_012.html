c. 1572 CE Early Modern (15th–18th c.)

Paris

France

c. 1572, Paris: The Picatrix (Ghāyat al-Hakīm), a medieval Arabo-Latin text on celestial magic and its applications to transformation, circulates among Parisian scholars investigating correlations between astronomical cycles and the success or failure of Transmutation experiments. Some practitioners believe that precise timing and planetary alignment amplify the efficacy of chemical chemical-practice, merging astrology with laboratory work. The text's detailed accounts of ritual and correspondence inform both theoretical and practical approaches to Transmutation. Despite skeptical voices, the integration of astrological timing into alchemical practice remains widespread among Parisian practitioners. The engagement with Picatrix (Ghāyat al-Hakīm) in Paris exemplifies how magical frameworks continued to structure alchemical experiment into the seventeenth century.
