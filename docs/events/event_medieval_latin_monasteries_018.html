c. 1070 CE Medieval (7th–15th c.)

Fulda

Germany

c. 1070 CE, Fulda: Fulda's monks produce or acquire a copy of the Summa Perfectionis (Sum of Perfection), attributed to Jabir ibn Hayyan, which represents the most comprehensive and systematic medieval Latin account of Operational Chemistry and Distillation procedures. The Summa becomes the canonical text for Fulda's scholars, who produce multiple commentaries attempting to clarify its often cryptic descriptions and reconcile its prescriptions with their own experimental practice. Fulda's engagement with the Summa marks a turning point: the monastery transitions from ad hoc accumulation of chemical knowledge toward a more disciplined, text-centered approach grounded in systematic commentary. The Summa's authority as a work attributed to Jabir lends prestige to Fulda's chemical investigations. This moment exemplifies the high medieval trend of organizing knowledge through systematic commentary on authoritative texts, a methodology that will dominate European natural philosophy for centuries.
