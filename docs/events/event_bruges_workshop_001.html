Scholarly Context

Brian Copenhaver demonstrated Renaissance humanists recovered and reinterpreted ancient alchemical and hermetic texts in *Hermetica* (1992) ch.4.

c. 1420, Bruges: The workshops and markets of Bruges connected painters, metalworkers, merchants, apothecaries, and manuscript owners in one of northern Europe's busiest commercial environments. Pamela Smith's account of artisanal epistemology helps explain why craft practice matters for alchemy: knowledge of pigments, metals, heat, vessels, and recipes was embodied in skilled work. Distillation is only one of the operations that could cross boundaries between pharmacy, dyeing, and learned experimentation. Its historiographical significance is that Bruges shifts attention from famous authors to the urban craft networks that made alchemical materials, instruments, and practical know-how available.
