c. 1580 Early Modern (15th–18th c.)

Paris

France

c. 1580 CE, Paris: The Italian Dominican friar and natural philosopher Giordano Bruno, forced to flee Italy in the 1570s, settles in Paris and begins circulating his radical cosmological and philosophical works. Bruno's Hermeticism, grounded in the Corpus Hermeticum and Renaissance Neoplatonism, integrates alchemical principles into a revolutionary cosmology positing infinite worlds and the divinity of matter. His defense of Hermeticism against Aristotelian scholasticism positions alchemy not as marginal superstition but as expression of profound metaphysical truth. Bruno's integration of Hermetic principles with innovative cosmological theory establishes connections between alchemical operations and cosmic principles at unprecedented scale. His insistence on the unity of matter and spirit, the alchemical principle that material transformation reflects spiritual transformation, becomes central to his theological argument. Paris's intellectual environment, though increasingly hostile to heterodoxy, permits several years of Bruno's intellectual productivity before religious controversy forces his departure. Bruno's engagement with Hermetic philosophy and its alchemical dimensions reaches influential audiences among Paris's scholars and patrons. Though Bruno's cosmology provokes ecclesiastical opposition, his integration of alchemy into comprehensive philosophical vision influences subsequent philosophers of nature, particularly those seeking alternatives to Aristotelian scholasticism.
