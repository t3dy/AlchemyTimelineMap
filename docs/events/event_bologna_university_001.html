c. 1280 Medieval (7th–15th c.)

Bologna

Italy

c. 1280 CE, Bologna: The Summa Perfectionis (or Sum of Perfection), a comprehensive systematization of Operational Chemistry attributed to Jabir ibn Hayyan but compiled in the fourteenth century, becomes influential in university circles. Though Roger Bacon has died, the intellectual current he championed—that alchemy is legitimate natural philosophy—continues to spread through European universities. Bologna, as a center of legal and medical learning, attracts practitioners interested in grounding Operational Chemistry in systematic procedure and theoretical justification. The Summa Perfectionis provides precisely this systematization, presenting Distillation and other operations as reproducible techniques grounded in natural philosophical principles. University masters, seeking to incorporate alchemical knowledge within the curriculum without exposing themselves to accusations of fraud, adopt the Summa Perfectionis as authoritative text. The document's attribution to the legendary Jabir provides intellectual pedigree. Bologna's role as model for subsequent university establishment across Europe ensures that patterns of integration of the Summa Perfectionis ripple outward. By the late thirteenth century, alchemy has transitioned from marginal pursuit to recognized, if contested, university discipline in institutional settings across Latin Christendom.
