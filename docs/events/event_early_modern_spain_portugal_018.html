Scholarly Context

Paul Kristeller showed that Spanish court humanism included alchemical study and patronage in the 16th-17th centuries in *Renaissance Thought II* (1965) ch.4.

c. 1608 CE, Madrid: The Summa Perfectionis (Sum of Perfection), the most famous medieval alchemical treatise, circulates in new editions and translations among Spanish scholars. This pseudo-Jabir text, attributed to the legendary Arab alchemist but likely composed in medieval Europe, provides systematic instruction in Operational Chemistry grounded in the theory of Transmutation through manipulation of elemental principles. Spanish alchemists study the Summa alongside contemporary works, integrating medieval and early modern Operational Chemistry into coherent methodological frameworks. The continued relevance of the medieval text attests to alchemy's claim of unbroken transmission from antiquity, validating contemporary practice as restoration of ancient wisdom.
