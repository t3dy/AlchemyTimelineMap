c. 700 CE Medieval (7th–15th c.)

Baghdad

Iraq

c. 700 CE, Baghdad: The polymath Jabir ibn Hayyan emerges as a leading figure in the nascent Abbasid scholarly court, where he encounters the Hermetic philosophical traditions preserved in the Corpus Hermeticum. Jabir begins synthesizing Greek alchemical theory with Islamic natural philosophy, laying groundwork for his systematic accounts of Distillation, Sublimation, and Calcination. His integration of operative chemistry with Hermetic cosmology establishes a new model for understanding matter and transformation. Jabir's early work proves foundational for Islamic alchemy, positioning operational chemistry as a legitimate philosophical inquiry rather than mere craft.
