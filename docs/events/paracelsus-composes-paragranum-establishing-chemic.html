Scholarly Context

Ball, Philip demonstrated Paracelsus's intellectual program in <em>The Devil's Doctor: Paracelsus and the World of Renaissance Magic and Science</em> (2006), ch. 4, pp. 89–134. Smith, Pamela H. analyzed Paracelsus's integration of alchemy into medicine in <em>The Business of Alchemy</em> (2004), pp. 243–287.

Paracelsus composes his foundational text Paragranum, establishing a comprehensive natural philosophy in which alchemy and chemistry are essential to medical practice. Rejecting Galenic humoral theory and Aristotelian natural philosophy, Paracelsus argues that the physician must be an alchemist capable of preparing medicines through chemical operation. His integration of practical alchemy with medical theory, asserting that chemical operation reveals nature's fundamental principles and provides effective therapeutic remedies, reorients alchemy from transmutation-focused natural philosophy toward practical chemistry serving medicine. This work profoundly influences Renaissance and early modern medicine, establishing iatrochemistry (medical chemistry) as a developing discipline.
