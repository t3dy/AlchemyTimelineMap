c. 1324 CE Medieval (7th–15th c.)

Venice

Italy

c. 1324, Venice: Venetian scholars acquire a rare copy of Kitāb al-Ḥāsib, traditionally attributed to Jabir ibn Hayyan, through the city's bustling book trade with Arabic-speaking ports. The text's detailed accounts of Operational Chemistry including distillation, sublimation, and crystallization, provide Venetian apothecaries and physicians with reproducible protocols for producing acids, alkaline salts, and refined metals. The presence of this foundational Arabic manual in Venice demonstrates the Republic's capacity to maintain state-of-the-art knowledge in practical alchemy and Transmutation theory. This acquisition accelerates Venice's emergence as a center for craft-based rather than purely speculative alchemical practice.
