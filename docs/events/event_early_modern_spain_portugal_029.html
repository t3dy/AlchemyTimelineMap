Scholarly Context

Deborah Harkness demonstrated that Lisbon was a crucial hub for the circulation of alchemical knowledge through maritime trade and printing in early modern Europe in *The Jewel House* (2007) ch.6.

c. 1674 CE, Lisbon: Portuguese natural philosophers and chemists, influenced by experimental philosophy and the prestige of the scientific societies then flourishing in England and France, refocus alchemical investigation toward systematic documentation of chemical operations and their observable results. Operational Chemistry becomes increasingly central; Transmutation increasingly marginal as empirical investigation raises doubts about noble metal production. Lisbon's chemists begin positioning their work within frameworks of experimental philosophy rather than alchemical tradition. This shift marks the beginning of alchemy's fragmentation: the useful operations (distillation, crystallization, acid production) persist as chemistry; the theoretical goal (transmutation) fades into historical memory.
