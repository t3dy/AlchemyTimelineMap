Scholarly Context

Andrew Wear showed that Lisbon's position as a global trade center led to integration of alchemical and pharmaceutical knowledge from Africa and Asia in *Knowledge and Practice in English Medicine* (1992).

c. 1542 CE, Lisbon: Portuguese maritime exploration and the spice trade bring unprecedented quantities of exotic botanical and mineral substances into Lisbon's markets. Apothecaries and alchemists, confronted with unfamiliar materials, experiment with distillation and Operational Chemistry to extract essences and understand properties. The influx of New World and Asian materials forces practical reassessment of traditional Transmutation theory, as empirical observation of novel substances challenges medieval assumptions about elemental composition. Lisbon becomes a cosmopolitan center of alchemical experimentation where global trade and scholarly curiosity collide, creating new opportunities for practical chemistry grounded in genuine empirical discovery rather than inherited doctrine.
