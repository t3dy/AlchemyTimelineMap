Scholarly Context

Erwin F. Lange analyzed De Re Metallica within the anti-alchemical metallurgical tradition in 'Alchemy and the Sixteenth Century Metallurgists' in Ambix vol. 13 no. 2 (1966) pp.92-95.

Georg Agricola's De Re Metallica (Basel, 1556), published by Froben shortly after the author's death, constituted the most comprehensive sixteenth-century treatment of mining, assaying, and metallurgical processes, and represented the fullest development of the anti-alchemical empiricist tradition in early modern technical writing. Erwin F. Lange, in Ambix (1966), situates Agricola within the group of sixteenth-century metallurgists who brought systematic observational methods to the study of metals in explicit contrast to the allegorical and secretive practices of alchemical authors. The twelve books of De Re Metallica cover the organization and administration of mines; the identification and surveying of ore deposits; the construction and operation of furnaces, bellows, and pumping machinery; the assaying and smelting of all major metals; and the preparation of sulfur, salt, glass, and other mineral products. Written in humanist Latin and illustrated with over 270 woodcuts depicting mining and metallurgical equipment in unprecedented technical detail, the work established a standard of observational and representational precision that influenced subsequent natural philosophy and practical chemistry for a century. Pamela H. Smith, in The Body of the Artisan (2004), analyzes De Re Metallica as a paradigmatic instance of artisanal epistemology in the sixteenth century — the claim that direct material engagement generates reliable natural knowledge — placing Agricola within the broader transformation of European natural philosophy.
