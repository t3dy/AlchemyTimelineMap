Scholarly Context

Allen Debus demonstrated Paracelsian chemical philosophy challenged Galenic medicine and Aristotelian natural philosophy across European universities in *The Chemical Philosophy* (1977) vol.1.

c. 1550, Strasbourg: In the decades after Paracelsus (Theophrastus Bombastus von Hohenheim), Strasbourg and the Upper Rhine became important for the circulation of Paracelsian medical and alchemical ideas. Printers, physicians, and reform-minded readers debated mineral medicines, chemical preparation, and the authority of experience in healing. Allen Debus, Charles Webster, and Lawrence Principe all treat Paracelsianism as a transformation of medical chymistry rather than a simple survival of medieval gold-making. Distillation mattered because it offered a practical method for preparing remedies and separating useful substances. Its historiographical significance is that Strasbourg makes visible the medical afterlife of alchemy, where laboratory procedure entered debates over medicine, reform, and learned authority.
