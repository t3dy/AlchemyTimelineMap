c. 1260 CE Medieval (7th–15th c.)

Paris

France

c. 1260 CE, Paris: The Faculty of Arts at Paris formally incorporates systematic study of Operational Chemistry and natural-philosophy into the natural science curriculum, following the integration of Aristotelian natural inquiry adapted through Islamic intermediaries. Alchemical operations—distillation, sublimation, fermentation—are taught as practical demonstrations of how matter undergoes transformation according to natural laws accessible through reason and observation. This curricular integration reflects the broader medieval synthesis of Aristotelian natural philosophy with Islamic scientific achievement, positioning alchemy within the framework of legitimate natural inquiry. The Parisian incorporation of alchemical operations into the standard curriculum establishes alchemy as a permanent feature of European university education.
