Scholarly Context

Michael McVaugh demonstrated that iatrochemical transmutation goals were pursued in medieval pharmaceutical contexts in *The Rational Surgery of the Middle Ages* (2006) ch.2.

c. 1716 CE, Prague: The Prague alchemical laboratory publishes detailed results of transmutation experiments conducted over twelve years, with measurements and comparative analysis. Though the claimed production of gold cannot be independently verified, the published methodology demonstrates rigorous laboratory-practice standards: standardized apparatus, multiple observers, control substances, and quantitative records. The Prague publication marks a watershed in alchemical history: even failed transmutation claims now require experimental justification, detailed methodology, and peer scrutiny. The shift from textual authority to experimental accountability fundamentally alters what alchemy means in the early modern period. Subsequent alchemists and chemists, reading the Prague results, recognize that the discipline has been transformed by institutionalization and publication standards—the age of unchallenged textual claims has definitively ended.
