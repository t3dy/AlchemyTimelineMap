France

c. 1680, Paris: The late seventeenth-century reappearance of De Sex Rerum Principiis (On the Six Principles of Things) in new editions and scholarly commentaries reflects antiquarian interest in understanding how medieval and Islamic philosophers conceptualized material transformation. Late seventeenth-century scholars treat the Six Principles framework as a historical artifact illuminating pre-modern cosmology and natural philosophy. The careful study of De Sex Rerum Principiis (On the Six Principles of Things) within historical contexts enables recognition of its technical insights without endorsing its metaphysical foundation. The historical scholarship on De Sex Rerum Principiis (On the Six Principles of Things) in late seventeenth-century Paris transformed alchemy into an object of rigorous historical study.
