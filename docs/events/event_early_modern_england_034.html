c. 1704 CE Early Modern (15th–18th c.)

London

England

c. 1704 CE, London: The Royal Society intensifies its incorporation of Operational Chemistry into the broader natural-philosophy project initiated by Robert Boyle and Isaac Newton. Through systematic study of distillation, acid-base reactions, and metal dissolution—the core Operational Chemistry operations—Fellows establish protocols for repeatable experimentation and publication of results. The Society's fellows debate transmutation claims with unprecedented rigor, requiring experimental demonstration rather than textual authority. This institutionalization of alchemy into the natural philosophy curriculum of the Royal Society establishes chemical operations as legitimate natural-philosophical inquiry rather than occult or esoteric knowledge, transforming alchemy from a marginal pursuit into a respected domain of empirical study.
