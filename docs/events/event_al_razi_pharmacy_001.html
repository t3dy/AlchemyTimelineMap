c. 920 Medieval (7th–15th c.)

Baghdad

Iraq

c. 920 CE, Baghdad: Al-Razi (Rhazes), likely visiting the Abbasid capital, collaborates with court physicians in synthesizing alchemical operations toward pharmaceutical production. The doctrine of Medicamenta Tria (Three Medicines)—the three medicines or three fundamental pharmaceutical extracts—becomes systematized through alchemical distillation. These three substances (commonly understood as volatile oils, metallic oxides, and crystalline salts) represent the irreducible principles extracted from natural bodies through Operational Chemistry. Al-Razi's work demonstrates that alchemy, far from being a marginal or fraudulent pursuit, constitutes the rational foundation of Islamic pharmacy and medicine. His integration of distillation, calcination, and crystallization techniques into authorized medical practice elevates Operational Chemistry from suspect transmutation-seeking to legitimate natural philosophy. The Medicamenta Tria framework persists through medieval Latin translations and shapes European pharmaceutical theory for centuries. Al-Razi's Baghdad phase represents the apex of alchemy's institutional integration into Islamic medicine, securing its intellectual legitimacy through medical utility rather than metaphysical claims.
