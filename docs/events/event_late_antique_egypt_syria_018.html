Scholarly Context

Lawrence Principe demonstrated that Alexandria remained a major center of alchemical transmission from Late Antiquity through the medieval Islamic period in *The Secrets of Alchemy* (2013) ch.1-2.

c. 470 CE, Alexandria: In the late fifth century, alchemical texts continue to circulate throughout the Mediterranean, increasingly reaching scholars in Islamic lands and Byzantine centers beyond Egypt. The Summa Perfectionis (Sum of Perfection), attributed to Jabir ibn Hayyan, represents a later synthesis of Zosimosian methods with Islamic philosophical and theological frameworks. While the text's written composition dates to the Islamic period, its dependence on late antique alchemical sources, particularly the work of Zosimos, demonstrates the deep continuity between Mediterranean and Islamic alchemy. The transmission of Operational Chemistry practices from late antique Egypt to the Islamic world occurs through multiple channels: manuscript copies, merchant networks, and the movement of scholars across religious and political boundaries. By 470 CE, the alchemical tradition established by Zosimos has achieved a genuinely cosmopolitan character, encompassing practitioners and theorists across the Mediterranean and into Mesopotamia. The discipline's grounding in observable material transformation and repeatable operations gives it a universal applicability that transcends particular religious, philosophical, or political contexts.
