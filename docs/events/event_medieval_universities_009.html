c. 1308 CE Medieval (7th–15th c.)

Paris

France

c. 1308 CE, Paris: The Faculty of Arts at Paris incorporates systematic study of the Emerald Tablet (Tabula Smaragdina), the foundational Hermetic text attributed to Hermes Trismegistus, into its natural philosophy curriculum alongside treatments of Operational Chemistry and Aristotelian natural science. The Emerald Tablet's cryptic formulations about the unity of all matter and the principle that "as above, so below" provide philosophical warrant for viewing alchemical operations as expressions of universal natural law rather than arbitrary manipulation. The circulation of the Emerald Tablet within the Parisian curriculum reflects the broader medieval synthesis of Hermetic philosophy, Aristotelian logic, and Islamic scientific achievement. This integration of Hermetic philosophy with empirical alchemical study shapes European natural philosophy for centuries.
