1544 Early Modern (15th–18th c.)

Florence

Italy

1544, Florence: Benedetto Varchi (1503–1565), Florentine humanist and member of the Accademia Fiorentina, delivers a disputation on the philosophical question of alchemy at the Accademia Fiorentina. Drawing on the Aristotelian Meteorologica commentary tradition, particularly the arguments of Agostino Nifo and Lodovico Boccadiferro, Varchi presents the strongest available Aristotelian case against metallic transmutation to a court audience. The disputation remains in manuscript until its first printing in 1827. Craig Martin, in 'Alchemy and the Renaissance Commentary Tradition on Meteorologica IV' (Ambix 51:3, 2004), identifies this as a case study in the transmission of university philosophical scepticism about alchemy into humanist literary culture.
