Scholarly Context

Deborah Harkness demonstrated that Lisbon was a crucial hub for the circulation of alchemical knowledge through maritime trade and printing in early modern Europe in *The Jewel House* (2007) ch.6.

c. 1626 CE, Lisbon: The Liber XXIV Philosophorum (Book of Twenty-Four Philosophers), a medieval compilatory text on alchemical philosophy, appears in Portuguese manuscript collections and scholarly libraries. This text, ascribed to twenty-four ancient philosophers, provides Lisbon's scholars with a cosmological framework for understanding Transmutation as participation in the creative processes governing the material universe. Portuguese alchemists debate the text's metaphysical implications, seeking to reconcile Christian theology with the pantheistic suggestions of medieval alchemy. Lisbon's cosmopolitan intellectual community, enriched by diverse religious and cultural traditions, sustains sophisticated theoretical debates about alchemy's place in natural philosophy.
