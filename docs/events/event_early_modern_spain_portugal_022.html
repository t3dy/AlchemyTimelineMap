Scholarly Context

Paul Kristeller showed that Spanish court humanism included alchemical study and patronage in the 16th-17th centuries in *Renaissance Thought II* (1965) ch.4.

c. 1632 CE, Madrid: Spanish chemists and alchemists, increasingly confident in the legitimacy of their discipline, publish systematic treatises describing Operational Chemistry with unprecedented technical precision. These works treat distillation, sublimation, and crystallization not as mystical operations but as chemical processes with measurable outcomes. Transmutation remains theoretically debated, but the methodological rigor of Operational Chemistry is beyond question. Madrid's academic and courtly establishment now publicly supports alchemical research as valuable natural philosophy. The emergence of technically rigorous, non-mystical alchemical discourse marks alchemy's gradual transformation into chemistry.
