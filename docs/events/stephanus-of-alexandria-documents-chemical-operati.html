Scholarly Context

Fowden, Garth established Stephanus's position in the transmission of alchemy in <em>The Egyptian Hermes</em> (1986). Newman, William R. discusses Stephanus in <em>Atoms and Alchemy</em> (2006), pp. 78–112.

Stephanus of Alexandria, working during the tumultuous transition from Byzantine to Arab rule, documents alchemical operations and theoretical frameworks that would influence both Byzantine and Islamic traditions. His treatises demonstrate engagement with Zosimos's foundational texts while incorporating new philosophical perspectives drawn from Neoplatonism and Christian theology. Stephanus emphasizes the reality of material transformation while integrating chemical operations within mystical and cosmological systems. His work represents the crucial bridge between late antique Alexandrian alchemy and the subsequent Islamic scientific renaissance, preserving and transmitting Zosimosian knowledge to later Arabic and Persian alchemists.
