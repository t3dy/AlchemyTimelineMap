Lisbon

Iberia

c. 1650 CE, Lisbon: Mid-century Lisbon represents alchemy at its apogee: fully institutionalized, economically significant, theoretically sophisticated, and practically consequential. University-trained physicians apply Operational Chemistry to medicine; merchant guilds regulate chemical production; private collectors assemble vast libraries of alchemical texts; artisans work in well-equipped laboratories. Transmutation remains contested, but the utility and legitimacy of alchemical operations are established beyond serious doubt. Lisbon's integration of alchemy into urban professional, merchant, and scholarly life exemplifies alchemy's successful transition from marginal practice to central component of early modern knowledge production.
