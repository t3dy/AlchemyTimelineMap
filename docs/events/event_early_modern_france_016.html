c. 1596 CE Early Modern (15th–18th c.)

Paris

France

c. 1596, Paris: The approach of the seventeenth century finds Parisian alchemists and natural philosophers increasingly preoccupied with reconciling Transmutation claims with mechanical and mathematical frameworks. Scholars influenced by new natural philosophy attempt to account for chemical phenomena without recourse to occult forces, seeking instead to understand how matter rearranges according to necessary physical laws. The debate between alchemists and mechanical philosophers creates intellectual ferment in Paris and beyond. This period marks the beginning of alchemy's eclipse by emerging mechanical philosophy. The late sixteenth-century encounter between alchemy and mechanical philosophy in Paris initiated the long transition toward modern chemistry.
