1649 Early Modern (15th–18th c.)

Kassel

Germany

1649, Kassel: The physician Ludwig Combach (1590–1657), ordinary physician to the Landgraves of Hesse-Kassel, publishes Opera omnia chemica, cum praefatione a Ludovico Combachio (Kassel, 1649), the first collected edition of works attributed to George Ripley (d. ca. 1490) and the culmination of a century of European manuscript transmission rooted in the alchemical circle of Edward Kelley. The Opera comprises twelve texts, including the Liber duodecim portarum (Book of the Twelve Gates), the Medulla philosophiae chemicae, the Clavis aurea portae, the Pupilla alchemiae, the Terra terrae philosophicae, the Viaticum seu varia practica, and the Cantilena. Combach's primary manuscript exemplar was Kassel Landesbibliothek 4° MS chem. 67, annotated by the Prague alchemist Nicolaus Mai and containing recipe fragments attributed to Kelley; Combach accordingly attributes to Kelley the Latin translation of the Liber duodecim portarum and two other works — an attribution Jennifer M. Rampling, in 'Transmission and Transmutation' (Early Science and Medicine 17, 2012), identifies as historically spurious but understandable given the manuscript's provenance. Combach's preface, partly translated by Elias Ashmole for the Theatrum Chemicum Britannicum (London, 1652), defends Ripley as clear and reliable and mounts a belated response to Nicolas Guibert's 1603 attack on the alchemical tradition.
