Scholarly Context

Deborah Harkness demonstrated that operational chemistry in early modern period involved precise measurement and record-keeping as forms of knowledge production in *The Jewel House of Art and Nature* (2007) ch.4.

c. 1205 CE, Canterbury: Canterbury's scholars possess copies of the Kitāb al-Ḥāsib, and use it as a text for teaching advanced students in Operational Chemistry and Distillation techniques. The cathedral school at Canterbury has become sufficiently developed to support sophisticated natural philosophical instruction. Canterbury's monks and clerics train students who will carry their knowledge to emerging universities and royal courts. The monastery's laboratories remain active sites of chemical experimentation, now increasingly informed by the new scholastic natural philosophy being developed in emerging universities. Canterbury represents a crucial transitional institution: maintaining the monastic tradition of chemical investigation while also serving as a conduit for transmitting that knowledge to new institutional settings. This moment marks the beginning of the shift from monastic to university-centered learning in chemical and natural philosophical studies.
