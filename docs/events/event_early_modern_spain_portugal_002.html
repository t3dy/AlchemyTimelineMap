Scholarly Context

Wouter Hanegraaff situated Agrippa's De Occulta Philosophia as the most comprehensive systematization of Renaissance natural magic in *Dictionary of Gnosis and Western Esotericism* (2006) pp.4-8.

c. 1512 CE, Madrid: The Renaissance magus Henry Cornelius Agrippa, whose Three Books of Occult Philosophy synthesize medieval scholasticism, Neoplatonism, and practical magic, gains influence in Spanish courtly and university circles. Agrippa's systematic treatment of sympathetic magic and correspondence between celestial and terrestrial realms provides a learned framework for understanding Transmutation not as mere chemical manipulation but as theurgic action in harmony with cosmic order. Madrid's university scholars debate the metaphysical underpinnings of Operational Chemistry, establishing a discourse that positions alchemy within natural magic and Renaissance philosophy. Agrippa's synthesis represents a critical moment when alchemy enters mainstream academic discourse as legitimate natural philosophy.
