Scholarly Context

Sébastien Moureau documented the 1572 Basel edition in 'Some Considerations Concerning the Alchemy of the De anima in arte alchemiae of Pseudo-Avicenna' in Ambix vol. 56 no. 1 (2009) pp.49-56, noting the edition's contents and printer.

The Sienese humanist Mino Celsi edited and published the pseudo-Avicennan De Anima in Arte Alchemiae at Basel in 1572, through the printer Pietro Perna, in a collection entitled Artis Chemicae Principes, Avicenna atque Geber (The Principal Authors of the Chemical Art, Avicenna and Geber). Sébastien Moureau, in Ambix (2009), records the collection as combining the De Anima in Arte Alchemiae with four key texts of Geber: the De Investigatione Perfectionis, the Summa Perfectionis, the De Inventione Veritatis, and the Liber Fornacum — making the 1572 volume a foundational print collection of the two most authoritative names in the medieval Latin alchemical tradition. Pietro Perna (ca. 1519–1582), the Basel printer, was a major figure in the publication of heterodox and humanist texts in the second half of the sixteenth century, and the Artis Chemicae Principes appeared alongside his other alchemical and natural-philosophical publications. By attributing the De Anima to Avicenna and pairing it with Geber's foundational chemical treatises, the collection presented a coherent Latin tradition of authoritative Arabic chemical knowledge whose authenticity would not be questioned until Julius Ruska's research in the 1930s. The volume consolidated the printed availability of the key texts of medieval Latin alchemy at a moment when alchemical print culture was reaching its greatest density, providing scholars, physicians, and alchemical practitioners with a single-volume anthology of the most revered authorities in the Latin tradition.
