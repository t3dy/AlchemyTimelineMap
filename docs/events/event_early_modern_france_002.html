c. 1512 CE Early Modern (15th–18th c.)

Paris

France

c. 1512, Paris: Early sixteenth-century Parisian scholars, informed by readings in occult philosophy and natural magic, begin systematic investigation of Transmutation claims through theoretical frameworks drawn from Neoplatonic metaphysics. The influence of Hermetic and magical philosophy on chemical-practice deepens as French intellectuals seek to understand the cosmological underpinnings of alchemical operations. Networks of learned men exchange correspondence and manuscripts, testing whether natural magic theory can account for observed chemical phenomena. This intellectual ferment positions Paris at the intersection of classical philosophy, Christian theology, and practical alchemy. The integration of philosophical frameworks with empirical chemical-practice marks a turning point in how educated Europeans conceptualized transmutation.
