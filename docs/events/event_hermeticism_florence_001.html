Florence

Italy

c. 1490, Florence: Florentine humanists read the Corpus Hermeticum through Platonist and Christian interpretive frameworks associated with figures such as Marsilio Ficino and Pico della Mirandola. Brian Copenhaver's scholarship sharply revises older claims about Renaissance Hermetism by emphasizing philology, translation, and late antique context, while Wouter Hanegraaff distinguishes historical actors' claims about ancient wisdom from modern analytical categories. The link to Operational Chemistry is therefore mediated through authority, cosmology, and learned natural philosophy, not direct laboratory instruction. Its historiographical significance is that Florence shows how Hermetic prestige shaped alchemical reception while remaining distinct from practical chemical procedure.
