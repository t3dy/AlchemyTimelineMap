c. 928 CE Medieval (7th–15th c.)

Ray (Rhages)

Persia

Scholarly Context

William Newman demonstrated calcination was understood as the reduction of metals toward prima materia in preparation for reconstitution in a purer form in *The Summa Perfectionis* (1991) pp.110-135.

c. 928 CE, Rayy: The translator Gerard of Cremona represents the future institutional role of translation in transmitting Islamic knowledge to the Latin West. Though Gerard's actual work occurs centuries later in Toledo, by this date the alchemical texts of Jabir ibn Hayyan and Al-Razi (Rhazes) have reached a level of textual stability and circulation that makes them worthy of translation. The sophisticated treatments of Distillation, Sublimation, and Calcination in Rayy's alchemical schools constitute the precise material that translation networks will eventually convey to European universities.
