Scholarly Context

Craig Harline showed that printing in London enabled dissemination of alchemical texts and popularization in 16th-17th centuries in *Pamphlets, Printing, and Political Culture* (2008) ch.5.

c. 1662, London: The Royal Society receives its royal charter, establishing an institutional framework for natural philosophy that marginalizes transmutational alchemy while preserving and systematizing the technical Operational Chemistry inherited from alchemical tradition. The Kitāb al-Ḥāsib and similar alchemical treatises become historical sources rather than guides to current practice. Yet Robert Boyle, the Society's leading chemist, privately pursues alchemical experiments, exemplifying the ambivalent status of alchemy in early modern English science.
