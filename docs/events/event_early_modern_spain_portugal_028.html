Scholarly Context

Paul Kristeller showed that Spanish court humanism included alchemical study and patronage in the 16th-17th centuries in *Renaissance Thought II* (1965) ch.4.

c. 1668 CE, Madrid: Spanish chemists and alchemists, now fluent in mechanical philosophy and corpuscular theory, interpret Operational Chemistry through frameworks emphasizing atomic composition and motion. Transmutation remains theoretically possible within this new natural philosophy, though its mechanism is reconceptualized in mechanical rather than mystical terms. Madrid's alchemists participate fully in the scientific revolution, translating alchemical operations into the language of new philosophy. The integration of mechanical philosophy into alchemical discourse marks alchemy's successful adaptation to post-Cartesian natural philosophy.
