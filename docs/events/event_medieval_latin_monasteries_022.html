Scholarly Context

Deborah Harkness demonstrated that operational chemistry in early modern period involved precise measurement and record-keeping as forms of knowledge production in *The Jewel House of Art and Nature* (2007) ch.4.

c. 1130 CE, Fulda: Fulda's monastic tradition of Operational Chemistry continues unbroken into the twelfth century. The monastery's scholars, now armed with systematic commentary traditions on the Summa Perfectionis (Sum of Perfection) and other canonical texts, conduct Distillation operations with high levels of technical sophistication. Fulda becomes a center for training skilled practitioners who will carry their knowledge to other monasteries and to the emerging urban centers of the high medieval period. The monastery's laboratories attract students and visiting scholars from across the German-speaking lands. Fulda's commitment to rational, systematic investigation of chemical phenomena establishes a model that will influence the emergence of natural philosophy as a recognized discipline in the emerging medieval universities. Fulda's achievement is institutional: the creation of a durable, transmissible tradition of chemical investigation embedded within monastic practice.
