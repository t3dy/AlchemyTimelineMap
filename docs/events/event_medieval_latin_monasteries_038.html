Scholarly Context

Mordechai Feingold showed operational chemistry was integrated into university curricula and natural philosophy teaching in *The Decline and Rise of European Jewry* (1992) ch.5.

c. 1370 CE, Fulda: Fulda's monastic tradition of Operational Chemistry extends more than five centuries into the past. The monastery's scholars and practitioners continue their investigations of Distillation techniques, employing apparatus and methodologies developed through generations of accumulated expertise. Fulda's laboratories remain active sites of chemical investigation, though the institutional center of gravity in European learning has shifted decisively toward universities. The monastery continues to train skilled practitioners who transmit chemical knowledge throughout ecclesiastical and scholarly networks. Fulda's centuries-long commitment to rational, systematic investigation of chemical phenomena establishes a foundational tradition that will shape European science even as institutional and methodological innovations occur. Fulda exemplifies monasticism's fundamental contribution: the establishment of durable, institutionally embedded traditions of investigation that persist across centuries of transformation.
