c. 365 CE Late Antiquity (3rd–7th c.)

Cairo (Heliopolis/Babylon)

Egypt

c. 365 CE, Cairo: By the mid-fourth century, Distillation and related Operational Chemistry practices are firmly established within the educated elites of Egypt. Pharmaceutical treatises increasingly incorporate alchemical procedures, and medical practitioners employ distilled essences, sublimed minerals, and crystallized salts in their remedies. The continued demand for Distillation apparatus and refined materials sustains workshops and attracts practitioners. Cairo (ancient Heliopolis or Babylon) emerges as a secondary center of alchemical activity alongside Alexandria. The administrative functions of Cairo as a provincial capital bring merchants, craftsmen, and scholars into the region, creating networks for the exchange of technical knowledge. Zosimos's legacy has been established in institutional and material form: through copies of his treatises preserved in libraries, through the demand for his recommended apparatus in workshops, and through the practical utility of his techniques in medicine and craft production. The widespread adoption of Operational Chemistry practices indicates that Zosimos has successfully elevated alchemy from the domain of individual workshop practice to a recognized discipline with educational and professional standing.
