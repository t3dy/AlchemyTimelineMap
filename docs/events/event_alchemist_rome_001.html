c. 1350 Medieval (7th–15th c.)

Rome

Italy

c. 1350, Rome: Papal and curial Rome provided a setting in which clerics, physicians, and administrators encountered alchemical promises about medicine, wealth, and natural secrets. The city should not be treated as a single alchemical school; rather, it represents the institutional world in which manuscripts, patronage, and suspicion met. Michela Pereira and Lawrence Principe both emphasize that late medieval alchemy moved through religious and administrative settings as well as workshops. The procedures grouped here as Operational Chemistry included preparation and testing, while transmutational claims remained disputed. Its historiographical significance is that Rome reveals how alchemy circulated inside institutions that could patronize, regulate, or condemn it.
