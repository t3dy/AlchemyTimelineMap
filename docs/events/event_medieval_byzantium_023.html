Scholarly Context

Wouter Hanegraaff showed late medieval alchemical literature merged practical laboratory operations with mystical and hermetic philosophy in *Western Esotericism* (2012) ch.3.

c. 1290 CE, Prague: The alchemical laboratory and library of Holy Roman Emperor Rudolf II's court begin to take shape, reflecting the growing prestige of alchemy among European nobility. Rudolf's systematic acquisition of alchemical texts, instruments, and manuscripts from across Europe consolidates alchemy as a courtly science worthy of imperial patronage. This investment in alchemy's material and intellectual infrastructure positions it as a major field of early modern natural philosophy.
