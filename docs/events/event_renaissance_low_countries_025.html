Scholarly Context

Craig Harline demonstrated that Bruges was a center for manuscript production and alchemical manuscript circulation in the 15th-16th centuries in *The Book in Early Modern Europe* (2012) ch.5.

c. 1600, Bruges: The threshold of the 17th century found Bruges maintaining its archival and intellectual heritage while Antwerp dominated contemporary alchemical activity. The city's libraries contained centuries of alchemical manuscripts documenting the evolution of theory and practice, preserving knowledge of Distillation and chemical-experimentation for future generations. This archival function represented an important historical contribution even as Bruges' role as an active center diminished.
