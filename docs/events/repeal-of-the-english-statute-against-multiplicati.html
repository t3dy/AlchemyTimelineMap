Scholarly Context

D. Geoghegan documented the repeal and Boyle's role in 'A Licence of Henry VI to Practise Alchemy' in Ambix vol. 6 (1957) pp.10-17, citing Newton's letter to Locke of 2 August 1692.

The English Statute of 5 Henry IV (1403–4), which had made the multiplication of gold and silver a felony without royal license, was formally repealed in 1689. D. Geoghegan, in Ambix (1957), notes that Robert Boyle used his influence in connection with this repeal — a detail documented by Isaac Newton in a letter to John Locke of 2 August 1692, in which Newton stated his belief that it was 'for the sake of a recipe concerning a process about the red earth and mercury' that Boyle procured the repeal (cf. F. S. Taylor, Ambix V, 1956, p. 59). Geoghegan records the statutory repeal in the Statutes at Large (London, 1770, vol. III, p. 436). The repeal effectively ended almost three centuries of legal restriction on alchemical multiplication in England, coming at precisely the moment when practical chemistry under the influence of Boyle, Newton, and their contemporaries was transforming into a distinct discipline. The timing is historically significant: by 1689 the institutional and intellectual context of alchemy had already been substantially reframed, so the repeal opened legal space for chemical research at the moment when alchemy was giving way to what Newton's contemporaries were beginning to call 'chymistry' in a new sense.
