Scholarly Context

Paul Kristeller showed that Spanish court humanism included alchemical study and patronage in the 16th-17th centuries in *Renaissance Thought II* (1965) ch.4.

c. 1620 CE, Madrid: Spanish alchemists working in royal service pursue metallurgical experiments aimed at perfecting gold-working and silver-refining techniques. Systematic Operational Chemistry applied to precious metals—dissolution, crystallization, and recombination—grounds the pursuit of Transmutation in economically rational investigation. Madrid's courtly patronage ensures resources and prestige for these experiments; the royal need for refined metals ensures practical relevance. The convergence of economic interest and scholarly ambition creates powerful incentives for alchemical innovation. Madrid's position as center of imperial power translates into material support for systematic metallurgical research rooted in alchemical theory.
