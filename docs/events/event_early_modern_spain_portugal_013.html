c. 1578 CE Early Modern (15th–18th c.)

Lisbon

Iberia

c. 1578 CE, Lisbon: Portuguese merchant guilds and craft practitioners establish formal apprenticeships in alchemical and pharmaceutical disciplines, transmitting technical knowledge of distillation, metallurgy, and dye production through structured training. Unknown artisans and their apprentices constitute the working backbone of urban Operational Chemistry, producing the substances and techniques upon which academic alchemy theorizes. These practitioners, absent from university records and surviving documentation, perform Transmutation at scale, converting raw materials into valuable compounds for medicine, cosmetics, and industry. Lisbon's craft workshops represent the material reality of alchemy as embedded in labor and production, not merely in books and theory.
