c. 300 CE Late Antiquity (3rd–7th c.)

Cairo (Heliopolis/Babylon)

Egypt

c. 300 CE, Cairo: Zosimos of Panopolis, now in the final decades of his life, has established himself as the preeminent authority on alchemy in the Mediterranean world. His treatises on Distillation and Operational Chemistry represent the most systematic and detailed accounts of chemical practice yet produced. Zosimos's influence extends from Egypt to Syria, Mesopotamia, and into Byzantine intellectual circles. His method of grounding alchemy in careful observation and repeatable procedure marks a decisive break from purely mystical or speculative approaches. He demonstrates that chemical change is governed by intelligible principles, and that systematic study of material transformation can yield genuine knowledge. His treatises are copied and recopied, commented upon by later scholars, and eventually translated into Arabic, where they become foundational texts for the Islamic alchemical tradition. By 300 CE, Zosimos has not merely practiced alchemy but has established it as a discipline worthy of serious intellectual engagement, rooted in observable phenomena and communicable through writing.
