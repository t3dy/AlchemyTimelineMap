Scholarly Context

Andrew Wear showed that Lisbon's position as a global trade center led to integration of alchemical and pharmaceutical knowledge from Africa and Asia in *Knowledge and Practice in English Medicine* (1992).

c. 1530 CE, Lisbon: The Portuguese court's patronage of alchemical and medical research expands as private practitioners establish workshops and laboratories in Lisbon's commercial districts. Unknown alchemists working in the shadows of official institutions pursue Transmutation experiments and produce treatises on Operational Chemistry that circulate in manuscript form among networks of sympathetic merchants, physicians, and courtiers. These shadowy practitioners leave few names and fewer traces in surviving records, yet their work represents the normalization of alchemy as practical craft knowledge integrated into urban pharmacological and metallurgical production. The absence of famous names marks a crucial historiographical moment: alchemy becomes embedded in the ordinary technical infrastructure of early modern cities.
