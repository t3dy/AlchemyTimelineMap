Scholarly Context

William Newman traced the Jabirian corpus as the foundational Arabic synthesis establishing the sulphur-mercury theory as the dominant framework for metallic composition in *The Summa Perfectionis* (1991).

c. 760 CE, Rayy: Jabir ibn Hayyan consolidates his mature theory of alchemical practice, emphasizing the philosophical and operational unity of Distillation, Sublimation, and Calcination. His treatises, composed or compiled in Rayy and Baghdad, present alchemy as a legitimate natural science grounded in observable transformation of matter. Jabir's insistence on the reproducibility of operations and the necessity of precise proportions establishes chemistry as a discipline requiring both theoretical knowledge and manual skill. His work becomes the authoritative reference across the Islamic world for three centuries.
