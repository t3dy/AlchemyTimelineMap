Scholarly Context

Karin C. Ryding analyzed the alchemical chapter of the Mafatih al-Ulum and provided an English translation in 'Islamic Alchemy According to Al-Khwarizmi' in Ambix vol. 41 no. 3 (1994) pp.121-133.

Abu Abdallah Muhammad ibn Ahmad al-Khwarizmi (d. ca. 997), an Arabic court scribe and encyclopedist, composed the Mafatih al-Ulum (Keys to the Sciences) between 976 and 980 for the court of the Samanid dynasty at Bukhara, dedicating it to the vizier Abu al-Hasan Ubayd Allah ibn Ahmad al-Utbi, who served the Samanid ruler Nuh II ibn Mansur (r. 976–997). Karin C. Ryding, in Ambix (1994), identifies the work as a 'substantial yet succinct reference work' intended as a professional manual for court scribes who required definitions and explanations of the technical terminology of the diverse sciences of the Islamic world. The work is organized in two discourses: traditional Islamic sciences and secular sciences (logic, philosophy, medicine, arithmetic, geometry, astronomy, music, mechanics, and alchemy). Ryding characterizes the Mafatih al-Ulum's ninth chapter, on alchemy, as 'a unique and valuable cultural artifact for it is an objective description of medieval alchemy written by a non-alchemist' — unlike the abstruse writing of alchemical adepts, it was designed to elucidate rather than conceal. Al-Khwarizmi's taxonomy of alchemy — covering fifteen types of apparatus, a systematic classification of substances, and thirteen types of operation — was based primarily on the works of Muhammad ibn Zakariya al-Razi (d. 925). The Mafatih al-Ulum appeared alongside two other major Arabic encyclopedias of the same period: the Fihrist of Ibn al-Nadim (d. 995) and the Ikhwan al-Safa' epistles, constituting a major moment of synthesis and codification of Islamic scientific knowledge at the height of the Samanid cultural renaissance.
