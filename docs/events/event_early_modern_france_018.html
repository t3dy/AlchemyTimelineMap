France

c. 1608, Paris: The medieval masterwork Summa Perfectionis (Sum of Perfection) (Sum of Perfection), attributed to Jabir ibn Hayyan, continues to anchor alchemical study in early seventeenth-century Paris despite mounting criticism from mechanical philosophers. The Summa's meticulous accounts of distillation, sublimation, and other chemical operations remain valued for their technical precision, even as scholars question the text's claims about ultimate Transmutation. Early seventeenth-century readers attempt to extract operational knowledge from the Summa while discarding its metaphysical framework. The continuing study of Summa Perfectionis (Sum of Perfection) in Parisian circles exemplifies how practical chemistry persisted as mechanical philosophy advanced.
