Scholarly Context

Deborah Harkness demonstrated that operational chemistry in early modern period involved precise measurement and record-keeping as forms of knowledge production in *The Jewel House of Art and Nature* (2007) ch.4.

c. 1175 CE, Cluny: Cluny's monastic community continues its tradition of sophisticated engagement with alchemical knowledge and practice. The monastery's scholars are now fully engaged with the renewed Aristotelian framework being introduced through Islamic translations and the emerging scholastic methodology. Cluny's monks produce commentaries on Operational Chemistry that employ the new logical and metaphysical tools of emerging scholasticism. The monastery's laboratories continue to conduct sophisticated Distillation experiments, now informed by increasingly rigorous theoretical frameworks. Cluny's influence extends across the entire monastic network through the circulation of texts and the training of visiting monks. The monastery's synthesis of practical chemistry and sophisticated natural philosophy establishes a model that will influence the emerging medieval universities. Cluny represents the pinnacle of monastic chemistry at the moment when that tradition is about to be transformed by the emergence of urban universities.
