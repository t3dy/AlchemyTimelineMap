c. 1506 CE Early Modern (15th–18th c.)

London

England

c. 1506, London: English natural philosophers at Oxford and Cambridge begin circulating works attributed to Michael Maier, whose elaborate alchemical emblems and theoretical syntheses represent the latest Continental developments. Maier's integration of musical, geometric, and alchemical symbolism appeals to a growing English interest in transmutational theory framed within Renaissance neoplatonism. This marks the transition of alchemy from purely medicinal interest to a comprehensive natural philosophy that claims to govern both matter and celestial motion.
