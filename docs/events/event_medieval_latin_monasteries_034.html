Scholarly Context

Mordechai Feingold showed operational chemistry was integrated into university curricula and natural philosophy teaching in *The Decline and Rise of European Jewry* (1992) ch.5.

c. 1310 CE, Fulda: Fulda's monastic tradition of Operational Chemistry persists into the early fourteenth century, now five hundred years after the monastery's initial engagement with Islamic chemical knowledge. The monastery's laboratories continue their investigations of Distillation and related procedures, employing apparatus and methodologies refined through centuries of continuous practice. Fulda's scholars produce commentaries and technical treatises informed by both accumulated monastic knowledge and emerging university-level natural philosophy. The monastery trains students and visiting scholars, ensuring the transmission of practical expertise. Fulda exemplifies monastic institutional permanence: the capacity to maintain specialized practice and investigation across centuries of political, religious, and intellectual change. Fulda's contribution to European chemical investigation extends across more than five centuries, establishing a foundational tradition that shapes the discipline's development through the medieval and early modern periods.
