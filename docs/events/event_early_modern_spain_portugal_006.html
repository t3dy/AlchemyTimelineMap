Scholarly Context

Mauricio Sánchez Martínez showed that Madrid's royal court patronage supported alchemical research in early modern Spain in *The Construction of an Identity* (2005).

c. 1536 CE, Madrid: The medieval alchemical text De Sex Rerum Principiis (On the Six Principles of Things), a treatise on the six principles (Mercury, Sulfur, Salt, Water, Oil, and Spirit) circulates in Madrid's university libraries and appears in scholars' reading lists. This systematization of elemental and operative theory provides Spanish alchemists with a coherent natural philosophy grounded in the transformation of matter. The text's emphasis on Operational Chemistry through distillation, sublimation, and crystallization offers practical methodology alongside theoretical justification. Spanish scholastics debate the text's compatibility with Aristotelian natural philosophy, establishing frameworks that would govern alchemy's institutional legitimacy in the Iberian peninsula for generations.
