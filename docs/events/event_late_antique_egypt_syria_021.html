c. 515 CE Late Antiquity (3rd–7th c.)

Cairo (Heliopolis/Babylon)

Egypt

c. 515 CE, Cairo: In the early sixth century, alchemical knowledge becomes increasingly integrated with medieval philosophical and theological frameworks. The Liber XXIV Philosophorum (Book of Twenty-Four Philosophers), attributed to the tradition of the Twenty-Four Philosophers, reflects the synthesis of Neoplatonic, Christian, and emerging Islamic philosophy with alchemical practice. This text, compiled during the medieval period, draws heavily on late antique sources while reinterpreting them through medieval concerns. By 515 CE, alchemical practitioners in Cairo and throughout Egypt engage with materials and methods inherited from Zosimos while adapting them to new institutional and intellectual contexts. The fifth and sixth centuries witness the gradual transformation of alchemical knowledge from a discipline rooted in Hellenistic philosophy to one embedded in Byzantine, Islamic, and emerging medieval European intellectual traditions. The core commitment to Operational Chemistry and careful documentation of Distillation and related processes remains constant across these cultural transformations. Alchemy survives late antiquity's end by transcending the particular philosophical or religious frameworks within which it emerged, establishing itself as a universal approach to material transformation.
