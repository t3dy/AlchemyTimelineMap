Scholarly Context

Principe, Lawrence M. established the distinction between historical Flamel and legendary Flamel in <em>The Secrets of Alchemy</em> (2013), pp. 156–189.

Nicholas Flamel, a prosperous Parisian scribe and book dealer, begins his career in manuscript trade and wealth accumulation. Whether Flamel engaged in actual alchemical work remains historically uncertain, but later alchemists attributed to him the discovery of the Philosopher's Stone and the secret of transmutation. Flamel's historical success as a merchant became conflated with legendary tales of alchemical achievement, making him one of the most famous names in Western alchemy despite the dubious historical foundation of his alchemical reputation. His example demonstrates how historical fact and alchemical legend became intertwined in the medieval and Renaissance imagination.
