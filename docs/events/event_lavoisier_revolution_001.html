Scholarly Context

Janet Coleman demonstrated that Paris university natural philosophy included alchemical theory and practice in 13th-14th centuries in *Medieval Readers and Writers* (1981) ch.6.

c. 1780, Paris: Antoine Lavoisier and his collaborators reorganized chemical language around weighing, gases, combustion, and the rejection of phlogiston. This event sits after the main medieval and early modern alchemical arc, but it matters because later chemistry retrospectively changed how earlier alchemy was judged. Frederic L. Holmes and Jan Golinski both show that the Chemical Revolution involved instruments, institutions, nomenclature, and persuasion as well as experiments. Operations such as Distillation remained useful, but they were interpreted within a new quantitative framework. Its historiographical significance is that Paris marks the point where older chymical practices were not simply erased, but reclassified under a different chemical order.
