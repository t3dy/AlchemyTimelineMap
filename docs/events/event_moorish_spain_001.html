c. 1200 Medieval (7th–15th c.)

Toledo

Spain

c. 1200, Toledo: The Iberian translation movement made Toledo a central conduit for Arabic natural philosophy, medicine, astrology, and technical writing into Latin. Translators associated with the city, including Gerard of Cremona, helped create the conditions in which Latin readers could encounter alchemical procedures and classify them as natural knowledge. Charles Burnett, Michela Pereira, William Newman, and Lawrence Principe all treat Iberia as crucial to the Latin reception of Arabic science. The category Operational Chemistry is used analytically here for reproducible procedures, not as a medieval actor term. Its historiographical significance is that Toledo makes translation, not isolated genius, the engine of medieval alchemical transmission.
