Scholarly Context

Wouter Hanegraaff situated Agrippa's De Occulta Philosophia as the most comprehensive systematization of Renaissance natural magic in *Dictionary of Gnosis and Western Esotericism* (2006) pp.4-8.

c. 1512, Prague: The philosopher and occultist Henry Cornelius Agrippa, known for his systematic philosophy of natural and ceremonial magic, brings his intellectual framework to Prague during a period of extraordinary receptivity to esoteric learning. Agrippa's synthesis of Neoplatonic philosophy with practical alchemy establishes a systematic model for understanding the operational basis of magical and alchemical practice. Agrippa's influence on early modern Central European alchemy lies in his demonstration that transmutation theory required philosophical grounding in correspondence between microcosm and macrocosm.
