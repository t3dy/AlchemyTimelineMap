Lisbon

Iberia

c. 1602 CE, Lisbon: The turn of the seventeenth century finds Lisbon's scholarly and merchant communities fully engaged with alchemical practice and theory. University physicians teach courses on medical-chemical preparation; merchant guilds regulate the production of alchemical substances; private collectors assemble libraries of alchemical texts. The normalization of Operational Chemistry within urban institutions marks alchemy's transition from marginal practice to integrated knowledge system. Transmutation remains theoretically contested, but the practical utility of alchemical distillation and metal-working is beyond question. Lisbon exemplifies alchemy's successful establishment within early modern urban commercial and intellectual infrastructure.
